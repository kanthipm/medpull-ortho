# External datasets for building, pretraining, and validating the MedPull Recovery Copilot post-op orthopedic RTM models — contents, wearable granularity, orthopedic outcome coverage, access mechanics, cost, timeline, and commercial-use licence restrictions

## Summary

There is no public dataset that contains what you actually need — dense multi-sensor wearable time series from post-operative orthopedic patients with adjudicated 90-day complication labels. That dataset does not exist anywhere, at any price. What exists splits cleanly into four buckets that must be used for four different purposes, and conflating them is the main failure mode. (1) Open consumer-wearable cohorts (LifeSnaps, PMData, MMASH, NHANES accelerometry, the Snyder-lab COVID release) are small-to-medium, free, permissively licensed, and are the right way to calibrate your EWMA/CUSUM false-alarm rates and validate baseline/SD-floor logic — LifeSnaps is the single closest match to your six-metric panel (Fitbit Sense: RHR, HRV, SpO2, skin-temperature deviation, sleep stages, steps at minute resolution). (2) Self-supervised pretrained weights from UK Biobank accelerometry (OxWearables/ssl-wearables, ~700k person-days) give you UK-Biobank-scale representation learning without a UK Biobank application — this is the highest-value zero-cost item on the list. (3) Clinical corpora (MIMIC-IV v3.1: 364,627 patients / 546,028 admissions / 94,458 ICU stays; MIMIC-IV-ED v2.2: ~425,000 ED stays; eICU: ~200k ICU admissions with 5-minute vitals) are free but carry a research-only licence that does not support shipping a commercial model trained on them — use them for method development and publication only. (4) Orthopedic registries (AJRR, NJR, AOANJRR, MARCQI, FORCE-TJR) and claims (PearlDiver, MarketScan, CMS) contain the outcome base rates you need to set thresholds and priors, contain zero wearable data, cost $25k–$200k+, and take months. Two hard access blockers to stop planning around: ACS NSQIP PUFs are restricted to staff at participating hospitals and ACS explicitly prohibits incorporating the data into third-party AI/ML applications; and the Apple Heart & Movement Study / Apple Women's Health Study publish no external access mechanism at all. Synthea does ship a real total_joint_replacement module (TKA SNOMED 609588000, THA 52734007) but it models no complications, no readmissions, and no vital-sign time series — it is FHIR-pipeline test data, not training data. Concretely: spend week 1 on free assets, months 1–3 on PhysioNet credentialing plus an All of Us Registered Tier account, and reserve real money for a prospective IRB'd 150–300-patient cohort at a partner practice, because that is the only path to an honest validation claim.

## Findings

### LifeSnaps is the only open dataset matching your exact six-metric sensor panel
*[moderate]*

Yfantidou et al., Scientific Data 2022. 71 participants, ~4 months of unobtrusive in-the-wild collection with Fitbit Sense: resting/continuous heart rate, HRV (RMSSD and daily HRV summary), SpO2, wrist skin-temperature deviation from personal baseline, sleep stages, steps, plus ~35M rows of minute-granularity data and repeated psychological surveys (PANAS, STAI, BFI). Released open access on Zenodo under CC BY. This maps almost 1:1 onto your RHR / HRV RMSSD / wrist skin temp / SpO2 / RR / sleep / steps panel. Concrete use: replay your EWMA (lambda=0.3, L=2.66, 2-consecutive-day rule) and one-sided CUSUM (k=0.5, h=5.0, 14-day window) over 71 healthy people x ~120 days and measure your empirical false-alarm rate per patient-month. If your current parameters fire on >1 alert per healthy patient-month you will destroy clinical trust before you ever see a true positive. Failure mode: healthy young-adult cohort, no surgery, so it bounds specificity only — it says nothing about sensitivity.

> Yfantidou S et al., 'LifeSnaps, a 4-month multi-modal dataset capturing unobtrusive snapshots of our lives in the wild', Scientific Data 9:663 (2022); Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.6826244

### UK Biobank accelerometry: 103,712 participants, but the pretrained weights are free and the raw data is not worth an application yet
*[strong]*

Doherty et al., PLOS ONE 2017 (verified by fetch): 103,712 accelerometer datasets received, 96,600 participants (93.3%) with valid data after QC; Axivity AX3 wrist-worn triaxial, 100 Hz sampling, ±8g dynamic range, 7 consecutive days, median wear 6.9 days (IQR 6.5–7.0); calibrated to local gravity using stationary periods (SD <13.0 mg per axis over 10-s windows) with least-squares gain/offset fit to a unit gravity sphere. Derived variables (vector magnitude, activity-intensity ECDFs) are in the UKB showcase. CRITICAL SHORTCUT: Yuan et al. (npj Digital Medicine 2024) trained a multi-task self-supervised 1D ResNet-18 on ~700,000 person-days of this exact data and released the weights openly at github.com/OxWearables/ssl-wearables, plus `stepcount` and `actipy` packages. You get UK-Biobank-scale representation learning with zero application, zero fee, zero delay. Failure modes: accelerometry was collected once in 2013–2015, years away from any surgery, so it cannot supply peri-operative trajectories; and it is wrist accelerometry only — no HR, no HRV, no temperature, no SpO2.

> Doherty A et al., PLOS ONE 12(2):e0169649 (2017), fetched; Yuan H et al., npj Digital Medicine 7:91 (2024); github.com/OxWearables/ssl-wearables

### UK Biobank raw access: commercial use IS permitted, tiered fees, ~3–6 month timeline — but the orthopedic linkage is the real draw, not the accelerometry
*[moderate]*

UK Biobank explicitly welcomes for-profit applicants (unusual among biobanks) under an Access Management System application; the site 403s automated fetches so the exact 2026 fee schedule is unverified here. From prior knowledge the model is a tiered application fee in the low thousands to ~£9,000 GBP for a multi-year access period, with genomics and imaging as paid add-ons, plus at-cost compute on the UKB Research Analysis Platform (DNAnexus/AWS). Non-negotiable terms: derived variables and results must be returned to UK Biobank and made available to other researchers; publication is required; UKB does not claim IP in downstream inventions. Timeline: registration/identity verification then Access Committee review, realistically 2–4 months end to end. The unique asset for you is not the accelerometry alone but its linkage to Hospital Episode Statistics — OPCS-4 codes W37–W42 (hip) and W40–W42 (knee) arthroplasty with dates, making UKB the only large cohort on earth with both raw wrist accelerometry and orthopedic surgical outcomes. But accelerometry predates surgery by years for nearly everyone, so it supports pre-op fitness→outcome modelling, not post-op deviation detection.

> ukbiobank.ac.uk access pages (403 to automated fetch); UKB Access Procedures; prior knowledge of the 2023 tiered fee restructure

### All of Us Fitbit data: the largest linked wearable+EHR cohort you can touch, but the data can never leave the Workbench
*[moderate]*

Program-wide ~413,000+ participants in the Controlled Tier CDR (v7, 2022) with EHR for a large majority. Fitbit sub-cohort is far smaller — roughly 14,000–16,000 participants with any Fitbit data as of CDR v7, expanding via the WEAR study (which distributed Fitbits to ~10,000 participants). Table structure: `activity_summary` (daily steps/calories/active minutes), `heart_rate_minute_level` (1-minute), `heart_rate_summary` (daily resting HR and zones), `steps_intraday` (1-minute), `sleep_daily_summary` and `sleep_level` (sleep-stage segments). Cost model: Researcher Workbench itself is free; you get ~$300 of initial cloud credits, after which you attach your own GCP billing account and pay at-cost compute/storage — a typical Jupyter/dataproc analysis runs $50–$500/month. Access requires institutional Data Use and Registration Agreement plus identity verification and the Responsible Conduct of Research training; industry/for-profit affiliation is not per se disqualifying but the institution must execute the agreement. THE BLOCKER: Controlled Tier data cannot be exported; egress of anything (including model weights) goes through review. You can learn distributions and validate methods there, but you cannot train a model in the Workbench and ship it without an explicit egress process. Plan for it as a distribution-calibration and literature-grade validation environment, not a training pipeline.

> researchallofus.org Data Sources page (fetched — confirms Fitbit HR/activity/sleep collection and WEAR study, withholds counts); Data Snapshots counts redacted at fetch time; participant/table figures from prior knowledge of CDR v7

### MIMIC-IV v3.1 verified scale, and MIMIC-IV-ED is a genuinely underrated source of post-discharge ED revisits
*[strong]*

MIMIC-IV v3.1 (published 2024-10-11): 364,627 unique patients, 546,028 hospitalizations, 94,458 ICU stays, 2008–2022 (dates shifted to 2100–2200; recover era via `anchor_year_group`). `hosp` module has ICD-9/10 diagnoses and procedures — you can pull TKA/THA via ICD-10-PCS 0SRC/0SRD (knee) and 0SR9/0SRB (hip), ICD-9 81.54/81.51, or DRG 469/470. MIMIC-IV-ED v2.2 (2023-01-05): ~425,000 ED stays 2011–2019, tables edstays/triage/vitalsign/medrecon/pyxis/diagnosis, with triage acuity 1–5, chief complaint free text, and aperiodic vitals (temp, HR, RR, SpO2, BP, pain). Because ED stays link to hosp admissions by subject_id, MIMIC-IV-ED is one of the very few public sources of post-discharge ED return within 30/90 days of an index surgical admission — which is much closer to your actual outcome than anything in an ICU table. Failure mode: BIDMC is a tertiary academic center, so elective arthroplasty volume is modest (expect low thousands of TJA hospitalizations, of which only hundreds reach ICU); and there is zero wearable or home-monitoring signal.

> physionet.org/content/mimiciv/ and /mimic-iv-ed/ (both fetched)

### PhysioNet's credentialed licence is research-only — treat MIMIC and eICU as off-limits for your shipped model
*[strong]*

PhysioNet Credentialed Health Data License 1.5.0, verbatim: 'The LICENSEE will use the data for the sole purpose of lawful use in scientific research and no other.' It also requires: 'The LICENSEE agrees to contribute code associated with publications arising from this data to a repository that is open to the research community.' The licence does not say the word 'commercial' — it is a narrowing clause, not an explicit ban — but 'scientific research and no other' plus mandatory open-sourcing of analysis code is incompatible with a proprietary commercial training pipeline. Practical rule for MedPull: use MIMIC-IV / MIMIC-IV-ED / eICU for method development, benchmark papers, and hiring/credibility, and keep an auditable wall between that work and anything in the production model artifact. If you want a commercial licence, MIT-LCP will discuss it — but budget months and do not assume yes. Access mechanics for the research path: PhysioNet account, CITI 'Data or Specimens Only Research' training (~4–8 hours, free via many institutions), signed DUA, human review — realistically 1–4 weeks, $0.

> physionet.org/about/licenses/physionet-credentialed-health-data-license-150/ (fetched)

### eICU-CRD is the densest free physiologic-deterioration corpus, at 5-minute granularity
*[moderate]*

eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0: ~200,859 ICU admissions, ~139,367 unique patients, 208 US hospitals, 2014–2015, PhysioNet credentialed (same research-only licence). The `vitalPeriodic` table carries HR, SpO2, respiratory rate, temperature and BP at 5-minute resolution — orders of magnitude denser than your daily aggregates, and the single largest free source of labelled physiologic deterioration (it also ships APACHE IVa scores and predicted vs actual mortality/LOS for benchmarking). Use case for you: pretrain or sanity-check a deterioration encoder on multivariate vital trajectories, then fine-tune/transfer to daily home data. Failure mode, and it is severe: ICU deterioration physiology is not home post-op orthopedic physiology. ICU HR variation is driven by pressors, sedation and mechanical ventilation; your patient's is driven by pain, sleep, opioids and ambulation. Domain shift will be brutal, and any transfer must be validated on real MedPull patients before it touches a risk tier. Very few elective arthroplasty patients appear in eICU at all.

> physionet.org/content/eicu-crd/ ; Pollard TJ et al., Scientific Data 5:180178 (2018)

### ACS NSQIP PUF: 963,565 cases in 2024 alone, and you almost certainly cannot get it
*[strong]*

Verified from the ACS site: the 2024 PUF contains 963,565 cases from 656 participating sites; 18 additional annual files bring the cumulative total to >12.5 million cases, with 30-day risk-adjusted outcomes (SSI including deep/organ-space, readmission, reoperation, VTE, sepsis) and rich pre-op risk variables — exactly the outcome vocabulary your reason codes should map onto, and the closest thing to a gold-standard denominator for post-op orthopedic complications in the US. TWO HARD BLOCKERS, both verified: (1) 'ACS NSQIP PUF access is a benefit of NSQIP participation and is reserved for staff at participating and active ACS NSQIP hospitals' — a startup cannot request it directly; (2) ACS prohibits 'using, uploading, sharing, or incorporating any content, materials, data, or information provided by the ACS into any third-party applications' without prior written authorization, and explicitly names AI and machine learning applications. Only viable path: a co-investigator at a participating hospital, for published benchmarking of complication rates, with ACS written authorization if anything touches a model. Cost is nominal-to-free; the constraint is eligibility, not price.

> facs.org/quality-programs/data-and-registries/acs-nsqip/participant-use-data-file/ (fetched)

### Synthea does model total joint replacement — and the module confirms synthetic data is useless for your core model
*[strong]*

Verified by reading src/main/resources/modules/total_joint_replacement.json in the Synthea repo. It models TKA (SNOMED-CT 609588000) and THA (SNOMED-CT 52734007), gated on age >=50 with the `joint_replacement` attribute set by the arthritis modules; pre-surgical evaluation encounter with functional status; 3–5 day non-urgent orthopedic inpatient admission; post-op care plan with physical therapy and stretching; moderate opioid analgesia via submodule; home health visits at 50% probability; follow-up at 30–150 days (hip) and 240–335 days (knee); 'History of artificial joint' condition with a revision loop at roughly 3.7% probability per 5 years. What it explicitly does NOT have: no complications, no readmissions, no vital-sign or wearable time series, only the success pathway. Apache 2.0, free, unlimited, no DUA, commercial use fine. Correct use: generate 10,000 synthetic TJA episodes as FHIR to load-test your ingestion, exercise your SQLAlchemy schema, seed demo environments, and build screenshots for sales — and nothing else. It cannot train, tune, or validate a deterioration model, because the generator contains no information about deterioration.

> github.com/synthetichealth/synthea, src/main/resources/modules/total_joint_replacement.json (fetched)

### NHANES accelerometry is the only large, truly unrestricted, commercially usable activity dataset in the US
*[moderate]*

NHANES 2003–2004 and 2005–2006: ActiGraph 7164, waist-worn, 1-minute epoch counts, ~14,600 participants with valid wear across both cycles. NHANES 2011–2012 (PAXMIN_G) and 2013–2014 (PAXMIN_H): ActiGraph GT3X+ wrist-worn at 80 Hz, worn 24h/day for 9 consecutive days, released as minute-level MIMS (Monitor-Independent Movement Summary) triaxial values with wear/sleep flags — roughly 6,900–7,800 examinees per cycle, multi-gigabyte files. It is US Government work in the public domain: no DUA, no application, no fee, no commercial restriction, instant download. Uniquely, it links to NHANES demographics, BMI, comorbidity questionnaires and NDI mortality linkage. Use for MedPull: build age/sex/BMI-stratified reference distributions of daily activity so your logistic recovery curve f(d) = floor + (1-floor)/(1+exp(-r(d-d50))) has an empirically grounded `floor` per patient stratum rather than a hand-picked constant. Failure mode: no HR, no HRV, no temperature, no SpO2, no surgery, cross-sectional 9-day snapshot with no longitudinal recovery trajectory.

> CDC NHANES Physical Activity Monitor files PAXMIN_G / PAXMIN_H (direct URL 404'd at fetch; specifications from prior knowledge); wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Nhanes/

### Stanford Snyder-lab COVID wearable data is publicly released and is the closest published analogue to your detection problem
*[moderate]*

Mishra, Snyder et al., Nature Biomedical Engineering 4:1208–1220 (2020). ~5,000 enrolled wearable users, 32 individuals with confirmed COVID-19 analyzed, ~26 of whom (81%) showed detectable resting-HR / steps / sleep changes; detection preceded symptom onset by a median of ~3 days (up to 9 days). Three algorithms were published, and their shapes are directly relevant to yours: RHR-Diff (offline residual analysis against a personal baseline), HROS-AD (heart-rate-over-steps anomaly detection with an unsupervised detector), and a CuSum online detector operating on standardized residuals from a rolling personal baseline. This is the single best published precedent for 'per-person baseline + control chart on consumer-wearable RHR detects an inflammatory event days before symptoms', which is exactly your infection-detection thesis. Data and code were released publicly (Stanford GCP bucket + GitHub, per the paper's data availability statement). Failure mode and the reason to be sober: with n=32 events the confidence intervals on sensitivity are enormous, and specificity in that study was poor enough that alert burden was a recognized problem — the same trap your composite index threshold (>2.0 high, >1.2 elevated) faces.

> Mishra T et al., Nature Biomedical Engineering 4:1208 (2020) — nature.com blocked automated fetch; figures from prior knowledge. Data: storage.googleapis.com/gbsc-gcp-project-ipop_public/COVID-19/

### TemPredict/Oura and both Apple studies are closed — remove them from the plan
*[strong]*

TemPredict (UCSF, Ashley Mason / Benjamin Smarr): ~65,000 Oura Ring wearers enrolled; the headline Scientific Reports 2020 analysis rested on ~50 confirmed COVID cases and reported temperature-based detection preceding symptoms. The dataset was never publicly released; access is by direct research collaboration with UCSF, negotiated case by case, with no published fee or timeline — assume 6+ months and an academic co-PI. Oura's own research program is partnership-based, not open. Apple Heart and Movement Study (Brigham/AHA/Apple): the study site (fetched) describes collection of heart, motion/activity and sound-exposure signals and emphasizes participant control of sharing, but publishes NO external researcher access mechanism, NO data-sharing policy, and NO application process. Same for the Apple Women's Health Study (Harvard Chan/NIEHS/Apple). Fitbit/Google likewise have no general public research dataset — the Fitbit COVID detection study data was not released. Practical consequence: the four largest consumer-wearable health cohorts in existence are permanently unavailable to you. Any roadmap item that depends on them should be deleted rather than deferred.

> appleheartandmovementstudy.bwh.harvard.edu (fetched — no data-sharing policy present); Smarr BL et al., Scientific Reports 10:21640 (2020)

### Small open wearable datasets worth an afternoon each, with exact sizes
*[strong]*

MMASH (verified by fetch): 22 healthy young adult males, 24 continuous hours, Polar H7 beat-to-beat RR intervals plus ActiGraph wGT3X-BT at 1-second granularity, plus salivary cortisol/melatonin and questionnaires at 10:00/14:00/18:00/22:00/09:00. Open Data Commons ODbL v1.0, open access, no login, direct ZIP/wget/AWS CLI download. Use: unit-test and validate your HRV RMSSD computation against beat-to-beat ground truth — your engine consumes vendor-summarized RMSSD, and MMASH is how you check the arithmetic. PMData (Simula): 16 participants, 5 months, Fitbit Versa 2 — steps, HR, sleep, weight, plus food and training logs; open. Capture-24 (Oxford, CC BY): 151 participants wearing Axivity AX3 at 100 Hz with wearable-camera-derived ground-truth activity labels — the labelled set behind the UKB activity classifiers; use only if you need activity-type classification. Kaggle 'FitBit Fitness Tracker Data': 30 users, 31 days, minute-level, CC0 — schema/pipeline smoke test only. AI-READI (NIH Bridge2AI): ~4,000 participants with type 2 diabetes including Garmin Vivosmart 5 wearables and CGM, tiered access via FAIRhub — worth watching, wrong disease. PAMAP2 (9 subjects) and UCI HAR (30 subjects) are HAR benchmarks only, free from UCI, and have no clinical value for you.

> physionet.org/content/mmash/1.0.0/ (fetched); PMData (Thambawita et al., MMSys 2020); Chan Chang S et al., Capture-24, Scientific Data (2024)

### Orthopedic registries: huge outcome volume, zero wearable data, months of process, and none will train your model
*[moderate]*

AJRR (AAOS): the largest US arthroplasty registry, ~3+ million hip and knee procedures from ~1,300+ institutions since 2012; Level II/III participation adds PROMs (HOOS JR, KOOS JR, PROMIS-10 Global, VR-12) and implant/revision data. External access via an AAOS Registry data-use request through the Registry Analytics Institute; industry requests are accepted but fee-based and negotiated; expect a research committee cycle of 3–6 months. NJR (England, Wales, NI, IoM): >3.5 million procedures, access via the NJR Research Committee with fees in the low thousands of GBP and a 3–6 month cycle; commercial applicants accepted. AOANJRR (Australia): >1.7 million procedures, fee-based data requests, routinely used by implant manufacturers. Swedish Arthroplasty Register: ~600k+ procedures but requires Swedish ethics approval — impractical for a US startup. FORCE-TJR: ~30,000 TJR patients with paired pre/post PROMs from ~130 surgeons in 22 states, the most PROM-rich US TJR cohort, access only by direct negotiation with the UMass team. MARCQI: ~330,000+ procedures from ~75 Michigan hospitals, BCBSM-funded, access effectively restricted to member sites. Bottom line: use registries to source the priors your risk tiers encode (revision rates, 90-day PJI ~0.5–1%, MUA after TKA ~2–4%, 90-day readmission ~4–6%) and to find clinical champions — not as training data. Not one of them records a single wearable observation.

> aaos.org/registries/ (fetch failed with header overflow); NJR, AOANJRR and MARCQI annual reports; Franklin PD et al. on FORCE-TJR

### Claims databases: correct for base rates and denominators, wrong for anything time-resolved, $25k–$200k+
*[weak]*

PearlDiver Mariner: all-payer de-identified claims on roughly 160–180 million US patients, 2010–present, the workhorse of orthopedic health-services research (CPT 27447 TKA, 27130 THA, with 30/90-day complication and readmission tracking). Pricing is negotiated: academic single-seat subsets have historically run ~$25k–$50k/year; commercial licences materially higher ($75k–$200k+). Turnaround is fast — it is a vendor, so weeks not months. Merative MarketScan Commercial Claims and Encounters: ~30–40M covered lives per year, 250M+ unique patients since 1995, priced roughly $50k–$150k+/year depending on files and year-span. Premier Healthcare Database: ~1,300 hospitals, ~25% of US inpatient discharges, with chargemaster line-item detail by hospital DAY — the only claims-adjacent source with day-level post-op resolution (day-3 antibiotic starts, day-2 imaging), sold project-wise by Premier Applied Sciences at roughly $50k–$250k. Vizient Clinical Data Base is a membership benefit for member hospitals and is not purchasable by a startup — drop it. Universal failure mode: claims contain no vitals, no wearables, no PROMs, and no timestamps finer than a service date; and they miss anything that did not generate a bill. Buy claims to answer 'what fraction of TKA patients are readmitted by day 90, and for what', which is exactly what you need to set alert thresholds — not to train a detector.

> pearldiverinc.com (pricing page 404'd at fetch); Merative MarketScan product pages; Premier Applied Sciences — all figures from prior knowledge, verify by quote

### CMS data: for-profit entities are routed to the CCW VRDC, and the timeline is the real cost
*[moderate]*

Verified from ResDAC: 'For-profit organizations and innovators must use the CCW VRDC' — the research VRDC path is not open to you, and CMS has progressively pushed identifiable-data users into the virtual environment rather than shipping physical files. Neither ResDAC nor the CCW pricing page yielded figures to automated fetch (403/404), so treat these as unverified: Limited Data Set files have historically been priced per file per year in the low thousands of dollars; 100% Research Identifiable Files require a DUA plus privacy-board review; VRDC seats have historically run in the tens of thousands of dollars per year per project. The dominant cost is time: DUA negotiation, IRB/privacy board, and CMS review realistically take 3–9 months. What you would get: Medicare fee-for-service claims covering essentially every TKA/THA in the 65+ population, with 90-day episode outcomes — the definitive denominator for the CJR/bundled-payment framing that your RTM product's ROI story depends on. What you would not get: any vital sign, any wearable observation, any PROM. Recommendation: do not pursue CMS in year one; buy PearlDiver instead if you need orthopedic base rates fast, and revisit CMS only when a payer contract makes the episode economics load-bearing.

> resdac.org/cms-virtual-research-data-center-vrdc (fetched — for-profit/CCW routing confirmed, no pricing); ccwdata.org pricing page 404 at fetch

### Generative time-series synthesis cannot manufacture the information you lack, and is actively dangerous for clinical validation
*[strong]*

The candidate methods are TimeGAN (Yoon et al., NeurIPS 2019), DoppelGANger (Lin et al., IMC 2020; implemented in `gretel-synthetics`), TimeVAE, and diffusion models (Diffusion-TS, TSDiff). All were demonstrated on thousands-to-millions of sequences; TimeGAN's own experiments use datasets with >10k sequences and it is well documented to mode-collapse and to memorize on small data. With 10–1000 patients and single-digit-percent event rates you have perhaps 1–50 positive trajectories — a generator fit to that will reproduce those exact trajectories, and any classifier trained on its output will report an inflated AUROC that evaporates on real patients. INAPPROPRIATE AT YOUR SCALE, unambiguously. Legitimate uses of synthetic data for you, in order of value: (1) Synthea-generated FHIR for pipeline/schema/load testing; (2) privacy-safe demo and sales environments; (3) parametric simulation — not learned generation — where YOU specify the recovery curve, the noise model and the injected anomaly, to compute the detection power of your EWMA/CUSUM configuration analytically (this is the right way to justify lambda=0.3, L=2.66 and h=5.0 to a clinician). Never permissible: reporting any performance number derived from synthetic data as evidence of clinical validity, or including synthetic patients in a validation denominator.

> Yoon J et al., 'Time-series Generative Adversarial Networks', NeurIPS 2019; Lin Z et al., 'Using GANs for Sharing Networked Time Series Data', IMC 2020; gretelai/gretel-synthetics

### Patient-donated data (OpenAPS model) is the governance template you should copy, not a dataset to consume
*[moderate]*

The OpenAPS Data Commons collected CGM and insulin-pump data from ~180+ people with type 1 diabetes who explicitly donated it, originally brokered through Open Humans (which wound down as a platform, forcing migration of hosted datasets to Zenodo and similar archives). Content is irrelevant to orthopedics. The transferable lesson is the mechanism: a plain-language donation consent, tiered access (open summary tier + credentialed row-level tier), a named data steward, and per-project review — built by a tiny volunteer team and accepted by an IRB-adjacent community. For MedPull this is the single cheapest path to a proprietary training corpus: build donation consent into your patient onboarding from day one, with a separate, revocable, opt-in checkbox for 'allow my de-identified recovery data to improve the product', and a data-retention and withdrawal policy. Every month you ship without that checkbox is a month of unusable data. The cautionary lesson is equally important: Open Humans' shutdown shows that a single-platform dependency for donated data is a real continuity risk — hold your own copy under your own DUA, do not outsource the custody.

> OpenAPS Data Commons; Open Humans platform wind-down (2024); openaps.org/outcomes/


## Implications for backend

- Build the data-donation consent into patient onboarding THIS quarter, before anything else on this list. A separate, revocable, opt-in checkbox ('allow my de-identified recovery data to improve the product') plus a retention/withdrawal policy is the only mechanism that turns your 10–1000 patients into a proprietary corpus. Every month shipped without it is a month of permanently unusable data, and no external dataset substitutes for it. Schema implication: add `consent_research_use` (bool), `consent_version` (str), `consent_timestamp`, and `withdrawal_timestamp` to the patient model, and make every analytics query filter on them.
- Create a hard, auditable licence boundary in the codebase between research-only data and product data. MIMIC-IV, MIMIC-IV-ED and eICU are 'scientific research and no other' with mandatory open-sourcing of analysis code; ACS NSQIP prohibits incorporation into third-party AI/ML applications; All of Us Controlled Tier data cannot leave the Workbench. Practical structure: a separate `research/` repo or top-level directory with its own README stating the licence, never imported by `backend/app/engine`, plus a CI check that fails if any module under `app/` imports from it. Record the provenance and licence of every fitted constant that reaches production in a `PARAMETER_PROVENANCE.md` — you will be asked in diligence.
- Add explicit provenance and calibration metadata to the engine's parameters rather than leaving them as literals. The EWMA (lambda=0.3, L=2.66), CUSUM (k=0.5, h=5.0), composite weights (RHR .25, temp .25, HRV .20, RR .15, steps .15) and thresholds (2.0 / 1.2) are currently unjustified constants. Refactor them into a versioned config object carrying, per parameter, its source (textbook default vs LifeSnaps-calibrated vs NHANES-derived), the dataset and n it was calibrated on, and the measured false-alarm rate. Golden-tier tests should pin the config version, not scattered literals.
- Instrument and log the realized alert rate per patient-month per tier in production, from day one, as a first-class metric. The LifeSnaps replay gives you a specificity target; only production telemetry tells you whether you are meeting it. Emit a structured event on every tier transition with the contributing reason codes, and build a weekly rollup of alerts-per-patient-month by tier. If 'high' exceeds ~0.5/patient-month you have an alert-fatigue emergency regardless of what the offline numbers said.
- Treat the coverage confidence gate (>=3 of 6 metrics, low if <40% of last 7 days) as a fairness surface, not just a data-quality check, and log its firing rate stratified by patient age and any demographics you hold. Device wear adherence is strongly patterned by age and socioeconomic status; a naive gate silently downgrades monitoring for exactly the patients at highest risk. Measure this in All of Us before you have enough patients to measure it yourself, and add a stratified-adherence panel to the provider console.
- Make the recovery-curve `floor` parameter per-stratum rather than per-procedure only. Refactor f(d) = floor + (1-floor)/(1+exp(-r(d-d50))) so `floor` is looked up from an age/sex/BMI-stratified table seeded from NHANES quantile regression, with the current per-procedure constant as the fallback for missing demographics. This is a small, self-contained change that removes a real demographic bias from the ratio-to-expected metric for steps and walking speed.
- Store intraday accelerometry in a form compatible with the OxWearables pretrained encoder if you ever intend to use it: 3-axis, resampled to 30 Hz, gravity-calibrated in g units, in 10-second windows. If you only ever persist daily aggregates you permanently foreclose the single best free pretraining asset available. At minimum, retain raw intraday samples for consenting patients in cold storage (S3 Glacier) even if the engine only consumes dailies today — SQLite is the wrong home for this, so plan the Postgres/object-store split now.
- Design the outcome-label schema now, before you have labels. Rare-outcome studies fail on inconsistent adjudication, not on modelling. Add an `outcome_event` table with event type (PJI / readmission / MUA / ED visit / VTE), the adjudication criteria version (e.g. MSIS 2018 for PJI), adjudicator identity, date of event, days-from-surgery, and a free-text note. With single-digit-percent event rates you will have 15–35 events at n=300 — every single one must be adjudicated identically or your sensitivity estimate is noise.
- Do not build a supervised deterioration classifier on your own data at current scale, and structure the code so nobody is tempted to. With 10–1000 patients and single-digit-percent event rates you will have 1–50 positives; any learned model will overfit and report a fictitious AUROC. Keep the deterministic rule engine as the decision-maker; use external data and simulation to tune its thresholds; and reserve the prospective cohort purely for locked-model evaluation. If you need per-patient uncertainty, use conformal prediction (mapie/crepes) over the existing deviation index rather than a new learned model.
- Build a parametric simulator — not a learned generator — for detection-power analysis, and make it a testable module. Specify the recovery curve, the per-metric noise model (fit to LifeSnaps), and an injected anomaly of known magnitude and onset, then compute the probability your EWMA/CUSUM configuration detects it within N days. This is the artifact that justifies lambda=0.3 and h=5.0 to a clinician or a reviewer, and it is far more defensible than a TimeGAN. Keep it well away from anything that could be mistaken for validation evidence.
- Use Synthea for pipeline and demo data explicitly and label it as such everywhere. Generate 10k synthetic TJA episodes as FHIR from the total_joint_replacement module to load-test ingestion and exercise the SQLAlchemy schema, and tag every synthetic record with an unmistakable `is_synthetic=True` flag that propagates through the API. The module has no complications, no readmissions and no vitals, so any code path that treats it as clinically meaningful is a bug waiting to become a demo disaster.
