# aiarch — adversarial critiques


## Verdict
Conditionally approvable — the deterministic-first, LLM-as-renderer architecture is the right clinical-safety skeleton and most of the DO-NOT lists are correct, but as a package these decisions are safe on paper and fail-open in practice. The recommendations obsess over what the LLM might say and are nearly silent on the classic ways monitoring products actually kill patients: the unmatched free-text reply nobody triages, the alert nobody acknowledges over a long weekend, the patient who texted STOP and vanished from the roster, the summary that omits the wound-drainage flag while passing every faithfulness gate, and the stale cached tier a clinician trusts as current. Every one of those is a concrete missed-red-flag or false-reassurance pathway present in the current design. Additionally, the FDA decision rests on a January 2026 guidance revision I could not independently retrieve (the cited fda.gov PDF URL 404'd), and it contradicts the SMS decision's keyword-triggered 911 auto-replies. I would not enroll my own post-op patients until the must-change list is implemented and an end-to-end shadow-mode sensitivity pilot against nurse phone triage passes a predefined threshold.

### Objections

**TARGET:** SMS check-in decision — deterministic keyword/branch red-flag screen with LLM classification afterward

- **OBJECTION:** The screen fails open. Keyword matching misses misspellings ('cant breth', 'hart racing'), voice-to-text garble, emoji, negation, and any non-English reply ('me duele el pecho y no puedo respirar' matches zero English keywords). The design then hands the unmatched text to an LLM classifier that can bucket it into a benign reason code, and template acknowledgments ('Glad you're on track!') can be sent in reply to a message that contained the missed symptom. Nothing in the decision states what happens to replies that match no branch and no keyword — which in practice means they are silently dropped.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** A Spanish-speaking patient reports dyspnea and chest pain on POD 6; the screen matches nothing, the LLM classifies it as 'general discomfort', the bot replies with a cheerful acknowledgment, and the patient — falsely reassured that the practice saw the message — dies of a pulmonary embolism at home. This is the single most probable severe-harm pathway in the entire product.
- **FIX:** Make triage fail-closed: any inbound free text that matches no deterministic branch/keyword — including detected non-English, unparseable, or low-confidence content — routes to a human review queue with a same-business-day SLA and gets a static reply directing the patient to call the practice/911 if urgent. LLM classification is strictly monotonic: it may ADD flags, never clear, downgrade, or gate one. Suppress all acknowledgment templates whenever any flag fires or any reply is unclassified. The keyword/misspelling list is authored, signed, and periodically re-reviewed by the supervising surgeon, and tested against a corpus of real misspelled/translated symptom reports.

**TARGET:** Entire worklist/alerting model across all six decisions

- **OBJECTION:** Every decision assumes a clinician reads the worklist promptly, and none of them designs for that assumption being false. There is no alert lifecycle (new→acknowledged→resolved), no unacknowledged-alert aging or escalation, no after-hours/weekend/vacation coverage requirement, and no named accountable owner per practice. Worse, RTM economics actively work against vigilance: 98980 pays for 20 minutes a month, not daily review, so the billing model funds exactly the inattention the safety model cannot tolerate.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** A fever-plus-wound-drainage flag lands on the worklist Friday at 4:55pm and is first seen Monday morning — a deep periprosthetic infection that needed washout within 24-48 hours now needs explant. 'An alert nobody reads' is the most common failure mode in every RTM/RPM malpractice case, and this design has no defense against it.
- **FIX:** Go-live prerequisites per practice: a named clinician owner and documented coverage policy including after-hours; an auditable alert lifecycle with timestamps; automatic escalation of unacknowledged red-flag items after a defined SLA (e.g., 4 business hours: notify on-call, and send the patient a static 'we have not been able to review your report — if symptoms persist call the practice / seek urgent care' message); and a weekly report to the practice of alert-response times. If a practice cannot staff this, do not enroll their patients.

**TARGET:** SMS check-in + runtime decisions — silence treated as absence of signal

- **OBJECTION:** Non-response and opt-out are invisible. A patient who texts STOP (carrier-mandated, processed automatically at the aggregator level, potentially without your application ever branching on it) or who simply stops answering disappears from monitoring with no clinician notification. The deviation engine compounds this: no data in → no deviation out → the patient renders as unremarkable or low-risk. The coverage-confidence gate exists in the analytics but no decision requires 'insufficient data' to be a distinct, elevated attention state on the worklist.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Two flavors of harm: (1) the practice believes a patient is being monitored who is not — the obtunded, readmitted-elsewhere, or deteriorating patient is precisely the one who stops replying; (2) a patient with two check-ins in ten days is displayed as low-risk when the honest answer is 'we do not know.' Both are false reassurance delivered to the clinician, the harder-to-detect variant of false reassurance delivered to the patient.
- **FIX:** STOP/opt-out, delivery failures, and N consecutive missed check-ins become first-class typed reason codes that surface as worklist events with their own tier; enrollment/monitoring status is displayed on every patient view; low-coverage patients are ranked as an explicit 'insufficient data — needs outreach' state above routine patients and are never rendered with a green/low-risk tier; a monitoring-gap outreach workflow (call or letter) is triggered and documented.

**TARGET:** LLM QA decision — three-gate verifier (schema, numeric fidelity, attribution/entailment)

- **OBJECTION:** All three gates verify what the narrative says; none verifies what it omits. Numeric-fidelity checks catch fabricated numbers, and MiniCheck-style entailment confirms every stated claim is grounded — but a summary that silently drops the fever mention or the wound-drainage reason code is 100% faithful, 100% schema-valid, and passes all three gates while being the most dangerous artifact the clinician will read that day. Omission is the dominant failure mode of summarization, and the pipeline as specified is blind to it. Relatedly, the decision's own human-factors evidence (radiologists at ~20% accuracy under wrong AI suggestions) argues that the narrative will anchor the clinician regardless of what the raw data says.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Clinician reads 'recovering as expected, mild pain trending down,' never expands the raw check-in, and misses the drainage flag the deterministic engine correctly raised — automation bias converts a correct analytics bundle into a missed infection.
- **FIX:** Add a fourth, pure-Python completeness gate: every red-flag and elevated-tier reason code present in the analytics bundle MUST appear (by ID-level mapping) in the rendered narrative, else regenerate-once-then-deterministic-fallback. Deterministic tier and typed reason codes are always displayed adjacent to — never replaceable by — the narrative, and worklist ordering is driven exclusively by the deterministic tier, never by any property of LLM text. Log every fallback event; silent-to-the-clinician is acceptable, silent-to-the-QA-dashboard is not.

**TARGET:** Runtime architecture decision — event-driven precompute, cache-by-hash, nightly reconciliation

- **OBJECTION:** The precompute design has no freshness contract. If SQS backs up, a worker throws into the DLQ, a webhook is dropped, or a hash-canonicalization bug poisons the cache, the clinician page serves yesterday's tier with today's date and no visible indication — and the decision's own cost framing ('nightly reconciliation') institutionalizes up-to-24-hour staleness with no priority lane for red-flag ingests. Separately, the decision correctly condemns SQLite-in-S3-with-a-lock-file but does not make the Postgres migration a before-first-patient gate; serialized writes losing a concurrent webhook means a check-in — possibly the febrile one — is lost with no trace.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Clinician trusts a number that is not trustworthy in the most literal sense: acts on a 'low deviation' tier computed before the fever check-in arrived, or never sees a check-in that a write collision silently discarded.
- **FIX:** Per-patient 'data as of / last check-in processed' timestamp on every worklist row and patient view, with an explicit staleness banner (not the old tier) beyond a threshold; CloudWatch alarms with human paging on queue age and DLQ depth; red-flag-bearing ingests processed on a priority path ahead of batch work, not the nightly job; end-to-end ingest acknowledgment (webhook receipt → processed row) reconciled daily with alerting on gaps; Postgres migration completed and load-tested for concurrent webhooks BEFORE the first real patient, not as a scaling improvement.

**TARGET:** FDA decision × SMS decision — internal contradiction on patient-facing urgent messaging, atop an unverifiable guidance citation

- **OBJECTION:** Two problems. (1) The FDA decision's DO-NOT list forbids time-critical alarms and any software recommendation reaching a patient without clinician review, while the SMS decision mandates an instant, keyword-triggered 'call 911' auto-reply — a patient-facing, time-critical, automated response to a detected symptom. It may well be defensible as static triage messaging rather than CDS, but the two decisions assert opposite postures and nobody has reconciled them; and over-broad keywords (e.g., 'pain' in a post-op population where 100% of patients have pain) firing 911-tier advice will produce cry-wolf desensitization before the real PE. (2) The entire non-device analysis rests on a January 29, 2026 guidance revision whose cited PDF (fda.gov/media/109618/download) returned 404 when I attempted verification; a document that load-bearing cannot be accepted on a research agent's say-so.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Either regulatory exposure from an unexamined patient-facing triage function, or — clinically worse — patients trained by false 911 alarms to ignore the one that matters. And if the guidance claim is wrong or misread, the whole 'we are comfortably non-device' posture is built on sand.
- **FIX:** Regulatory counsel (not an agent) verifies the current CDS guidance text and issue date and signs a memo on (a) the risk-tier/basis-panel posture and (b) specifically whether keyword-triggered urgent auto-replies are static safety messaging or a device function. Implement tiered static responses signed by the supervising surgeon: 911-tier reserved for chest pain / dyspnea / stroke signs / uncontrolled bleeding; 'call the practice today' tier for fever, drainage, escalating calf swelling; audit over- and under-triage rates quarterly.

**TARGET:** RAG decision — practice protocol and patient-education corpus served via cached prompt

- **OBJECTION:** Letting an LLM paraphrase the practice's own protocols is an unforced clinical error channel that the banned-diagnostic-language filter does nothing to catch: 'non-weight-bearing 6 weeks' rendered as 'take it easy on that leg,' or a superseded anticoagulation duration served from a stale cached prompt after the surgeon updates the pathway. The decision specifies prompt caching but no cache invalidation on protocol edit and no verbatim-rendering requirement for safety-critical instructions.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Patient mobilizes on a non-weight-bearing extremity (hardware failure, fall) or stops DVT prophylaxis early (PE) because the education text drifted from the surgeon's actual, current protocol.
- **FIX:** Protocol documents are version-stamped with effective dates and a named approving clinician; safety-critical content (weight-bearing status, medication instructions, wound care, driving, red-flag lists) is rendered verbatim from the current document, never LLM-paraphrased; any protocol edit invalidates all derived caches; and every protocol-derived patient-facing artifact passes the same named-clinician review gate as drafted messages (which is also the AB 3030 safe harbor the decisions already rely on).

**TARGET:** Model training decision — future GBT/logistic risk model triggered at 200-500 outcome-labeled episodes

- **OBJECTION:** The trigger is set on episodes, not events. At a realistic 5-10% complication rate, 200-500 episodes yields 10-50 outcome events — an order of magnitude below accepted events-per-variable minimums for a stable, calibrated clinical prediction model. A model trained at that size will be miscalibrated in exactly the subgroups (elderly, diabetic, revision arthroplasty) where it matters most, and its scikit-learn respectability will make clinicians trust it more than the rule table it replaced.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Systematic false reassurance for identifiable patient subgroups, delivered with the authority of a 'trained model' — a downgrade from the current transparent rule tier disguised as an upgrade.
- **FIX:** Restate the trigger in events: ≥100-200 outcome events minimum, plus mandatory temporal/external validation, published calibration (not just discrimination) metrics, subgroup performance reporting, and a shadow-mode period where the learned tier runs silently alongside the rule tier with discordance review — it influences no worklist until it demonstrably beats the rules on that comparison. Keep typed reason codes as the displayed basis regardless.

**TARGET:** All six decisions collectively — absence of end-to-end clinical validation before go-live

- **OBJECTION:** Every evaluation artifact proposed (golden regression suite, RAGAS harness, LLM jury, red-team suite) measures text quality of the LLM layer. Nothing anywhere measures the clinical sensitivity of the whole pipeline — SMS delivery → patient comprehension at sixth-grade level → red-flag screen → worklist → human response — against the standard of care it will quietly displace, which is a triage nurse on the phone. The product will reduce calls to the office (Campbell 2019: 2.6 → 0.6 calls); if its capture of what those calls used to catch is even modestly imperfect, it has destroyed a working safety channel and replaced it with an unmeasured one.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** The system's real-world red-flag miss rate is discovered on a live patient instead of in a pilot — the exact scenario a safety officer exists to prevent.
- **FIX:** Mandatory shadow-mode pilot before autonomous operation: 30-60 patients receive both the SMS system and usual-care nurse check-in calls; every red flag caught by either channel is adjudicated; predefined acceptance criterion (e.g., 100% sensitivity for the surgeon-defined critical symptom set, with documented root-cause and remediation for any miss) gates go-live. Repeat a scaled-down version whenever the keyword screen, question flow, or classifier changes materially.

**TARGET:** SMS check-in decision — 'not monitored in real time' disclosure as the primary defense

- **OBJECTION:** The disclosure is necessary but the decision over-relies on it. A recurring footer habituates within days (the same habituation logic the QA decision correctly applies to the guardrail sentence), and the interaction design fights the disclosure: a bot that replies instantly to every message behaviorally teaches patients that someone is watching in real time, whatever the footer says. Patients in pain at 2am will text the bot instead of calling, because the bot always answers.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** A patient reports crescendo chest pain to the bot at 2am, receives an instant automated reply, and waits for the practice to 'get back to them' — the instant-response behavior overrode the written disclosure, as human-factors evidence predicts it will.
- **FIX:** Design the behavior, not just the text: after-hours inbound messages containing any flag or any unmatched free text get an immediate static reply that leads with 'This line is not monitored right now. If you are worried, call 911 or [after-hours line] NOW' before anything else; comprehension of the non-real-time model is verified at enrollment (teach-back question in the onboarding flow, documented); and the consent process is delivered by a human, not only a text blob.

### Must change
- Fail-closed inbound triage: any SMS reply matching no deterministic branch/keyword — including non-English, misspelled, or unparseable text — routes to a human review queue with a same-business-day SLA and a static urgent-care-directions reply; it is never silently dropped or left to LLM classification alone.
- LLM classification is monotonic: it may add flags but can never clear, downgrade, rephrase, or gate a red-flag escalation; all acknowledgment templates are suppressed whenever any flag fires or any reply is unclassified.
- Alert lifecycle with escalation: auditable new→acknowledged→resolved states, automatic escalation of unacknowledged red-flag items after a defined SLA, a named accountable clinician per practice, and a documented after-hours/weekend coverage policy as a go-live prerequisite — no coverage, no enrollment.
- STOP/opt-out, delivery failures, and consecutive missed check-ins become typed worklist events; monitoring status is displayed on every patient view; low-coverage patients render as an explicit 'insufficient data — needs outreach' state, never as low-risk.
- Add a completeness gate to the QA pipeline: every red-flag/elevated-tier reason code in the analytics bundle must appear in the narrative or the renderer falls back to deterministic text; deterministic tier and reason codes are always displayed alongside the narrative and are the sole driver of worklist ordering.
- Freshness contract in the runtime: per-patient 'data as of' timestamps with staleness banners, paging alarms on SQS queue age and DLQ depth, a priority processing lane for red-flag-bearing ingests, and completion of the Postgres migration (with concurrent-webhook testing) before the first real patient.
- Regulatory counsel independently verifies the January 2026 CDS guidance (my retrieval of the cited fda.gov PDF failed) and rules on the keyword-triggered 911 auto-reply, resolving the contradiction between the FDA decision's 'no time-critical patient-facing recommendations' and the SMS decision's design; implement surgeon-signed tiered responses (911-tier vs call-practice-today tier) to prevent alarm fatigue.
- Version-stamped, clinician-approved protocol documents with cache invalidation on edit; safety-critical patient instructions (weight-bearing, medications, wound care) rendered verbatim, never LLM-paraphrased.
- Restate the future tabular-risk-model trigger in outcome events (≥100-200 events), with calibration reporting, subgroup validation, and shadow-mode comparison against the rule tier before it touches any worklist.
- Shadow-mode clinical pilot before autonomous operation: 30-60 patients on both the SMS system and usual-care nurse calls, adjudicated red-flag sensitivity against a predefined acceptance threshold (target 100% on the surgeon-defined critical set), repeated after material changes to the screen or classifier.
- Groq BAA (or Bedrock rerouting) before any PHI-bearing transcript leaves the system — already stated in two decisions; elevate it from recommendation to hard launch blocker with an owner and date.

### Endorsed
- No free-form LLM conversation for check-ins; deterministic branching state machine with the LLM confined to post-screen classification and template rendering — this is the single most important safety call in the package and it is correct.
- The LLM must never suppress, rephrase, or gate a red-flag escalation, and no LLM anywhere in deviation detection or risk scoring — correct and non-negotiable.
- Deterministic-first architecture with LLM as renderer, silent fallback to deterministic text, banned-diagnostic-language filter, and mandatory guardrail sentence — right skeleton (with the completeness-gate and adjacency additions above).
- Do not build RAG now; specifically the refusal to put vector top-k retrieval anywhere near roster queries — the 'no recall guarantee for who-reported-fever' argument is exactly the right safety reasoning, and deterministic red-flag tagging at ingest is the correct upgrade.
- No MCP anywhere in a PHI path today; no community MCP servers; no write-capable tools on any future facade — the lethal-trifecta analysis is sound and the read-only-facade re-evaluation posture is appropriately conservative.
- Train nothing today; no medical open models as generators; the observation that their clinical-use-forbidden licenses remove no validation burden is correct; the eval-and-logging flywheel to accumulate clinician-verified data is the right preparatory move.
- Do not use an LLM judge as a runtime production gate; do not let Llama judge Llama; frozen golden regression suite in CI graded by deterministic checks first — all correct.
- Do not claim readmission reduction anywhere — respecting the null Bressman 2024 RCT over the favorable 2022 cohort is intellectually honest and legally wise; sell engagement, triage efficiency, and PRO capture instead.
- KOOS JR/HOOS JR delivered intact via web link at fixed timepoints, never conversationally decomposed — correct; conversational decomposition would invalidate the scores.
- Clinician review before any software-generated content reaches a patient (device-boundary logic and AB 3030 safe harbor simultaneously) — correct, and the basis-panel requirement (inputs, missing-data flags, plain-language algorithm description) is genuinely good clinical design independent of its regulatory purpose.
- No 98980/98981 billing off an SMS thread; defer 98977 pending a device position — correct reading of the billing traps.
- Postgres with practice_id + RLS from day one, no PHI in SMS bodies, no PHI in CloudWatch logs, no third-party trackers on patient-linked pages, AWS End User Messaging over Twilio for BAA reasons — all correct (my independent web verification of the AWS messaging HIPAA claim was blocked by budget; it matches my prior knowledge but should be confirmed against the current HIPAA-eligible services list when signing the AWS BAA addendum).
- Composite deviation index framed as deviation-from-expected-recovery rather than condition likelihood, and no continuous wearable streams into the analytics — the right side of every bright line identified.

## Verdict
Unusually regulatory-literate for an engineering plan — the four-criteria CDS analysis, the clinician-review gate, the refusal to let the LLM touch the risk tier, and the priced 510(k) fallback are all correct — but it is NOT clearable as written. Three findings are disqualifying until fixed: (1) the RTM billing design contradicts the non-device strategy at its core — the entire CPT 9897x family presupposes monitoring via a §201(h) medical device, so 'bill 98975/98980 now, defer only 98977' means telling CMS you are a device while telling FDA you are not, which is False Claims Act exposure, not a deferral; (2) the LLM-generated 'suggested actions' feature originates clinical recommendation content that the deterministic engine never produced, failing Criterion 4 (no faithfully displayable basis) by the team's own stated logic, which it applied to the risk tier but forgot to apply here; (3) the keyword-triggered 'call 911' auto-reply is software-generated patient-facing triage — a device per se under the bright line the team itself drew — and needs to be re-engineered and documented as static pre-disclosed safety messaging, not analysis. Beyond those: the permissive reading of a twice-revised, six-month-old guidance I could not re-verify today should be treated as perishable (the statute, not the guidance, is the shelter, and the daily-check-in deviation-surveillance use pattern is closer to the eCART/QNL device fact pattern than to the guidance's episodic planning-risk example — marketing discipline is the only thing separating them); the state-AI analysis is one state deep (missing Utah AIPA's proactive GenAI disclosure for licensed-healthcare interactions, the Colorado AI Act now presumptively effective June 30, 2026, and Texas TRAIGA); and the SMS plan has carrier registration but no TCPA consent/revocation architecture, a strict-liability, class-action-magnet gap. All fixable; nothing requires abandoning a recommendation.

### Objections

**TARGET:** Conversational check-in / RTM billing design ('Bill RTM with a human monthly phone call for 98980 and defer 98977 until counsel blesses a device position')

- **OBJECTION:** The RTM code family is not severable the way the plan assumes. AMA CPT prefatory language and CMS (CY2022 PFS final rule, 86 FR 65111 et seq.) define RTM as monitoring via a 'medical device as defined by the FDA' under FD&C §201(h); 98975 (setup/education on the equipment) and 98980/98981 (RTM treatment management) presuppose that device-based RTM is actually occurring. Billing any 9897x code while the flagship FDA strategy is 'we are not a device' is an internal contradiction: you cannot represent device status to CMS and non-device status to FDA about the same data flow. The plan's 'defer 98977 only' framing shows the team believes the device requirement attaches only to the supply code — it attaches to the service definition.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Claims submitted without a defensible §201(h) device predicate are False Claims Act exposure (31 U.S.C. §3729 — treble damages plus per-claim penalties), and RTM is an active OIG/MAC audit target. Conversely, papering a device position carelessly to save the billing hands FDA an admission against the CDS exemption.
- **FIX:** The positions ARE reconcilable, but only deliberately: bifurcate the product so the data-capture front end (the SMS check-in/PRO module) is taken as a §201(h) SaMD — potentially 510(k)-exempt Class I with registration/listing and Part 820 hygiene — while the analytics/CDS back end remains non-device under 520(o)(1)(E) (device definition and premarket-review obligation are separate questions). Get a written coding-and-coverage opinion memorializing the bifurcation before the first claim; until then bill no 9897x codes at all. Separately: document real 20-minute time and synchronous audio for 98980 (correctly identified), the 16-day data requirement for 98977, and never let LLM-templated encounter notes populate time entries.

**TARGET:** Existing product feature: LLM-generated 'suggested actions' (and the QA plan's failure to single it out)

- **OBJECTION:** The deterministic engine outputs numbers and typed reason codes — it does not output clinical actions. Therefore the LLM is ORIGINATING treatment-management recommendation content, not rendering deterministic output, and the whole 'LLM is just a renderer' defense collapses for this one feature. Criterion 4 of §520(o)(1)(E) requires the HCP be able to independently review the basis; the team's own FDA analysis concedes 'an LLM cannot currently explain its basis faithfully' but applies that only to the risk tier. A generative model choosing which actions to suggest has no displayable basis, and the moment a suggestion becomes specific and directive ('order a wound culture', 'start DVT prophylaxis') it also crosses Criterion 3's informing-vs-directing line.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** This is the single feature most likely to make the entire product a device in a reviewer's eyes — it converts 'analytics with narrative gloss' into 'software that recommends treatment with an unexplainable basis', and it undermines the credibility of the otherwise-strong non-device memo.
- **FIX:** Move action content into the deterministic layer: a versioned, clinician-authored action library keyed 1:1 to typed reason codes, reviewed like a formulary. The LLM may only SELECT from and lightly phrase library entries; the basis panel displays the reason-code-to-action mapping; action text stays at the 'consider per your practice protocol' genus level, never patient-specific orders. This costs a week and closes the biggest hole in the pipeline.

**TARGET:** Conversational check-in: deterministic red-flag screen with instant 'call 911' auto-reply

- **OBJECTION:** By the plan's own bright line ('any recommendation delivered to the patient rather than the clinician is categorically a device'), a keyword-triggered automated message that analyzes a patient's symptom report and returns an urgency disposition to the patient is patient-facing triage software — the symptom-checker category FDA treats as device CDS, and in its alarm framing it is adjacent to the guidance's alarm/life-threatening device example. The plan treats this as obviously safe because it is deterministic; determinism is irrelevant to the patient-facing analysis. Note the bind: this feature is also ethically and liability-wise mandatory — you cannot ship SMS check-ins without it.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** An unexamined device function embedded in the safety-critical path — the worst place to discover device status, because you can neither remove it nor defend it without a pre-written rationale.
- **FIX:** Engineer and document it as static safety messaging, not analysis: (a) trivially simple, fully disclosed keyword/branch logic, no scoring and no LLM anywhere in the path (the plan already gets the no-LLM part right); (b) message text framed as standing safety instructions identical to pre-disclosed consent language ('If you have chest pain or trouble breathing, call 911 now') — never 'our system has determined/detected'; (c) simultaneous alert to the practice; (d) a signed risk-analysis memo concluding low-risk general safety messaging consistent with FDA's historical posture on generic 911-referral, written NOW, not during an inspection. If counsel concludes it is nonetheless a device function, that memo is also your enforcement-discretion position.

**TARGET:** FDA boundary decision: reliance on the January 29, 2026 CDS guidance's permissive risk-prediction example

- **OBJECTION:** Two problems. First, durability: the guidance was revised twice in one month (Jan 6 → Jan 29, 2026), is non-binding, and I could not re-verify it today (fda.gov/media/109618/download returned 404 from my environment) — the statute's four criteria are the only durable shelter, and the memo should be written statute-first with the guidance as persuasive gloss. Second, and more substantively: the safe-harbor example is an EPISODIC risk prediction 'for planning' (e.g., 90-day post-op risk reviewed by a clinician). MedPull's actual use pattern — daily check-ins feeding EWMA/CUSUM deviation detection that surfaces a prioritized worklist — is ONGOING deterioration surveillance, which is the eCART/QNL fact pattern (a cleared device, K233253, which the team itself cites as its predicate). What separates MedPull from its own named predicate is purely operational: non-time-critical framing, no same-day urgency semantics, full basis display. That separation lives in marketing copy, UI microcopy, notification wording, and sales decks — none of which engineering controls.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** Intended use is constructed from promotional materials. One sales deck saying 'catch complications before they become readmissions' simultaneously destroys the CDS exemption (detection claim referencing a condition) and creates independent FTC §5 unsubstantiated-AI-claims exposure (Operation AI Comply, Sept 2024). The code-level banned-stem filter does nothing for the sales team.
- **FIX:** A written intended-use statement approved by counsel; a marketing-claims SOP with an allowed/banned claims list that mirrors the product's banned-language filter (ban 'detect', 'alert', 'catch early', 'predict complications', any readmission or outcome number); quarterly re-verification of the current guidance revision; and a standing regulatory-rationale memo mapping each product function to each of the four 520(o)(1)(E) criteria — the document you hand FDA if asked.

**TARGET:** State AI law analysis (FDA/state section covers only California AB 3030)

- **OBJECTION:** One state deep. Missing: (a) Utah AI Policy Act (SB 149, eff. May 2024, as amended 2025) — persons in regulated occupations, including licensed healthcare, must PROACTIVELY and prominently disclose at the start of an interaction that the consumer is interacting with generative AI; the LLM classifying free-text replies and rendering acknowledgments inside the SMS thread plausibly triggers this for the practice's Utah patients, and MedPull as vendor will wear the contractual flow-down. (b) Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205, implementation delayed to June 30, 2026 — i.e., presumptively in effect as of this month; current status must be verified) — an AI system that is a substantial factor in decisions concerning health-care services is 'high-risk', imposing developer duties on MedPull (impact assessment support, disclosures, reasonable-care against algorithmic discrimination) and deployer duties on customer practices. (c) Texas TRAIGA (eff. Jan 1, 2026) and a fast-moving 2026 landscape. 'AB 3030 compliance' is a false all-clear.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** State-AG enforcement and, worse, sales friction: hospital and MSO buyers now send AI-governance questionnaires keyed to Colorado-style obligations; having no answer loses deals even where enforcement never comes.
- **FIX:** Build a 50-state disclosure matrix before SMS go-live and comply to the strictest common denominator: proactive 'this is an automated/AI-assisted program' disclosure in the consent, the welcome message, and the recurring footer (the plan already builds this footer for the 911 disclaimer — extend it). Package the planned HTI-1-aligned model card as the core of a Colorado-style impact-assessment kit; it covers most of the content already.

**TARGET:** Conversational check-in: SMS infrastructure plan (A2P 10DLC registration treated as the compliance step)

- **OBJECTION:** Carrier registration is hygiene, not law. Automated texts require prior express consent under the TCPA (47 U.S.C. §227); healthcare-treatment messages receive favorable treatment but are not consent-exempt, and the FCC's 2024 revocation order (effective April 11, 2025) requires honoring opt-outs expressed by ANY reasonable means, propagated within 10 business days, across message types. Nothing in the plan covers consent capture at enrollment, STOP/HELP handling, revocation propagation, quiet hours, or the content firewall that keeps treatment messaging from drifting into marketing ('rate us', 'upgrade your plan'), which flips the requirement to prior express WRITTEN consent. There is also no documented OCR-expected patient acknowledgment that SMS is unencrypted.
- **CONSEQUENCE:** TCPA is strict liability at $500–$1,500 per text and is the plaintiff class-action bar's favorite statute. A 1,000-patient roster texting daily under a broken consent flow is eight-figure theoretical exposure from a single bug — dwarfing every other liability in this plan.
- **FIX:** Enrollment-time written consent naming the practice and describing message frequency; automated STOP/HELP wired and tested before the first production message; revocation synced across channels within the deadline; quiet-hours enforcement; a treatment-only content policy enforced in the template library; and SMS-risk acknowledgment language in the consent per OCR guidance.
