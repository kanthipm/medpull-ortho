# Model training strategy: train, fine-tune, or distil our own model — per-task analysis for MedPull Recovery Copilot

## Summary

Verdict: MedPull should fine-tune nothing today — not one of the six model-shaped tasks clears the bar, and two of them (risk scoring, deviation detection) should never involve an LLM at all. The evidence is consistent: prompting a strong generalist beats fine-tuned medical specialists on reasoning (MedPrompt, >90% MedQA), fine-tuning's genuine wins are format adherence (LIMA, ~1k examples) and narrow extraction (UniversalNER, distilled 7B beating its ChatGPT teacher by 7-9 F1), and fine-tuning cannot inject knowledge (Ovadia et al.) — but the format problem is already solved by the deterministic renderer with contract validation, leaving structured symptom extraction from check-in transcripts as the single future fine-tune candidate, triggered only once ~1,500 clinician-verified de-identified transcripts exist. Training cost is a non-issue ($4-15 per LoRA run on Together/Fireworks); the real blockers are that MedPull has zero training data, no BAA on Groq's free tier (a live compliance gap for PHI-bearing transcripts, independent of fine-tuning), and no serving path — Groq only serves custom models by enterprise inquiry, so a fine-tune forces a provider split to Fireworks/Together serverless LoRA (fine-tuned served at base-model per-token prices) since Bedrock Custom Model Import's scale-to-zero cold starts are unusable for interactive latency and self-hosted GPU endpoints run ~$1k+/month. Medical open models (MedGemma 27B: 89.8% MedQA, HAI-DEF license; OpenBioLLM-70B: beats GPT-4 on benchmark averages yet forbids clinical use) win medical-exam benchmarks that do not transfer to narration or extraction, and their licenses add flow-through obligations without removing any validation burden. The highest-ROI moves are a paid Groq tier, better few-shot prompts with schema-validated extraction, and an eval-and-logging flywheel that turns today's clinician edits into tomorrow's training set — so that if the extraction fine-tune trigger ever fires, the data already exists.

## Findings

### Prompting a strong generalist beats fine-tuned medical specialists on reasoning tasks (MedPrompt)
*[strong]*

Microsoft's MedPrompt (Nokes et al., arXiv 2311.16452, Nov 2023) showed GPT-4 with pure prompt engineering (dynamic few-shot + CoT + ensembling) beat the fine-tuned specialist Med-PaLM 2 across all nine MultiMedQA datasets, achieving >90% on MedQA — a 27% error-rate reduction over the best specialist models — with zero domain-specific training. Conclusion generalizes: for tasks bounded by reasoning quality (our roster NL query, conversational check-in), a better base model with better prompting beats a fine-tuned smaller model.

> https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16452

### Style/format fine-tuning needs ~1,000 examples, not tens of thousands (LIMA)
*[strong]*

LIMA (Zhou et al., arXiv 2305.11206) fine-tuned LLaMA-65B on exactly 1,000 curated prompt-response pairs and found models learn to follow specific response formats 'from only a handful of examples' — the 'superficial alignment hypothesis': fine-tuning teaches format/style, not knowledge. For our narrative and RTM-note tasks (pure format tasks), this means a LoRA run on 1-2k pairs generated by our own deterministic renderer would suffice — but it also means the ceiling of what fine-tuning buys us there is low, because our JSON-contract-validated prompting already enforces format.

> https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206

### Fine-tuning is a poor mechanism for injecting knowledge; retrieval wins (Ovadia et al.)
*[strong]*

'Fine-Tuning or Retrieval?' (Microsoft, arXiv 2312.05934) found 'LLMs struggle to learn new factual information through unsupervised fine-tuning' and RAG 'consistently outperforms it, both for existing knowledge encountered during training and entirely new knowledge.' Implication: never fine-tune to teach the model our protocols, recovery curves, or per-patient facts — keep injecting them in the prompt (our current analytics-bundle-in-context design is exactly right).

> https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934

### Targeted distillation into a small model beats its own teacher on narrow extraction (UniversalNER)
*[strong]*

UniversalNER (arXiv 2308.03279) distilled ChatGPT into 7B/13B students for NER and beat the teacher by 7-9 absolute F1 points on average across 43 datasets in 9 domains including biomedicine, with no direct supervision, and beat general instruction-tuned models (Alpaca/Vicuna) by >30 F1. This is the strongest evidence that ONE of our tasks — structured symptom extraction from check-in transcripts — is genuinely fine-tune-shaped, once we have transcripts to learn from.

> https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03279

### QLoRA makes training cost a rounding error; data curation is the real cost
*[strong]*

QLoRA (Dettmers et al., arXiv 2305.14314) fine-tunes a 65B model on a single 48GB GPU via 4-bit NF4 + LoRA; Guanaco reached 99.3% of ChatGPT's level after 24h on one GPU on a small high-quality dataset. Typical recipe for our scale: Llama-3.1-8B, LoRA rank 16-64, alpha 2x rank, target q/k/v/o + gate/up/down projections, 1-3k examples, 2-3 epochs — trainable free on Colab via Unsloth or for tens of dollars anywhere. Compute is not the bottleneck; labeled clinician-verified data is.

> https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314

### Hosted fine-tuning is nearly free at our data sizes: $4-$15 per run
*[strong]*

Together.ai (July 2026 pricing): SFT $0.48/1M training tokens for ≤16B models ($1.20 LoRA), DPO $0.54/M ($1.35 LoRA), 70B-class $2.90/M, $4.00 minimum per job. Fireworks: LoRA SFT from $0.50/1M tokens for ≤16B. A 2,000-example x ~1,500-token x 3-epoch run is ~9M tokens ≈ $4-11. Full fine-tuning via SageMaker (g5/p4d hourly) or 8xH100 rental (~$44-56/hr at $5.49-7.00/GPU-hr) is never justified at our task sizes — OpenBioLLM's own team used QLoRA even at 70B.

> https://www.together.ai/pricing ; https://fireworks.ai/pricing

### Serverless LoRA serving exists at base-model per-token prices — no GPU rental needed
*[strong]*

Fireworks explicitly: 'Serve fine-tuned models for the same price as base models' on serverless (8B-class roughly $0.10-0.20/M tokens); Together offers serverless LoRA inference similarly. AWS Bedrock Custom Model Import bills $0.05718 per Custom Model Unit per minute (~$3.43/hr; an 8B Llama needs 2 CMUs ≈ $6.86/hr active) in 5-minute windows, scales to zero after 5 idle minutes (i.e., real cold starts of tens of seconds to minutes), plus $1.95/CMU-month storage. For a Lambda-based backend, Fireworks/Together serverless LoRA is the only sane fine-tuned-hosting path; Bedrock CMI cold starts are hostile to interactive worklist latency.

> https://fireworks.ai/pricing ; https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/

### Groq free tier is the binding constraint, and Groq does not self-serve custom models
*[strong]*

Groq free tier for llama-3.3-70b-versatile: 30 RPM, 1,000 requests/day, 12,000 TPM, 100,000 tokens/day. Paid: $0.59/M input, $0.79/M output at ~394 tok/s; llama-3.1-8b-instant is $0.05/$0.08. Fine-tuned models on Groq are 'available for specific customer requests' only (enterprise inquiry, LoRA support not self-serve). So a fine-tune today would force a provider change; conversely, simply moving from free to paid Groq (or to 8B-instant for cheap tasks) removes the volume ceiling for roughly $1-2 per 1,000 briefings.

> https://groq.com/pricing ; https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits

### MedGemma is the best current medical open model, but its wins are exam wins, not our tasks
*[strong]*

MedGemma 27B-text-it (May 2025): MedQA 89.8% vs base Gemma 3 27B's 74.9%, MedMCQA 74.2% vs 62.6%. But the model card states outputs 'are not intended to directly inform clinical diagnosis, patient management decisions, treatment recommendations' and requires developer validation. Its gains are on medical-exam MCQA — knowledge our system deliberately does not rely on (all numbers come from the deterministic engine). It buys us nothing for narration, extraction, or dialogue quality.

> https://huggingface.co/google/medgemma-27b-text-it

### Medical open-model licenses carry flow-through obligations Llama/Apache models don't
*[strong]*

MedGemma is under the Health AI Developer Foundations terms: commercial use allowed, but you must obtain 'Health Regulatory Authorization' where required, pass the full terms + prohibited-use policy downstream to every recipient, and 'Model Derivatives' is defined broadly to include distillation — so even distilling FROM MedGemma binds you. OpenBioLLM-70B is under the Meta Llama 3 license with the authors 'strongly advis[ing] against ... clinical decision support'; BioMistral (arXiv 2402.10373) and Meditron (arXiv 2311.16079) are Apache/permissive but built on 2023-era 7B bases and are effectively superseded on quality. None reduces our regulatory/validation burden by a single step.

> https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/terms ; https://huggingface.co/aaditya/Llama3-OpenBioLLM-70B

### OpenBioLLM shows DPO-on-open-base can beat GPT-4 on benchmarks — and still ships a do-not-use-clinically disclaimer
*[strong]*

OpenBioLLM-70B (Llama-3-70B-Instruct + DPO + custom medical instruction data, QLoRA on 8xH100, 4 epochs) averages 86.06% across 9 biomedical datasets vs GPT-4's 82.85% and Med-PaLM-2's 84.08% — yet its own card says never use it for direct patient care. Benchmark superiority does not transfer to deployment permission; the guardrail/validation layer we already built is what actually matters, and it is model-agnostic.

> https://huggingface.co/aaditya/Llama3-OpenBioLLM-70B

### HIPAA: de-identified data is outside HIPAA entirely; training on raw PHI runs through your BAAs
*[moderate]*

Under 45 CFR 164.514, data de-identified via Safe Harbor (removal of 18 identifier categories) or Expert Determination is no longer PHI and may be used freely, including as training data. Training on identifiable patient transcripts is arguably permissible as 'health care operations' for the covered entity, but every processor in the chain needs a BAA — and Groq's free tier has no BAA, which is a compliance gap in the CURRENT pipeline (check-in transcripts sent to Groq likely contain PHI) that matters far more urgently than any fine-tuning question. Fireworks, Together (enterprise tiers), and AWS Bedrock all offer HIPAA-eligible BAAs; vendor terms generally prohibit the VENDOR training on your data, not you training your own adapter on your own de-identified data. (HHS de-identification guidance page returned 403 to automated fetch; regulation text cited from 45 CFR 164.514.)

> 45 CFR 164.514(a)-(b); https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html (fetch blocked)

### Predibase — frequently recommended for serverless LoRA — was absorbed by Rubrik; treat 2024-era Predibase advice as superseded
*[strong]*

predibase.com/pricing now 301-redirects to rubrik.com/products/rubrik-agent-cloud (Predibase was acquired by Rubrik, June 2025). Its LoRAX serverless-adapter story lives on open-source and inside Rubrik Agent Cloud, but as a self-serve startup fine-tuning vendor it is gone. Fireworks and Together are the surviving self-serve options; Bedrock and SageMaker JumpStart are the AWS-native ones.

> https://predibase.com/pricing (redirect observed 2026-07-31)

### Task 6 (deviation detection / risk scoring) is a statistics problem — agreed, and an LLM there would be a regression
*[moderate]*

EWMA/CUSUM, expected-curve residuals, and rule-based tiers with typed reason codes are auditable, deterministic, and cheap. A supervised model (gradient-boosted trees or regularized logistic regression on the engine's features) becomes worthwhile only once you have outcome labels — clinician tier overrides, escalations, ED visits/readmissions — realistically 200-500 labeled patient-episodes minimum for a calibrated binary risk model. Note the regulatory asymmetry: FDA's Clinical Decision Support guidance (Sept 2022, implementing 21st Century Cures Act §3060) keeps software non-device only if the clinician can independently review the basis of recommendations — transparent rules + reason codes satisfy this far more cleanly than a learned risk score, and 'predictive risk' on the roadmap should be scoped with that in mind.

> FDA Clinical Decision Support Software Guidance (Sept 2022); 21 USC 360j(o)

### Synthetic data from our own deterministic renderer is free distillation fuel — but circular for quality gains
*[moderate]*

We can generate unlimited (analytics-bundle → renderer-text) pairs with zero PHI, and use a frontier model to produce 'improved narrative' targets over de-identified bundles (classic teacher-student distillation into an 8B). This makes the future narrative fine-tune data problem trivially solvable. Caveat: pairs whose targets come from the deterministic renderer can only teach the student to imitate the renderer — you must generate targets with a stronger teacher model, and most frontier-model ToS (OpenAI, Anthropic) restrict using outputs to train competing models, while Llama-license and Apache teachers (Llama 3.3-70B, DeepSeek) permit it.

> Distillation precedent: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03279

### Tooling landscape if/when we do fine-tune
*[moderate]*

Self-managed: HuggingFace PEFT + TRL (SFTTrainer/DPOTrainer) is the reference stack; Axolotl for config-driven runs; Unsloth for 2x-speed QLoRA of an 8B on a single 24GB GPU (free Colab T4 works for rank-16 QLoRA). Managed: Together and Fireworks self-serve LoRA SFT/DPO with serverless serving; AWS-native: Bedrock model customization + Custom Model Import, SageMaker JumpStart fine-tuning (pay g5.2xlarge ~$1.21-1.52/hr plus endpoint hosting — an always-on ml.g5.2xlarge endpoint is ~$1,000/month, which is why serverless-LoRA vendors win for a startup).

> https://www.together.ai/pricing ; https://fireworks.ai/pricing ; https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/


## Implications for backend

- The current architecture (deterministic engine produces all numbers, LLM only verbalizes, contract-validated with silent deterministic fallback) is the correct long-term design and is model-agnostic — no backend refactor should assume a specific model or provider; keep the provider behind the existing chain abstraction so a future Fireworks/Together serverless-LoRA endpoint is a config change, not a rewrite.
- Add a training-data flywheel now even though no training is planned: persist (input-hash, analytics bundle, transcript, LLM output, validation verdict, fallback-used flag, clinician edit) tuples in SQLite/Postgres. This doubles as the eval harness and is the prerequisite for the one plausible future fine-tune (symptom extraction).
- Close the PHI/BAA gap in the Groq call path before conversational check-ins ship: either pseudonymize/strip identifiers server-side before the API call (Safe Harbor-style scrubbing of the 18 identifier categories, 45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)) or move to a BAA-covered provider tier; this constraint shapes the check-in transcript schema (separate identity fields from free text).
- Plan the conversational check-in as a deterministic question state machine (stage-aware branching owned by backend code) with the LLM doing paraphrase and slot-filling only — this keeps the fine-tuning question moot, keeps FDA non-device CDS posture, and means the extraction layer (task 2) can be swapped from prompted-JSON-schema to a distilled 8B later without touching dialogue logic.
- For future risk scoring, keep engine features (EWMA/CUSUM stats, curve residuals, adherence, coverage confidence) exported in a tabular, versioned form — a scikit-learn GBT/logistic model over those features runs inside Lambda with no GPU and preserves the typed-reason-code contract; design the tier module so a learned score can shadow the rule-based tier before ever replacing it.
- Groq free-tier limits (12k TPM / 100k tokens-day on 70B, 1k requests/day) are the nearest scaling cliff: budget for paid tier and consider routing short/cheap artifacts (worklist reason lines) to llama-3.1-8b-instant ($0.05/$0.08 per M) while keeping 70B for briefings and summaries.

## Recommendation
**Train nothing today. Do not fine-tune, do not distil, do not adopt a medical-domain open model, and definitely do not train from scratch. Every current task is either a format task your deterministic renderer already backstops (1, 4), a reasoning/tool-use task where a stronger prompted base model wins (3, 5), a statistics task where an LLM is inappropriate (6), or an extraction task (2) that is the ONE genuine future fine-tune candidate — and only after you have 1-3k real, clinician-verified, de-identified check-in transcripts. Per-task verdicts: (1) Narrative from analytics bundle — prompt Groq 70B few-shot forever; fine-tune trigger: never for quality, only if unit cost at >5M narratives/month makes an 8B LoRA on Fireworks (~$10 training, base-price serving) worth the vendor split. (2) Symptom extraction — prompt with JSON-schema validation now; trigger: ≥1,500 verified transcripts AND measured extraction F1 that prompting can't close; then distil (UniversalNER-style) into Llama-3.1-8B LoRA, ~$5-15 training + ~$5-20k data-curation labor, served serverless on Fireworks under BAA. (3) Adaptive conversational check-in — strongest available base model + deterministic question-state-machine scaffold; fine-tuning a small model for open medical dialogue is a safety and FDA-scope regression; trigger: effectively never at your scale. (4) RTM notes/monthly summaries — deterministic templates first (billing documents reward auditability), LLM polish via prompting; trigger: same as (1). (5) Roster NL query — function-calling/semantic-parsing on a strong base; MedPrompt evidence says prompting generalists beats tuned specialists; trigger: never. (6) Deviation/risk — you are right that it is statistics; add a small supervised tabular model (GBT/logistic, scikit-learn, runs fine in Lambda) only once ~200-500 outcome-labeled episodes exist, and keep it reason-code-transparent to stay inside FDA's non-device CDS criteria.**

Three evidence pillars: (a) prompting strong generalists beats fine-tuned specialists on medical reasoning (MedPrompt: GPT-4 prompted >90% MedQA, 27% error reduction over Med-PaLM 2); (b) fine-tuning's real wins are format adherence (LIMA: 1k examples) and narrow extraction (UniversalNER: distilled 7B beats its ChatGPT teacher by 7-9 F1) — but your format problem is already solved deterministically with contract validation and silent fallback, leaving extraction as the only task where fine-tuning's comparative advantage applies; and (c) fine-tuning cannot inject knowledge (Ovadia et al.), so it can never replace the analytics-bundle-in-context architecture, which is your actual moat. Economically, training is trivial ($4-15/run hosted) but you have zero training data today, no BAA on your current free-tier provider, and serving a custom model would force you off Groq (custom models are enterprise-inquiry only). Medical open models (MedGemma 89.8% MedQA) win exams, not your tasks, and HAI-DEF license flow-through obligations plus universal 'not for clinical use' disclaimers mean they reduce none of your validation burden. The cheapest capability upgrades available are a paid Groq tier (~$0.59/$0.79 per M tokens) and better few-shot prompts — orders of magnitude less effort than any training pipeline.

**Do NOT:**
- Do not train from scratch or full-parameter fine-tune anything — at your task sizes QLoRA/LoRA dominates and even 70B medical leaders (OpenBioLLM) trained with QLoRA.
- Do not adopt MedGemma/Meditron/OpenBioLLM/BioMistral as your generation model — MCQA benchmark wins don't transfer to narration/extraction, HAI-DEF terms add downstream flow-through and health-regulatory-authorization obligations, and every card forbids or disclaims clinical use anyway.
- Do not fine-tune for the conversational check-in — open-ended medical dialogue is where small tuned models fail unsafely; use the strongest base model behind a deterministic question state machine.
- Do not put an LLM anywhere in deviation detection or risk scoring; do not replace typed reason codes with an opaque learned score (jeopardizes FDA non-device CDS status).
- Do not fine-tune on raw patient transcripts — de-identify per 45 CFR 164.514 Safe Harbor first; and do not keep sending PHI-bearing transcripts to Groq's free tier without a BAA regardless of the fine-tuning decision.
- Do not build on 2024-era Predibase advice (acquired by Rubrik, June 2025) or plan on Bedrock Custom Model Import for interactive latency (scale-to-zero cold starts).

**Sequencing:**
- Now (days): Close the compliance gap — either strip/pseudonymize PHI before Groq calls or move to a BAA-covered provider tier; this is prerequisite to ever using this data for anything, training included.
- Now (1-2 weeks): Build the eval harness + data flywheel: log every (analytics bundle, transcript, LLM output, validation result, clinician edit/override) pair. This is simultaneously your quality dashboard and your only path to future fine-tuning data. Zero training work is defensible without it.
- Next (1-2 weeks): Invest in prompting, not weights — few-shot exemplars per artifact type, JSON-schema (function-calling) extraction for check-ins, upgraded roster-query tool use. Budget: move to Groq paid tier (~tens of dollars/month at current volume) and/or route cheap tasks to llama-3.1-8b-instant at $0.05/$0.08 per M.
- Later (when ≥1,500 clinician-verified de-identified transcripts exist AND prompted extraction F1 plateaus below target): run the one justified fine-tune — distil symptom extraction into Llama-3.1-8B via LoRA (r=16-32, alpha 32, q/k/v/o+MLP targets) on Together or Fireworks (~$5-15 compute, ~1-2 weeks eng effort), serve serverless-LoRA at base-model prices under BAA; keep Groq for generation tasks.
- Later (when 200-500 outcome-labeled episodes exist): train the supervised tabular risk model (GBT/logistic on engine features) as a shadow-mode complement to rule-based tiers, ~1 week effort, negligible cost, Lambda-hostable; promote only with calibration evidence and preserved reason codes.
- Only if narrative volume exceeds ~5M outputs/month or Groq economics break: LoRA-distil the narrative task into an 8B using frontier-teacher targets over de-identified bundles (license-check the teacher), ~$10-50 training, serverless serving.

## Open questions

- Will Groq offer a self-serve BAA/HIPAA tier at developer scale, or does PHI-bearing conversational check-in traffic force a provider move regardless of the fine-tuning question? (Groq lists enterprise custom-model serving by inquiry only; BAA availability at developer tier unconfirmed.)
- Does the practice's patient consent / notice-of-privacy-practices language cover secondary use of de-identified check-in data for model improvement, and who signs off on Expert Determination vs Safe Harbor if free-text transcripts prove hard to scrub reliably?
- What outcome labels can the practice realistically capture for the future risk model (clinician tier overrides are cheap; ED visits/readmissions require EHR integration that doesn't exist yet) — and at what volume per quarter, which sets the realistic date for the 200-500-episode trigger?
- Teacher-model licensing for the eventual extraction distillation: confirm current Llama 3.x / DeepSeek license terms permit using outputs as training targets at the time of the run, since frontier-lab ToS (OpenAI/Anthropic) restrict training competing models on outputs.
