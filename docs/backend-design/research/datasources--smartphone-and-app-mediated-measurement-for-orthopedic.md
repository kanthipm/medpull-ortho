# Smartphone and app-mediated measurement for orthopedic recovery: camera/IMU range of motion, HEP verification, guided self-administered performance tests, phone gait, wound photography, and passive digital phenotyping

## Summary

The phone can measure four things the wrist wearable structurally cannot: joint angle (ROM), guided performance-test scores (TUG, 30s chair stand, 6MWT), spatiotemporal gait quality (step length, double support, asymmetry), and wound appearance. Accuracy is uneven and modality-specific. The strongest evidence is for phone-as-inclinometer knee ROM self-measured by TKA patients themselves — mean absolute difference 4.5 deg flexion / 2.2 deg extension vs clinician goniometry, ICC 0.97-0.98, in a cohort whose median age was 66, with 100 percent task completion and 87 percent rating it easy. That is essentially our exact patient. Camera pose estimation is good for the knee and hip in the sagittal plane (OpenPose vs radiography after TKA: ICC 0.953 flexion, 2.3 deg fixed error in extension, actually beating goniometry's 4.1 deg underestimate) but degrades badly for shoulder rotation, elbow and ankle (MoveNet Thunder RMSE 5.3-9.3 deg shoulder but 25-27 deg elbow flexion; PoseNet 67-76 deg shoulder abduction — unusable). Guided tests are the best effort-to-signal trade: pocket-phone TUG r=0.99 vs a certified reference sensor with test-retest ICC 0.79, and waist-phone chair-stand cycle segmentation at 99 percent detection accuracy. GPS 6MWT fails a 100 m equivalence bound (LOA -77.6 to +103.9 m) and should be treated as a trend, not a clinical distance. Apple's Mobility metrics are free and passive but only walking speed and step length are trustworthy in seniors (ICC 0.85/0.76, percentage error 14.1/13.1); double support is not (ICC 0.42, PE 31.6 percent) — although its one-week test-retest is excellent (ICC 0.88-0.93), which makes it a good within-patient trend series and a bad absolute number. Wound photography is the highest-value, highest-risk item: photo-only telemedicine SSI detection pools at only 63.9 percent sensitivity, and the best single AI app (RedScar, NCT05485233) hit 100 percent sensitivity / 83.1 percent specificity on just 5 infections in 41 patients, with 42.9 percent of patients hitting upload problems. Build image capture and triage-to-human; do not build an autonomous SSI classifier. Passive digital phenotyping via Beiwe-style GPS is real but weak-signal (r about -0.36 vs pain VAS) and carries the heaviest privacy cost.

## Findings

### Phone-as-inclinometer knee ROM, self-measured by TKA patients, is the single best-validated smartphone measure for our exact population
*[strong]*

Prospective study of 30 primary unilateral TKA patients, median age 66.0 y (IQR 60.3-70.8), 60% female, median 8 weeks post-op (6.0-12.8). Custom accelerometer-based Android app on a Samsung Galaxy S10, phone strapped to the limb segment. Versus clinician universal goniometry: knee flexion mean absolute difference 4.5 deg (ICC 0.97), knee extension MAD 2.2 deg (ICC 0.98). Intra-rater ICC 0.98 flexion / 0.97 extension; inter-session ICC 0.97 / 0.98. 100% of patients completed the self-assessment unaided; 87% (26/30) scored it >=8/10 for ease of use, zero scored <=4. Known ceiling: cannot resolve >145 deg flexion or true 0 deg extension because the phone cannot be placed. A separate patient-performed smartphone goniometry study reports intra- and inter-session ICC >0.97, MAD 0.9-3.9 deg, MDC95 3.1-9.0 deg — so a real change must exceed roughly 5-9 deg to be believed.

> Feasibility, reliability and validity of self-measurement of knee range-of-motion using an accelerometer-based smartphone application by patients with total knee arthroplasty, 2024 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11449355/

### Camera pose estimation for knee ROM after TKA outperforms goniometry against radiographic truth, but flexion limits of agreement are wide
*[strong]*

OpenPose v1.7.0 (GPU release), 35 knees / 24 patients, mean age 73.0 y, 68.6% female. Test-retest ICC(1,1) = 1.000 (deterministic on a fixed video). Agreement with radiography: ICC(2,1) 0.953 flexion, 0.815 extension. Extension systematic error 2.3 deg for OpenPose vs 4.1 deg underestimation for manual goniometry — the algorithm was the more accurate of the two. But limits of agreement: 16.4 deg width in flexion (-7.5 to +8.9 deg) and 7.8 deg in extension (-1.6 to +6.1 deg). Interpretation for us: excellent for extension/flexion-contracture surveillance (the clinically decisive TKA metric), marginal for tracking small flexion gains, and OpenPose itself needs a GPU so it is a server-side or offline-processing model, not on-device.

> Reliability and validity of pose estimation algorithm for measurement of knee range of motion after total knee arthroplasty, Bone & Joint Research 12(5), 2023 — https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/2046-3758.125.BJR-2022-0257.R1

### On-device mobile pose models are joint-dependent: acceptable at the shoulder in the frontal/sagittal plane, unusable at the elbow and ankle
*[strong]*

52 participants (31 women), age 22.9 +/- 6.33 y, 10 upper-limb movements, NLMeasurer Android app, 1440x720 capture at 2.5 m standoff, Xiaomi Redmi Note 10S. RMSE by model: MoveNet Thunder FP16 — shoulder flexion 8.99-9.28 deg, shoulder extension 5.33-7.77 deg, shoulder abduction 7.94-8.48 deg, elbow extension 6.66-8.74 deg, but elbow flexion 25.27-27.39 deg. MoveNet Thunder INT8 — shoulder 5.63-11.24 deg, elbow flexion 25.44-25.78 deg. PoseNet (MobileNet) shoulder abduction 67.01-75.84 deg — catastrophic. Authors' verdict: accuracy is 'unsatisfactory' and 'the examiner's visual inspection remains indispensable', requiring manual keypoint correction before clinical use. A separate narrative review reports MoveNet Thunder RMSE <10 deg in 8 of 10 movements and MediaPipe mean Pearson r 0.80 +/- 0.1 vs motion capture for lower-limb movements. Direct implication: rotator-cuff internal/external rotation — the movement that actually matters clinically for RCR — is the axial-rotation case monocular 2D pose is worst at.

> Validity Analysis of Monocular Human Pose Estimation Models Interfaced with a Mobile Application for Assessing Upper Limb Range of Motion, Sensors 24(24):7983, 2024 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11679233/

### MediaPipe Pose Landmarker is the practical build option: 33 landmarks, 3D world coordinates, Apache 2.0, runs on Android/iOS/web/Python
*[strong]*

BlazePose-derived, MobileNetV2-like backbone with GHUM 3D body model. Outputs 33 landmarks in both normalized image coordinates and metric 3D world coordinates (origin at hip midpoint). Three variants (Lite / Full / Heavy), all float16, detector input 224x224x3, landmarker input 256x256x3. Platforms: Android, iOS, Web/JS, Python. Code samples Apache 2.0; docs CC-BY-4.0 — no license barrier and no per-call cost. Google does not publish mAP/PCK on the current Pose Landmarker page, so the accuracy evidence has to come from the third-party validation literature above. Apple's alternative is the Vision framework (VNDetectHumanBodyPoseRequest, 19 joints 2D, iOS 14+; VNHumanBodyPose3DObservation adds 3D on iOS 17+) which is free and on-device but iOS-only and fewer landmarks.

> Google MediaPipe Pose Landmarker documentation, accessed 2026 — https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker

### A commercial vision-AI ROM app (ViFive) is validated but only weakly for the hip and ankle, and in a young cohort
*[moderate]*

31 healthy adults and sports-medicine patients, mean age 30.7 +/- 12.0 y — not our population. Simultaneous universal goniometer as gold standard. Mixed-effects beta coefficients (slope of app vs goniometer): shoulder abduction 0.96, shoulder flexion 0.93, shoulder external rotation 0.78, knee flexion 0.78, hip flexion 0.62, ankle dorsiflexion 0.36. Intra-rater reliability good-to-excellent for most joints, worst at the ankle. Note the pattern: the two joints we most need for THA (hip) and ankle procedures are the two with the weakest slopes, i.e. the app compresses the true range.

> Meng et al., Evaluation of range of motion using vision artificial intelligence (AI) in musculoskeletal medicine, Journal of Medical Artificial Intelligence, 2024 — https://jmai.amegroups.org/article/view/9186/html

### Exer AI is the most licensable off-the-shelf US option: camera-only, FDA-registered Class II software, with a module set that maps almost 1:1 onto our needs
*[moderate]*

Nine modules: Fall Risk, Balance, Knee Health, Shoulder Health, Hip Health, multi-joint ROM, Posture (frontal/sagittal/transverse), Sit-to-Stand, and Gait Analysis (beta). Human pose estimation over 24 keypoints from a phone or tablet camera; explicitly no wearable or additional hardware. Self-described as FDA Registered Class II medical device software and HIPAA compliant, and it markets RTM billing support. Critical gap: the only accuracy evidence on their site is a single named-provider testimonial ('validated Exer in our clinic, comparing to traditional goniometry... impressively accurate') with no degrees, no ICC, no n, and no peer-reviewed citation. Treat their accuracy as unverified until they produce a study. Exer Scan (clinician-facing) and Exer Health (patient app) are separate SKUs; no public price list.

> Exer AI product/modules and solutions pages, accessed 2026 — https://www.exer.ai/product/modules and https://www.exer.ai/solutions/orthopedics

### Hinge Health and Sword Health both run hybrid sensor+vision stacks and neither publishes measurement-accuracy numbers — they publish outcomes, not validity
*[moderate]*

Hinge Health markets 'TrueMotion' motion tracking that 'tracks over 100 reference points across the body for real-time feedback', plus a proprietary HingeScore progress metric, and separately Enso (a wearable electrical nerve stimulation device for pain, NOT a motion sensor). Sword Health holds granted patents including 'Computer Vision-Driven Interactive Full-Body Motion Tracking' and a patent on motion-tracking adjustment using differences in sensor orientation, and its portfolio explicitly spans 'motion tracking technologies that combine wearable sensors and computer vision'. Neither company's public material contains a single degree-of-error, rep-count-accuracy, or ICC figure. Strategic read: these are competitors in the RTM/MSK space with large clinical-outcomes literatures but no published measurement-validity literature — meaning the measurement bar we must clear to be credible is set by the academic studies above, not by them, and there is no licensable measurement SDK from either.

> Hinge Health Precision Motion Technology page and Sword Health patent newsroom release, accessed 2026 — https://www.hingehealth.com/product/precision-motion-technology/ and https://swordhealth.com/newsroom/three-new-ai-care-patents

### Automated rep counting and form assessment from pose has almost no peer-reviewed accuracy literature — it is a build-and-validate-yourself area
*[weak]*

Targeted PubMed retrieval for pose-estimation repetition counting in rehabilitation returns essentially two relevant papers: Hsu YC, Efstratios T, Tsui KL, 'Viewpoint-invariant exercise repetition counting', Health Information Science and Systems, 2023 (PMID 38045021), using pose analysis plus spectrogram-based periodicity detection; and Ullah R, Asghar I, Nawaz R et al., 'Real time action scoring system for movement analysis', Scientific Reports, 2025 (PMID 41461803), which benchmarks against RepNet, the standard video repetition-counting baseline. Neither abstract discloses a repetition-counting accuracy percentage. Practical consequence: rep counting via signal periodicity on a joint-angle time series (peak detection with amplitude-adaptive thresholds, the same technique the chair-stand work uses at 99% accuracy) is a solved-enough engineering problem; automated FORM grading is not, and asserting form quality to a clinician without published validity is a liability we should not take on in v1.

> PubMed retrieval, pose estimation exercise repetition counting rehabilitation, accessed 2026 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38045021/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41461803/

### Smartphone-based Timed Up and Go self-assessment is near-perfectly valid concurrently but only moderately reproducible across days
*[strong]*

'up&go app', 52 community-dwelling older adults aged >67 y, phone in the FRONT TROUSER POCKET, reference = a medically certified lower-back belt-mounted inertial system. Concurrent validity r = 0.99 for total test duration and r = 0.97 for the 5 individual repetitions. Between-repetition agreement ICC 0.90 (0.84-0.94), rising to ICC 0.95 (0.92-0.97) when the first repetition is discarded — so always throw away rep 1 as a familiarization trial. Two-week test-retest ICC 0.79 (0.53-0.90) in a 37-participant subsample: this is the real constraint, day-to-day noise, not sensor error. Protocol uses 5 TUG repetitions rather than 1, which is what buys the reliability.

> A Smartphone-Based Timed Up and Go Test Self-Assessment for Older Adults: Validity and Reliability Study, JMIR Aging 2025;8:e67322 (PMID 40116726) — https://aging.jmir.org/2025/1/e67322

### 30-second chair stand can be segmented automatically from a waist-mounted phone at 99% cycle-detection accuracy with sub-40 ms timing error
*[strong]*

35 adults (10 younger, 20 older, 5 with Parkinson's disease) performed supervised 30s chair sit-to-stand trials with a waist-mounted smartphone logging accelerometer and gyroscope at 400 Hz. Amplitude-adaptive thresholds plus dominant-frequency intervals segmented 660 CST cycles at 99% accuracy, with mean absolute error under 40 ms per event averaged across participants; the method also classifies rise strategy. An earlier unsupervised home system for the same test reported automatic vs manual counts correlating at Pearson r = 1.0 (p=0.00) in adults of mean age 83.29 y, demonstrating that unsupervised self-administration works in a much older cohort than ours. Beyond a raw count, per-cycle timing lets us derive sit-to-stand power/velocity, which is a stronger lower-limb strength proxy than the count alone.

> Cycle Metrics and Strategy Detection for Automated Chair Sit-to-Stand Test Analysis Employing a Single Smartphone, Annals of Biomedical Engineering, 2025 — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10439-025-03943-4 ; and Automatic and Real-Time Computation of the 30-Seconds Chair-Stand Test without Professional Supervision, Sensors 20(20):5813, 2020 — https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/20/5813

### GPS-based 6-minute walk test FAILS a prespecified clinical equivalence bound and must not be reported as a 6MWD
*[strong]*

'Timed Walk' app, 35 participants with persistent pain (>9 months), mean age 49 y (SD 13), 83% female. GPS distance ran 13.2 m higher than the conventional shuttle 6MWT (SD 46; 95% CI -2.7 to +29.1). 95% limits of agreement +103.9 m (CI 87.4-134.1) and -77.6 m (CI -107.7 to -61.0), breaching the prespecified maximum allowable difference of 100 m. ICC 0.94 for both agreement and consistency. Authors: 'may not be interchangeable due to limited validity' but 'may still have clinical application as a complementary tool'. Separately, phone-carriage position dominates error in home 6MWT: an arm strap with Google Fit processed step data gave the lowest MAE, while app vs accelerometer step counts differed by -58.7 steps (-8.7%). Also note the MCID for 6MWD in TKA is well under 100 m, so the LOA here swamps the effect we would be trying to detect.

> Assessing a GPS-Based 6-Minute Walk Test for People With Persistent Pain: Validation Study, JMIR 2024 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10985605/ ; A Mobile Application to Perform the 6MWT at Home, Sensors 22(11):4277, 2022 — https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/11/4277

### Apple Mobility metrics: use walking speed and step length in seniors, do NOT use double support time as an absolute value
*[strong]*

Independent validation, iPhone SE (64 GB, iOS 15.4) in the right front pants pocket, 6MWT, versus APDM Mobility Lab. SENIORS (n=28-29): gait speed ICC 0.85, bias +0.06 m/s, percentage error 14.1%; step length ICC 0.76, bias +3.4 cm, PE 13.1%; double support time ICC 0.42, bias -0.6%, PE 31.6%. ADULTS (n=27-28): gait speed ICC 0.86, bias 0.04 m/s, PE 11.6%; step length ICC 0.78, bias 2.9 cm, PE 9.8%; double support ICC 0.58, PE 18.4%. One-week test-retest in seniors is much better than concurrent validity: gait speed ICC 0.88 (SEM 0.07 m/s), step length ICC 0.93 (SEM 2.2 cm), double support ICC 0.91 (SEM 0.7%). That dissociation is the key engineering fact — these metrics are biased but stable, so they are valid as a within-patient trend series with a per-patient baseline and invalid as a cross-patient absolute.

> Validity and reliability of the Apple Health app on iPhone for measuring gait parameters in children, adults, and seniors, Scientific Reports 13, 2023 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10067003/

### Apple's own Mobility validation gives the exact MDC thresholds we should hard-code as change-detection gates
*[strong]*

Apple white paper 'Measuring Walking Quality Through iPhone Mobility Metrics' (May 2022). Metrics require iPhone 8 or later and iOS 14+: walking speed, step length, double support time, walking asymmetry, plus pedometer steps. Design cohort A n=359 and validation cohort A n=179, mean age 74.7 (+/-5.4), range 64-95, 80-81% with musculoskeletal conditions, 69-72% cardiovascular — an unusually well-matched population to ours. Reference standard was a ProtoKinetics Zeno 8 m instrumented pressure walkway over a 12 m course. Reported standard deviation of absolute error: walking speed 0.09-0.15 m/s, step length 0.05 m, walking asymmetry 2.91-2.95%. Minimal detectable change at the most-sensitive 10th percentile: walking speed 0.07-0.08 m/s, step length 0.04 m, asymmetry 2.06-2.12%. Cohort B simulated unilateral pathology with a knee brace locked to 30 deg flexion / 10 deg extension — i.e. Apple explicitly tuned walking asymmetry on a post-op-knee-like gait, which is directly our use case. Phone must be at the hip: hip clip, front/back pocket, or waist bag.

> Apple, Measuring Walking Quality Through iPhone Mobility Metrics, May 2022 — https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Measuring_Walking_Quality_Through_iPhone_Mobility_Metrics.pdf

### A monocular smartphone camera can recover gait speed and stance-phase knee flexion with near-lab accuracy — but only validated in 20 healthy adults
*[moderate]*

Vision-based smartphone gait app from Hong Kong Polytechnic University, 20 healthy adults aged 23-68 (mean 35.5 +/- 12.8), BMI 21.9 +/- 3.3. Gait speed vs retroreflective E3JK-DS30M1 timing gates after algorithm adjustment: r = 0.975, mean difference -0.016 +/- 0.052 m/s, LOA -0.119 to +0.087 m/s, ICC(2,1) 0.94. Peak stance knee flexion vs Xsens MVN Awinda IMU motion capture: right r = 0.923, difference 0.490 +/- 1.353 deg, LOA -2.161 to +3.141 deg, ICC 0.86; left r = 0.907, difference 0.560 +/- 1.248 deg, LOA -1.886 to +3.006 deg, ICC 0.88. This is roughly 3x tighter on gait speed than Apple's passive metrics, because it is a structured, camera-in-a-fixed-position walk. The catch: healthy young-to-middle-aged subjects, no post-surgical gait, tiny n, and it requires a second person or a tripod to hold the phone.

> Validity and Reliability of Gait Speed and Knee Flexion Estimated by a Novel Vision-Based Smartphone Application, Sensors, 2024 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11644766/

### Wound photography alone has poor sensitivity for SSI — the pooled photo-only estimate is 63.9%, versus 87.8% for full telemedicine assessment
*[strong]*

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies: telemedicine overall for SSI detection gave summary sensitivity 87.8% (95% CI 68.4-96.1) and specificity 96.8% (95% CI 93.5-98.4). The photograph-only subgroup collapsed to sensitivity 63.9% (95% CI 30.4-87.8) with specificity 92.6% (95% CI 89.9-94.5). The gap between 87.8% and 63.9% is the symptom questionnaire and the live clinician interaction — i.e. the image is a weak signal on its own and only becomes useful when fused with patient-reported symptoms. Missing roughly 1 in 3 SSIs is not a tolerable failure mode for a product that patients may treat as reassurance, which makes the false-negative UX (never say 'looks fine') the central design constraint.

> Diagnostic accuracy of telemedicine for detection of surgical site infection: a systematic review and meta-analysis, npj Digital Medicine / BJS, 2022 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9349203/

### Best published wound-photo AI (RedScar) reports 100% sensitivity / 83.1% specificity, but on 5 infections, Android-only, with 42.9% of patients hitting upload failures
*[moderate]*

RedScar, University of the Balearic Islands, Spain. Prospective paired-cohort, single-blind, patients as own controls, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05485233. 47 enrolled, 41 completed both remote and in-person follow-up; median age 59. Photos uploaded on post-op days 3 and 10. Sensitivity 100% (all 5 SSIs, an infection rate of 12.19%), specificity 83.13%, Cohen's kappa 0.8171 vs human observers. Method is deliberately simple: patient questionnaire responses fused with gray-scale mathematical-morphology fuzzy-set redness analysis, optimal cutoff 0.63 proportion of red pixels. Requires Android 10+ and >=500x500 px camera; iOS not yet built. 42.85% of patients had technical trouble uploading. No CE mark and no FDA clearance. Comparators: a Vision Transformer approach reached only accuracy 0.73 / AUC 0.81, and a separate AI wound app hit 89% sensitivity against an 85% target. The REDSCAR multicenter trial protocol (2025) is the confirmatory study, not yet reported.

> Enhancing Surgical Wound Monitoring: A Paired Cohort Study Evaluating a New AI-Based Application for Automatic Detection of Potential Infections, J Clin Med, 2025 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11727995/ ; REDSCAR-Trial protocol, 2025 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11989369/

### Passive GPS digital phenotyping tracks post-op recovery in a ~67-year-old surgical cohort with zero dropout, but correlations with outcomes are only moderate
*[moderate]*

Beiwe (Onnela Lab, Harvard; open-source, iOS and Android, AWS backend) in 13 patients after surgery for cervical myelopathy, mean age 67 y (10M/3F). Five daily features: Distance Traveled, Home Duration, Significant Location Count (distinct pauses >=15 min and >=50 m apart), Significant Location Entropy, and accelerometer Steps. Week 1 to week 5 changes, all p<0.001: distance traveled 3 km -> 14 km, steps 109 -> 250, home duration 15 h -> 14 h, significant location count 1.33 -> 2.44. VAS, mJOA and ODI all correlated significantly with all five GPS variables; NDI correlated only with distance traveled and steps. Correlation magnitude was moderate at best, r approximately -0.36 between VAS pain and mobility. 4,545 observations per variable after imputation, 460 daily VAS surveys, zero dropouts. Read: excellent adherence in an age-matched surgical population, genuine week-over-week signal, but effect sizes too small to gate a clinical alert on alone — this is a covariate, not a trigger.

> Smartphone-based digital phenotyping for characterizing post-operative recovery in patients undergoing surgery for cervical myelopathy, Frontiers in Neurology, 2026 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2026.1694719/full


## Implications for backend

- Add a third observation class alongside the existing wearable Observation store: ASSESSMENT, distinct from passive vitals. Schema needs assessment_type (KNEE_ROM_FLEX, KNEE_ROM_EXT, HIP_ROM, SHOULDER_ROM_ABD, TUG_5REP, CST_30S, SIT_TO_STAND_POWER, SLS_BALANCE, GAIT_SPEED_GUIDED, 6MWT_GPS), value + unit, capture_modality (PHONE_IMU | PHONE_CAMERA_POSE | GPS | APPLE_MOBILITY | MANUAL), pose_model_version and app_version (accuracy is model-version-dependent — a MoveNet Lightning result and a MoveNet Thunder result are NOT interchangeable and must not land in the same series), device_model, phone_placement (POCKET_FRONT | WAIST | LIMB_STRAP | TRIPOD), rep_index, and a per-observation quality score. Reuse the existing dedupe-key idempotent upsert.
- Encode published MDC/LOA as per-metric change-detection gates in the analytics layer rather than letting the EWMA chart fire on sensor noise. Concrete floors from the literature: knee ROM self-measured MDC95 3.1-9.0 deg (require >=5 deg flexion change, >=3 deg extension change); Apple walking speed MDC 0.07-0.08 m/s and SEM 0.07 m/s in seniors; Apple step length MDC 0.04 m; walking asymmetry MDC ~2.1 percentage points; GPS 6MWD LOA +/-100 m so require a >=100 m change or do not surface it at all. This is a direct extension of the coverage/confidence gate that already exists.
- Model assessment metrics as within-patient trends against a per-patient baseline, never as cross-patient absolutes, because the validation data show bias-but-stable behavior: Apple gait speed carries +0.06 m/s bias and 14.1% percentage error in seniors yet 0.88 one-week test-retest ICC; ViFive compresses hip flexion (beta 0.62) and ankle (0.36). Store a raw_value and a baseline_delta, and have risk tiers consume only the delta. The existing per-patient pre-op baseline machinery is the right home for this.
- Gate every derived assessment on capture-quality preconditions and emit typed reason codes when they fail, mirroring the existing typed reason-code pattern: pose confidence below threshold on the specific landmarks used for the angle, subject out of frame, camera standoff outside ~2-3 m, insufficient lighting, phone placement mismatch (walking-speed data with the phone in a handbag is invalid), and fewer than the required repetitions. For TUG specifically, capture 5 reps and DISCARD rep 1 server-side before computing the score — that single rule moves agreement from ICC 0.90 to 0.95.
- Do not build an autonomous SSI classifier. Build a structured wound-photo capture flow (fixed distance, ruler/fiducial or ARKit scale, consistent white-balance, day 3 and day 10 prompts matching the RedScar protocol) that fuses the image with the symptom questionnaire we already collect, and route to a human. Photo-only sensitivity of 63.9% means the product must never emit a reassuring 'looks normal'; the only safe outputs are ESCALATE and NEEDS_CLINICIAN_REVIEW. Any redness/erythema feature we compute (RedScar's red-pixel-proportion at a 0.63 cutoff is a reproducible, non-black-box starting point) should be an input to the existing weighted composite deviation index, not a standalone verdict. An autonomous classifier would almost certainly be a regulated SaMD device rather than exempt clinical decision support.
- Camera pose runs on-device via MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (Apache 2.0, 33 landmarks with 3D world coordinates, Android/iOS/web) so no per-inference cost and no PHI video leaving the phone — upload only the derived joint-angle time series and a low-rate keypoint trace, never the raw video. That keeps the Lambda + SQLite/CloudFront footprint essentially unchanged and sidesteps the video-storage and BAA blast radius entirely. Budget a keypoint payload on the order of 33 landmarks x 30 fps x 30 s, which should be downsampled to peak-angle summaries plus a decimated trace before upload.
- Rep counting should be implemented as deterministic signal processing on the joint-angle series (amplitude-adaptive thresholds plus dominant-frequency segmentation — the technique that yields 99% cycle detection and <40 ms timing error for chair stands), not as an ML form-grader. This fits the project's cloud-first-LLM/deterministic-logic posture: the LLM narrates, the deterministic layer counts. Ship rep counts and cycle timing; withhold any 'your form was incorrect' assertion until we have our own validation.
- Two ingest paths, not one: (a) Apple HealthKit for the passive Mobility metrics — HKQuantityTypeIdentifierWalkingSpeed, WalkingStepLength, WalkingDoubleSupportPercentage, WalkingAsymmetryPercentage, AppleWalkingSteadiness — which requires iPhone 8+/iOS 14+ and the phone carried at the hip, and which the existing HealthKit stub already anticipates; and (b) an app-native assessment submission endpoint for the guided tests. Flag double-support-derived features as low-confidence in seniors (ICC 0.42, PE 31.6%) in the capability map, alongside the per-provider metric capability map that already exists.
- Passive digital-phenotyping features (radius of gyration, home duration, significant location count/entropy) belong behind a separate, explicitly revocable consent scope and a separate table, and should feed the risk model only as covariates with low weight given r ~ -0.36 effect sizes. Storing continuous GPS traces materially changes our privacy and breach posture; store only the derived daily aggregates server-side and compute the aggregation on-device if at all possible.

## Open questions

- Does the accelerometer knee-ROM result (MAD 4.5 deg flexion, 2.2 deg extension, ICC 0.97-0.98) replicate across iPhone and non-Samsung Android IMUs, and how much does phone strap placement variance on a swollen post-op knee degrade it? The published study used a single phone model.
- Is there any published camera-based validation of hip ROM and shoulder internal/external rotation in a POST-SURGICAL population? Everything found for hip (ViFive beta 0.62) and shoulder rotation (beta 0.78, MoveNet RMSE 5-11 deg) is in healthy young adults, and axial rotation is the known weak point of monocular 2D pose. THA and rotator cuff repair are two of our seven procedures.
- What does Exer AI actually charge, what is the licensing model (per-patient-per-month vs per-assessment vs enterprise), and will they produce the peer-reviewed validation behind their FDA Class II registration? 'FDA registered' for a Class II device is a materially weaker claim than 510(k) cleared and needs to be pinned down before we cite it to a clinician.
- Where exactly does the FDA line fall for our specific outputs? Reporting a measured knee angle is likely a measurement function; asserting 'possible surgical site infection' from a photograph is likely SaMD. Need the current FDA Clinical Decision Support Software guidance and the 21st Century Cures Act section 520(o) criteria read against each planned output, plus whether the transparency/basis-disclosure criterion is satisfiable for a CNN-derived output.
- What is real-world adherence for GUIDED tests in a 66-year-old post-op cohort? The Beiwe cervical-myelopathy study (mean age 67) had zero dropout for PASSIVE collection, and the TKA ROM study had 100% completion for a single SUPERVISED in-clinic self-measurement, but neither establishes weekly unsupervised at-home completion rates over a 12-week recovery. This is the single biggest product risk.
- Does a guided test require a second person? TUG, 30s chair stand and single-leg stance from a pocketed/waist phone are self-administrable; camera ROM at a 2.5 m standoff needs a tripod or a helper. Quantify how many of our patients live alone and whether a cheap phone stand should ship in the kit.
- No validation study for single-leg stance / postural sway from a smartphone accelerometer was retrieved before the search budget was exhausted — this sub-topic remains unresearched and needs a follow-up pass, as does sit-to-stand POWER (watts) estimation validity specifically.
- Do the SSI-photo models generalize across skin tones? RedScar's core feature is a gray-scale-morphology red-pixel proportion with a 0.63 cutoff, which is mechanistically likely to underperform on darker skin, and the source study reports neither skin-tone stratification nor a Fitzpatrick distribution. This is both a safety and an equity blocker.
