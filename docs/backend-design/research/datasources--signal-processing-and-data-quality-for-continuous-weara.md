# Signal processing and data quality for continuous wearable streams in a clinical RTM product (post-op orthopedic recovery)

## Summary

The classical wear/non-wear literature (Troiano, Choi, van Hees/GGIR) is built on raw or count-level accelerometry at 1-min or finer epochs, which consumer PPG wearables never expose — so the algorithms cannot be ported directly and must be replaced with a proxy-based wear model built from the presence/absence of vendor aggregates (HR samples per hour, step epochs, sleep sessions). The single most product-relevant validation finding is that wrist wearables essentially fail to count steps in TKA patients using a walker: Apple Watch Series 4 recorded 2.2±5.6 steps and Fitbit Charge 0.9±3.0 steps against 24.7±0.4 actual over 50 m (bias 22.5 and 24.37 steps), recovering to near-zero bias only once the gait aid is dropped. Any step-based recovery curve for days 0-14 post-TKA/THA is therefore measuring gait-aid use, not recovery, and must be gated on aid status. Minimum-data rules have empirical grounding: GGIR defaults to 16 valid hours per calendar day and per noon-to-noon night; ≥2 days of ≥10 h/day gives reliability 0.86 in a large accelerometry cohort, while sedentary-behavior estimates need 6-8 days. The noon-to-noon window is the standard, defensible fix for the sleep-spanning-midnight day-boundary problem. Circadian rest-activity metrics are the highest-value under-exploited signal for this population: in 53,417 UK Biobank participants, suppressed 24-h amplitude (Q1 vs Q4) carried HR 1.94 (1.53-2.46) and higher intradaily variability HR 1.49 (1.18-1.88) for incident delirium — directly relevant to a mean-age-66 arthroplasty cohort — and Sleep Regularity Index at the 5th percentile (SRI=41 vs median 60) carried all-cause mortality HR 1.53 (1.41-1.66) in 88,975 people. Cross-brand offsets are large and systematic (mechanical shaker: MAE 16.9 mg ActiGraph vs 32.5 mg Garmin; free-living steps: Apple +2.12%, Oura -6.24%, Fitbit +18.00% vs ActiGraph), so a mid-episode device change must force re-baselining rather than continuing the same control chart. Finally, vendor data is mutable: Fitbit is 150 API requests/hour/user, returns everything in the user's local time with only a UTC offset (no IANA zone), syncs on a 15-min best-effort basis, and scores such as stress can be restated up to a week later — which makes an append-only, versioned Observation store with a monotonic revision counter and recomputation triggers mandatory, not optional.

## Findings

### Choi 2011 non-wear algorithm — exact parameters, and why it needs count-level data
*[strong]*

Operates on 1-minute epoch counts. Classifies non-wear as ≥90 consecutive minutes of zero counts, allowing an interruption of up to 2 minutes of non-zero counts provided the 30-minute upstream AND downstream windows flanking the interruption are all-zero. The 90-min window (vs Troiano's 60-min) was specifically chosen to reduce misclassification of sedentary wear as non-wear. Direct implication: this requires zero/non-zero counts at 1-min resolution — Fitbit intraday activity (1-min steps) or Apple HealthKit step samples can support it; Oura/Junction daily aggregates cannot.

> Choi L et al., Validation of Accelerometer Wear and Nonwear Time Classification Algorithm, Med Sci Sports Exerc 2011 (PMID 20581716) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20581716/ and Assessment of wear/nonwear classification algorithms for triaxial accelerometer, 2012 (PMID 22525772) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22525772/

### Troiano 2008 (NHANES) non-wear — 60 min / 2 min spike tolerance / <100 cpm
*[moderate]*

NHANES 2003-2006 convention: non-wear = ≥60 consecutive minutes of zero counts at 1-min epochs, with an allowance of up to 2 minutes of counts below 100 counts-per-minute (cpm) inside the zero run. Known to over-call non-wear during quiet sitting relative to Choi; the 60→90 min change is the main documented difference. Both are hip-worn-derived; on the wrist both algorithms degrade because wrist devices register incidental hand motion during sedentary wear.

> Troiano RP et al., Physical activity in the United States measured by accelerometer, Med Sci Sports Exerc 2008; contrasted in Choi 2011 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20581716/ and in the wrist/hip validation https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12889-019-6568-9

### van Hees / GGIR raw non-wear rule and current GGIR defaults (note: HDCZA is a SLEEP algorithm, not non-wear)
*[strong]*

Raw-acceleration non-wear: a window is non-wear if, for at least 2 of 3 axes, the per-axis standard deviation is < 13 mg AND the per-axis value range is < 50 mg. GGIR windowsizes default = c(5, 900, 3600) seconds: 5 s short epoch, 900 s (15 min) medium epoch over which non-wear/clipping is defined, 3600 s (60 min) window used for the non-wear decision, sliding in 15-min steps. nonwear_approach default = '2023', which flags the FULL 60-min window as non-wear (the older '2013' approach flagged only the central 15-min window) — this changes non-wear totals between GGIR versions and is a reproducibility hazard. Current default nonwear_range_threshold = 150 mg (not the classic 50 mg), combined with brand-specific SD thresholds. HDCZA (Heuristic algorithm on Distribution of Change in Z-Angle, van Hees 2018) is the default *sleep period time window* detector, frequently mis-cited as a non-wear method.

> GGIR configuration parameters vignette, CRAN GGIR 3.x (2024-2025) https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/GGIR/vignettes/GGIRParameters.html ; GGIR SIB/HDCZA chapter https://wadpac.github.io/GGIR/articles/chapter8_SleepFundamentalsSibs.html

### Adapting wear detection to aggregate-only PPG wearables: a concrete proxy algorithm
*[speculative]*

None of Troiano/Choi/van Hees is computable from vendor aggregates. Implementable substitute: define hourly wear evidence W(h)=1 if (≥1 HR sample in the hour) OR (step epoch present) OR (hour falls inside a vendor sleep session). With Fitbit intraday HR at 1-min detail, expected samples/hour ≈ 60 when worn; set worn if HR sample count ≥ 12/hour (20% coverage) to tolerate PPG dropout, non-wear if 0 samples for ≥90 consecutive minutes (Choi-analogous window), and 'unknown' in between. Because devices are charged daily, a 60-min rule will fire on the charging window — hence require the 90-min window and additionally exclude a single contiguous ≤2 h daily gap from the 'concerning non-wear' classification while still excluding it from denominators.

> Design synthesis over Choi 2011 parameters and Fitbit intraday endpoint capabilities https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/intraday/ — no published validation of this proxy exists

### Minimum wear-time criteria: empirical reliability by hours/day × days
*[strong]*

Large accelerometry QC analysis tested 1-14+ h/day × 1-10+ days. At ≥10 h/day: 1 day → reliability 0.76; 2 days → 0.86; 7 days → 0.96, but sample retention fell from n=6,528 (≥10 h, ≥2 d) to n=2,177 (≥10 h, ≥7 d) out of n=7,704. Recommendation: ≥2 days of ≥10 h/day as the retention/reliability optimum. In adults, Spearman-Brown analyses give ICC 0.7 at 3-4 days for MVPA, 4-7 days for light PA, and 6-8 days for sedentary time when wear-time-adjusted (13-15 days unadjusted); ≥6 h/day and ≥10 h/day produced equivalent reliability in one adult cohort. GGIR defaults are stricter: includedaycrit = 16 valid hours per calendar day and includenightcrit = 16 valid hours per noon-to-noon window.

> Quality Control Methods in Accelerometer Data Processing: Defining Minimum Wear Time, PLOS ONE 2013 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3691227/ ; Reliability of Objectively Measured Sedentary Time and PA in Adults, PLOS ONE 2015 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508000/ ; GGIR parameters https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/GGIR/vignettes/GGIRParameters.html

### The day-boundary problem is solved in the literature by a noon-to-noon window
*[strong]*

GGIR explicitly defines the sleep-assessment unit as a '24 hour window between noon and noon' (includenightcrit), separate from the calendar-day unit for activity (includedaycrit). This keeps a sleep episode that starts 23:40 and ends 06:20 inside one analysis unit and prevents a single night from being split across two rows. Sleep Regularity Index in GGIR is computed on 30-second epochs, with day-pairs excluded when the valid-data fraction falls below 0.66; the original sleepreg implementation drops entire days with >6 h of non-wear.

> GGIR parameters vignette https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/GGIR/vignettes/GGIRParameters.html ; GGIR Sleep Regularity Index vignette https://wadpac.github.io/GGIR/articles/SleepRegularityIndex.html

### Step counting collapses in exactly the population and window we care about most: TKA with a gait aid
*[strong]*

n=23 TKA patients (9M/14F, mean age 68.5, mean BMI 32), 50-m hallway walk, Apple Watch Series 4 and Fitbit Charge. With a WALKER (post-op days 1-3): actual 24.7±0.4 steps; Apple recorded 2.2±5.6, Fitbit 0.9±3.0; bias 22.5 (LoA 11.7 to 33.3) and 24.37 (LoA 21.66 to 27.08) — many devices recorded zero steps. With a CANE (6 weeks): Apple bias 5.73 (LoA -13.51 to 24.97), Fitbit bias -2.8 (LoA -25.31 to 19.71). With NO aid (pre-op and 6 months): bias -0.87 to 1.36, LoA -10.82 to 15.91. Conclusion: a wrist step count is not a valid recovery signal while a walker is in use, and the apparent 'step surge' at aid weaning is an artifact.

> Commercially available activity monitors such as the Fitbit Charge and Apple Watch show poor validity in patients with gait aids after total knee arthroplasty, 2024 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11247726/

### Cross-brand systematic offsets: raw acceleration and step totals are not interchangeable
*[strong]*

Mechanical shaker-table comparison — sampling rates: ActiGraph 100 Hz, Apple Watch 100 Hz, Fitbit 50 Hz, Garmin 25 Hz; dynamic range ActiGraph wGT3X-BT ±8 g, Fitbit Sense ±4 g. Concordance correlation coefficient (mean aggregation): ActiGraph 0.88, Fitbit 0.85, Apple 0.83, Garmin 0.59. Mean absolute error: ActiGraph 16.9 mg, Apple 21.6 mg, Fitbit 22.0 mg, Garmin 32.5 mg; ActiGraph mean bias 0.0 mg (95% CI -40.0 to 41.0). Apple underestimated acceleration by >20 mg and Garmin by >30 mg relative to ActiGraph; no device was statistically equivalent to the reference. Free-living step totals vs ActiGraph: Apple Watch +2.12%, Oura Ring -6.24%, Fitbit +18.00%.

> Comparison of raw accelerometry data from ActiGraph, Apple Watch, Garmin, and Fitbit using a mechanical shaker table, 2024 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10980217/ ; Comparison of consumer-grade wearables with a research-grade instrument in free living https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12928483/

### PPG heart-rate accuracy envelope — what error the control chart must tolerate
*[strong]*

24-h ecologically valid validation: Apple Watch 3 MAPE 5.86%, Fitbit Charge 2 MAPE 5.96% overall; Apple mean error -1.80 bpm (LoA -16.31 to +12.71), Fitbit -3.47 bpm (LoA -15.54 to +8.62). During activities of daily living the error roughly doubles: Apple MAPE 13.70%, Fitbit 8.29%. Living systematic review of Apple Watch (2025): pooled HR bias -0.27 bpm (95% CI -0.72 to 0.17) with LoA -7.19 to +6.64 bpm. 2026 ten-device comparison: Fitbit Charge 6 MAE 4.5 bpm / MAPE 5.5% / CCC 0.93; Google Pixel Watch 2 MAE 4.9 bpm / 6.7% / CCC 0.87; Fitbit Inspire 3, Polar Ignite 3, Polar Pacer and Oura Ring MAE 9-14 bpm / MAPE 11-16%. Umbrella review of 24 systematic reviews / 249 validation studies / 430,465 participants: HR mean bias ±3%. Practical consequence: a ±7 to ±16 bpm limit of agreement means single-day resting-HR deviations under ~5 bpm are inside measurement noise for most devices.

> Accuracy of Consumer Wearable HR Measurement During an Ecologically Valid 24-Hour Period, JMIR 2019 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6431828/ ; Apple Watch living systematic review, npj Digital Medicine 2025 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02238-1 ; Accuracy of Optical HR for 10 Commercial Wearables, JMIR Form Res 2026 https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e85186 ; Keeping Pace with Wearables umbrella review 2024 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11560992/

### HRV artifact rejection: exact Kubios thresholds and the automatic dRR algorithm
*[strong]*

Threshold-based beat correction levels (an RR interval is corrected if it differs from the local median RR by more than): very low 0.45 s, low 0.35 s, medium 0.25 s, strong 0.15 s, very strong 0.05 s. Automatic correction: build the dRR series (successive RR differences), compute the quartile deviation of the 90 surrounding beats and multiply by 5.2 to cover 99.95% of normal beats (=threshold Th); detect ectopics via NPN/PNP sign patterns in dRR; missed beat if |RR(i)/2 - medRR(i)| < 2·Th; extra beat if |RR(i)+RR(i+1) - medRR(i)| < 2·Th. Classic Malik rule: reject an RR interval deviating >20% from the preceding one. Uncorrected artifacts inflate RMSSD dramatically — one PPG example: 229 ms uncorrected → 166 ms after correction vs 163 ms ECG reference (a 40% overestimate). Detrending uses smoothness priors (time-varying high-pass) with λ tuned to remove VLF only.

> Kubios HRV preprocessing documentation, 2025-2026 https://www.kubios.com/blog/preprocessing-of-hrv-data/ and Kubios HRV Scientific User's Guide 2026 https://www.kubios.com/downloads/HRV-Scientific-Users-Guide.pdf ; Beyond Motion Artifacts: Optimizing PPG Preprocessing for PRV, arXiv 2510.06158 (2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06158v1

### Nonparametric circadian metrics — exact formulas
*[moderate]*

With activity binned into p bins/day over n total bins, N days, mean activity x̄: Interdaily Stability IS = [N·Σ(h=1..p)(x̄_h - x̄)²] / [p·Σ(i=1..n)(x_i - x̄)²], where x̄_h is the mean of hour-bin h across days; IS≈0 = Gaussian noise, IS≈1 = perfect day-to-day stability. Intradaily Variability IV = [n·Σ(i=2..n)(x_i - x_{i-1})²] / [(n-1)·Σ(i=1..n)(x_i - x̄)²]; IV≈0 for a pure sine, IV≈2 for Gaussian noise, IV>2 possible with high-frequency switching. M10 = mean activity over the most active continuous 10 h; L5 = mean over the least active continuous 5 h; Relative Amplitude RA = (M10 - L5)/(M10 + L5), bounded [0,1]. Cosinor complements these with MESOR, amplitude and acrophase from a fitted 24-h cosine. Standard bin width is 60 min (some studies use 30 min); minimum 7 days is conventional for IS.

> Witting/van Someren nonparametric CRAR definitions as operationalized in UK Biobank and NHANES analyses; e.g. Blunted rest-activity circadian rhythm and mortality, Sci Rep 2022 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-24894-z ; Rest-Activity Rhythms and prevalent CVD (NHANES), JAHA 2024 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10863829/

### Circadian disruption predicts delirium — the strongest clinical hook for a post-op orthopedic cohort
*[strong]*

UK Biobank, n=53,417, median 5-year follow-up, 551 incident delirium cases, 61 progressing to dementia. Wrist actigraphy-derived rest-activity rhythm metrics (normalized amplitude, acrophase, IS, IV). Suppressed 24-h amplitude, lowest vs highest quartile: HR 1.94 (95% CI 1.53-2.46, p<0.001). Higher fragmentation (IV, Q4 vs Q1): HR 1.49 (1.18-1.88, p<0.001). Delayed acrophase, per hour: HR 1.13 (1.04-1.23, p=0.003). Among delirium cases, suppressed amplitude predicted progression to dementia HR 1.31 (1.03-1.67) per 1-SD decrease. Separately, delirious post-op patients showed lower activity on the first post-op night and lower L5 activity within the first 24 h than non-delirious patients.

> Gao L et al., Circadian Rest-Activity Rhythms, Delirium Risk, and Progression to Dementia, Annals of Neurology 2023 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36808743/ ; Quantitative Analysis of Rest-Activity Patterns in Elderly Postoperative Patients with Delirium https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2335393/

### Sleep Regularity Index: definition, computation rules, and mortality association
*[strong]*

SRI = percentage probability of being in the same state (asleep/awake) at any two time points exactly 24 h apart; computed as SRI = -100 + (200/(M(N-1))) · Σ δ(s_i, s_{i+M}) where M = epochs per day and δ=1 if the states match. Range -100 to +100 (100 = identical days, 0 = random). GGIR computes on 30-second epochs (forcing aggregation even when other analyses use 5 s), treats detected non-wear as missing, and excludes day-pairs where the valid-data fraction < 0.66. UK Biobank, n=88,975, Axivity AX3 worn 7 days, median SRI 60 (tertile means 48.5, 61.2, 70.3): all-cause mortality (3,010 deaths, 7.1-y median follow-up) HR 1.53 (1.41-1.66) at the 5th percentile (SRI=41) vs median; CVD mortality HR 1.88 (1.61-2.21); cancer mortality HR 1.36 (1.22-1.53). SRI carried risk information beyond SD of sleep duration and SD of sleep onset. External validation in MESA (n=1,978, mean age 68.7±9.2) linked irregularity to CVD risk, obesity, hypertension, glucose and HbA1c, independent of sleep duration.

> Windred DP et al., Sleep regularity and mortality: a prospective analysis in the UK Biobank, eLife 2023 https://elifesciences.org/articles/88359 ; Lunsford-Avery et al., Validation of the SRI in Older Adults, Sci Rep 2018 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-32402-5 ; GGIR SRI vignette https://wadpac.github.io/GGIR/articles/SleepRegularityIndex.html

### Fitbit API operational limits, timezone semantics and access gating
*[strong]*

Rate limit: 150 API requests per hour per consented user; exceeding returns HTTP 429 with a seconds-until-reset header; limit resets near the top of each hour. Data only advances after the user syncs: automatic Bluetooth sync every ~15 minutes when in range; MobileTrack ~30 minutes. All date/time fields are rendered in the resource owner's local timezone; there is NO IANA timezone in the payload — you must call Get Profile for the current timezone name and UTC offset in milliseconds, which is a point-in-time value and does not describe historical travel. A few endpoint families (activity, heart rate) accept timezone=UTC. Intraday data (12 endpoints across Active Zone Minutes, Activity, Breathing Rate, Heart Rate, HRV, SpO2, each in by-date and by-interval forms) is automatically available only for a developer's own 'Personal' app; third-party/commercial access requires a case-by-case application via Google Issue Tracker with additional review. Sleep Logs API v1 is deprecated in favor of v1.2 stages.

> Fitbit Web API Application Design guide https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/developer-guide/application-design/ ; Fitbit Intraday reference https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/intraday/ ; Sleep Logs Version 1 (Deprecated) https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/sleep-v1/

### Vendor restatement (backfill) is real and breaks naive idempotency-by-dedupe-key
*[moderate]*

Fitbit-derived scores are recomputed server-side after the fact: developer-community reports document the stress score changing up to a week after the sleep period ended. More generally, sleep-stage segmentation, resting heart rate and readiness/recovery scores are derived values that vendors recompute when late-syncing partial data arrives or when the vendor ships a model update — the same (user, date, metric) key can legitimately return a different value days later. A store keyed only on a dedupe hash of (provider, user, metric, timestamp) will silently either reject the correction or overwrite history with no audit trail. Distinguish 'observation identity' (provider + external id + metric + effective interval) from 'observation version' (fetched_at, payload hash, monotonically increasing revision).

> Fitbit Web API developer community threads on retroactive score updates and timezone semantics https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Web-API-Development/Clarification-on-time-zones-and-date-time-values/td-p/925255 ; Fitbit Application Design guide (sync-dependent availability) https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/developer-guide/application-design/

### Imputation: GGIR's approach and the specific danger of imputing 'normal'
*[moderate]*

GGIR defaults do.imp = TRUE (g.impute) and imputeTimegaps = TRUE, imputing missing epochs from the average value at the same time of day on the other valid days for that participant — i.e., a within-person, time-of-day-matched imputation, not a global mean. This is defensible for descriptive physical-activity summaries but is precisely the wrong default for clinical deviation detection: imputing yesterday's same-hour value into a gap manufactures a 'normal' reading and suppresses the very deviation the surveillance index exists to detect. Non-wear in a post-op cohort is not missing-at-random — a patient who is febrile, in pain, hospitalized, or has stopped charging their device generates non-wear that is itself correlated with the outcome — so imputed values must never enter the numerator of a deviation score. Safe pattern: impute only for display/continuity, never for detection; propagate a per-day coverage fraction into the confidence gate; and treat a sudden drop in coverage as a first-class alertable event alongside physiological deviation.

> GGIR configuration parameters (do.imp, imputeTimegaps defaults) https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/GGIR/vignettes/GGIRParameters.html ; Addressing Data Quality Challenges in Observational Ambulatory Studies: wrist-worn wearable monitoring, arXiv 2401.13518 (2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13518

### Baseline with no pre-op data: partial pooling / James-Stein shrinkage toward a procedure-and-age prior
*[speculative]*

Concrete implementable scheme when a patient enrolls post-operatively with zero pre-op days. Let the population prior for metric m in stratum s (procedure × age band × sex) be N(μ_s, τ_s²) and the patient's n observed valid days give mean x̄ with within-person variance σ². The shrunken personal baseline is b = w·x̄ + (1-w)·μ_s with w = n/(n + σ²/τ_s²), i.e. the classic empirical-Bayes/partial-pooling weight; the posterior SD is sqrt(1/(n/σ² + 1/τ_s²)), which is the number that should drive the confidence gate and the control-chart limits. Practical consequence: with n=2 valid days and σ²≈τ_s², the baseline is ~2/3 population prior — so the emitted control limits are wide and few alerts fire, which is the correct behavior. Gate any clinical statement on posterior SD rather than on a raw day count. Note this is standard hierarchical-model methodology, not a validated post-op RTM baseline method — there is no published validation of shrinkage baselines specifically for orthopedic RTM.

> Standard empirical-Bayes/partial-pooling methodology (James-Stein/hierarchical models); no domain-specific validation located for post-op orthopedic RTM baselining

### Aggregation granularity: what each level costs you
*[moderate]*

Minute-level (Fitbit intraday 1-min HR/steps; HealthKit raw samples) is the only level at which Choi/Troiano-style non-wear, M10/L5 with a sliding 10-h and 5-h window, IV (which is defined on successive bins and is highly sensitive to bin width), and 30-s-epoch SRI are computable at all. Hourly retains IS, IV (at 60-min bins, the literature standard), M10/L5, RA and cosinor, and retains the noon-to-noon boundary, but loses per-minute artifact rejection and any within-hour non-wear detection. Daily aggregates retain only trend/EWMA on resting HR, HRV, steps and sleep duration and destroy every circadian metric — RA, IS, IV, M10/L5, SRI all become uncomputable. Storage cost of 1-min HR is ~1,440 rows/patient/day (~525k rows/patient/year); hourly is 24 rows/day. Recommended split: persist minute-level for HR/steps where the provider exposes it, materialize hourly bins as the analytic unit, and treat daily as a derived rollup only.

> Synthesis of GGIR metric definitions (60-min bins for IS/IV, 30-s for SRI) https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/GGIR/vignettes/GGIRParameters.html and Fitbit intraday detail levels https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/intraday/


## Implications for backend

- Observation store schema must separate identity from version. Key each row on (provider, provider_user_id, metric_code, effective_start, effective_end, aggregation_level) and add revision INTEGER, fetched_at, payload_sha256, superseded_at. On re-fetch, if payload_sha256 differs, insert a new row with revision+1 and set superseded_at on the prior row rather than UPDATE-ing in place — vendor restatement (Fitbit stress scores changing up to a week later, sleep-stage re-segmentation, resting-HR recomputation) makes overwrite-in-place lossy and unauditable. Add a derived_from_revisions JSON column on every computed analytic (baseline, EWMA state, CUSUM state, composite index) so a restatement can be traced to exactly which downstream statements must be recomputed and, if already surfaced to a clinician, retracted or annotated.
- Store every timestamp three ways: instant_utc (TIMESTAMPTZ), local_wall_clock (naive), and iana_tz (TEXT) captured at ingestion time, plus utc_offset_minutes. Fitbit returns local wall clock with no IANA zone and only a point-in-time offset from Get Profile, so historical travel and DST transitions are unrecoverable if you store only one representation. Every daily rollup must record which offset it was computed under. Handle DST explicitly: on spring-forward the local day is 23 h and on fall-back it is 25 h, so any 'hours of wear per day' denominator must be computed from the actual local-day length, not hardcoded 24, or you will spuriously fail or pass the wear-time gate twice a year. Compute all sleep-linked metrics (sleep duration, SRI, L5, acrophase) on a noon-to-noon local window per GGIR's includenightcrit convention, and store that window as an explicit analysis_window row rather than inferring it from calendar date.
- Implement a hard two-stage coverage gate before any clinical statement is emitted. Stage 1 (per day): valid_day = wear_hours >= 10 AND the largest contiguous gap outside the charging window <= 90 minutes; store wear_hours and coverage_fraction on the daily row. Stage 2 (per statement): require >= 2 valid days for HR/HRV trend statements (reliability 0.86 at 2 days x 10 h), >= 4 valid days for activity-volume statements, and >= 7 valid noon-to-noon nights for any circadian metric (IS, IV, RA, M10/L5, SRI). Emit an explicit typed reason code such as INSUFFICIENT_COVERAGE with the observed wear_hours and valid_day count rather than silently producing a low-confidence number — the existing coverage/confidence gate should consume wear_hours as an input, not a boolean.
- Never impute into the detection path. Add a computed_from enum on every metric row with values MEASURED, IMPUTED, EXTRAPOLATED. The composite deviation index, CUSUM and EWMA updaters must filter to MEASURED only; imputed values may be used solely for chart continuity in the UI and must be visually distinguished. Additionally, treat coverage collapse as its own alertable signal: a patient whose wear_hours drops from a 7-day median of 20 h to <6 h for two consecutive days should raise a distinct DATA_LOSS / DISENGAGEMENT reason code, because non-wear in a post-op cohort is informatively missing (illness, hospitalization, pain, readmission) rather than missing-at-random. Do not let a non-wear day 'reset' a CUSUM — freeze CUSUM state across non-wear rather than feeding it a zero or an imputed normal.
- Gate step-derived recovery curves on gait-aid status for the first 6 postoperative weeks. Wrist devices recorded 2.2 of 24.7 actual steps with a walker (bias 22.5 steps over 50 m) and only reach acceptable agreement once the aid is dropped, so any TKA/THA logistic expected-step curve covering days 0-14 is fitting gait-aid artifact. Add a patient-reported or clinician-entered gait_aid field (walker / crutches / cane / none) with an effective date, suppress step-count deviation reason codes while gait_aid IN ('walker','crutches'), and route early-recovery mobility inference to walking-bout duration, sleep, and circadian amplitude instead. Expect and explicitly annotate the apparent step 'jump' at aid weaning as an instrumentation change, not clinical improvement.
- Model device change as an episode boundary, not a continuation. Add a device_epoch table (patient_id, device_make, device_model, firmware, started_at, ended_at) and stamp device_epoch_id on every observation. On a new epoch: reset EWMA/CUSUM state, mark the prior personal baseline as superseded, and re-enter a shrinkage baseline period requiring the same minimum valid-day count as a new enrollment. Justification is quantitative: shaker-table MAE 16.9 mg (ActiGraph) vs 21.6 mg (Apple) vs 22.0 mg (Fitbit) vs 32.5 mg (Garmin), Apple underestimating >20 mg and Garmin >30 mg; free-living step totals differ by 24 percentage points between Fitbit (+18.00%) and Oura (-6.24%) against the same reference. Do not attempt a cross-brand conversion factor for HR/HRV — the LoA (roughly +/-7 bpm for Apple Watch, -16.3 to +12.7 bpm in 24-h free living) exceed most clinically meaningful deviations, so re-baselining is the only defensible option.
- Add per-metric plausibility and artifact filters at ingestion, before any statistic touches the value. Concrete parameters: reject HR samples outside 30-220 bpm; reject SpO2 outside 70-100%; reject skin temperature deltas outside +/-5 degC from the personal median; for RR/IBI data (if any provider exposes it) apply Malik's 20% successive-difference rule plus the Kubios automatic rule (quartile deviation of the 90 surrounding beats x 5.2, with missed-beat test |RR(i)/2 - medRR(i)| < 2*Th and extra-beat test |RR(i)+RR(i+1) - medRR(i)| < 2*Th), and refuse to emit RMSSD when the corrected-beat fraction exceeds 5% — uncorrected artifact inflated RMSSD from 163 ms (ECG truth) to 229 ms, a 40% error. For vendor-computed nightly HRV, require the sleep session to have >= 4 h duration and no more than one gap > 30 min before accepting the value.
- Store the personal baseline as a posterior, not a point. Persist (mu_prior, tau2_prior, x_bar, n_valid_days, sigma2_within, shrinkage_weight_w, posterior_mean, posterior_sd) per (patient, metric, device_epoch). Derive control limits from posterior_sd so that a patient with 2 valid days gets wide limits automatically and a patient with 21 days gets tight ones, and gate narrative generation on posterior_sd rather than on a raw day threshold. Seed mu_prior/tau2_prior per procedure x age band x sex stratum and version the prior table so a prior update is a traceable, replayable event.

## Open questions

- We could not verify Oura API v2, Terra, or Junction (fka Vital) restatement and backfill semantics this session (web search budget exhausted). Specifically unknown: whether Terra/Junction webhooks emit an update event when an upstream vendor restates a historical day, or whether they only emit on first arrival — this determines whether we need a scheduled re-pull sweep (e.g. re-fetch a trailing 14-day window nightly) or can rely on push. Confirm against Terra's data update / historical webhook docs and Junction's event types before finalizing the ingestion design.
- Apple HealthKit has no server-side API: data is on-device only and must be pushed by our app via HKAnchoredObjectQuery. We did not verify current behavior for deleted objects and anchor invalidation (e.g. after device restore or iCloud sync), which determines whether an Apple-sourced Observation can be retracted at all. Also unverified: whether HealthKit surfaces a stable UUID that survives a phone migration — if not, dedupe keys for Apple data must be content-derived.
- No published validation exists for aggregate-only wear detection on PPG wearables (the HR-sample-count proxy proposed above). The 12-samples-per-hour and 90-minute thresholds are reasoned analogues of Choi's parameters, not validated numbers. We should run an internal validation against a small cohort with concurrent self-reported wear diaries, and report sensitivity/specificity before this gate drives any clinical statement.
- Minimum wear-time criteria in the literature come from population physical-activity epidemiology (healthy adults and children), not from acute post-surgical surveillance where the relevant question is detecting a deviation within 24-48 hours, not estimating a habitual mean. The >=2 days x >=10 h / reliability 0.86 figure may be too permissive for infection surveillance and too strict for a day-3 statement. An internal analysis of how deviation-index sensitivity varies with coverage fraction is needed to set our own thresholds.
- We found strong circadian associations with delirium (amplitude Q1 vs Q4 HR 1.94; IV Q4 vs Q1 HR 1.49) and mortality (SRI 5th percentile HR 1.53), but did not locate any study linking IS/IV/RA/M10-L5 to surgical site infection or periprosthetic joint infection specifically. Whether circadian fragmentation adds incremental signal over resting HR and skin temperature for infection surveillance is unresolved and should be treated as a hypothesis, not a claim, in any clinician-facing narrative.
- Whether the 2026 ten-device optical HR study's Oura Ring figures (MAE 9-14 bpm, MAPE 11-16%) reflect the current Oura Ring 4 generation or an earlier model was not confirmed — the full text did not render. If we support Oura, this materially affects the device-specific measurement-noise parameter used to set control limits.
- GGIR changed its default non-wear approach from '2013' to '2023' and its nonwear_range_threshold default to 150 mg, which differs from the classic van Hees 50 mg range criterion. We should confirm which threshold set our chosen reference implementation uses and pin the version, because non-wear totals are not comparable across GGIR major versions and any internal validation would silently drift.
- Regulatory question not investigated here: whether emitting a coverage-gated clinical statement from restated (revision > 1) vendor data has any FDA SaMD or CMS RTM documentation implication — specifically whether an already-billed RTM treatment-management interaction based on a value the vendor later changed requires an amended record.
