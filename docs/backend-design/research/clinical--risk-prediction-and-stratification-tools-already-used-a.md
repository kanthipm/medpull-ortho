# Risk prediction and stratification tools already used and trusted in orthopedics (arthroplasty-focused): inputs, discrimination, calibration, openness, and the modifiable risk-factor thresholds surgeons act on

## Summary

Orthopedic surgeons already trust a small set of named, mostly open risk tools, and credibility for MedPull comes from reproducing them exactly rather than inventing a new score. The re-implementable ones (published items/weights, no license) are RAPT (6 items, 1-12 points, bands <6 / 6-9 / >9), mFI-5 (5 binary items), Charlson (19 conditions, weights 1/2/3/6, Quan ICD-10 maps), Elixhauser + van Walraven integer weights (AHRQ software is free), LACE (max 19 pts), HOSPITAL (7 items), ASA class, and the Tan PJI integer score. The ACS NSQIP Surgical Risk Calculator is free to use but its coefficients are not published, so it can only be linked to, not re-implemented. Honest discrimination ceilings matter: discharge disposition is the best-predicted outcome (RAPT AUC 0.772, CCI 0.729, mFI-5 0.720; ML ANN/HGB 0.78-0.89), PJI risk models land at c 0.69-0.84 with serious miscalibration above 3-4% predicted risk, and 30-day readmission is genuinely hard (ACS SRC 0.63; best externally-validated ML 0.61-0.72). Comorbidity indices are near-useless for complications/readmission (AUC 0.52-0.65), which is precisely the gap a longitudinal RTM signal can fill. The modifiable-risk thresholds surgeons actually enforce are concrete and should be first-class fields: BMI <40 kg/m², HbA1c ≤7.5% (deep infection OR 2.6 at ≥7.5%), albumin ≥3.5 g/dL (adjusted RR 1.5 for any complication below), Hgb >12 g/dL women / >13 g/dL men, smoking cessation ≥4 weeks, and S. aureus decolonization (S. aureus SSI RR 0.67). Finally, the CMS RSCR (MIPS #480) complication set with its 7/30/90-day windows is the outcome ontology that makes the analytics legible to surgeons and payers.

## Findings

### ACS NSQIP Universal Surgical Risk Calculator — inputs, discrimination, and why it cannot be re-implemented
*[strong]*

Bilimoria et al., J Am Coll Surg 2013: derived on 1,414,006 patients from 393 ACS NSQIP hospitals spanning 1,557 unique CPT codes; 21 preoperative inputs (age, sex, functional status, emergency case, ASA class, steroid use, ascites, systemic sepsis, ventilator dependence, disseminated cancer, diabetes, hypertension on meds, CHF within 30 days, dyspnea, current smoker within 1 yr, severe COPD, dialysis, acute renal failure, BMI/height/weight, plus procedure CPT) predicting 8 outcomes. Reported c-statistic 0.944 for mortality (morbidity reported ≈0.816). Free public web tool at riskcalculator.facs.org, but the regression coefficients have never been published — it is a black box you can link to but not recompute offline. Requires a CPT code as input, which the backend already has from RTM billing.

> Bilimoria KY, Liu Y, Paruch JL, et al. Development and Evaluation of the Universal ACS NSQIP Surgical Risk Calculator. J Am Coll Surg 2013;217(5):833-842. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24055383/

### ACS NSQIP calculator performs poorly for the outcomes an RTM product actually cares about
*[strong]*

In total joint arthroplasty validation, discrimination was: any complication AUC 0.72; DVT 0.70; periprosthetic joint infection 0.67; 90-day readmission 0.63 (Brier 0.048); return to OR 0.59. Predicted LOS overestimated actual by only 0.2 days on average but correlation was weak. Its one useful arthroplasty role is predicting discharge to SNF/rehab. Across orthopedic trauma it systematically underestimates serious complications (multiple rib fractures 8.3% predicted vs 17.2% observed; pelvic ring/acetabular fracture 6.1% vs 19.8%); in geriatric lumbar surgery, 13.7% predicted vs 43.8% observed all-complications. Treat it as a counseling aid, not a monitoring engine.

> ACS NSQIP SRC arthroplasty validation, J Arthroplasty 2017 (PMID 28899592); Validity of the ACS NSQIP SRC in subacute orthopedic trauma, Heliyon 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28899592/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10875421/

### RAPT — full item set and point values (fully open, trivially re-implementable)
*[strong]*

Oldmeadow 2003, 6 preoperative items: Age 50-65 = 2, 65-75 = 1, >75 = 0; Gender male = 2, female = 1; Walking distance ≥2 blocks = 2, 1-2 blocks = 1, housebound = 0; Gait aid none = 2, single-point stick = 1, crutches/frame = 0; Community support none or ≤1×/wk = 1, ≥2×/wk = 0; Caregiver at home after discharge yes = 3, no = 0. Total 1-12. Bands: >9 low risk (home), 6-9 intermediate, <6 high risk (extended inpatient rehab). Original validation n=650 THA/TKA, 75% overall accuracy, 89% in the high-risk band; Hansen 2015 n=3,213 → 78% overall (THA 80%, TKA 77%), scores <6 and >10 predicted disposition with >90% accuracy; Tan 2013 n=569 TKR → 85%. The intermediate 6-9 band is the weak spot at only 62.3% accuracy — exactly where a monitoring signal adds value. A proposed modified banding shifts cuts to <7 / 7-10 / >10.

> The Risk Assessment and Prediction Tool (RAPT) after Hip and Knee Replacement: A Systematic Review, PMC6930846 (2019). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6930846/

### RAPT discrimination and dose-response, head-to-head vs mFI-5 and CCI
*[strong]*

In 858 ERAS TJA patients (364 THA, 494 TKA, 2018-2020): RAPT AUC 0.772 (95% CI 0.725-0.819) for non-home discharge — best of all indices; prolonged LOS AUC 0.656; any complication 0.535; 90-day readmission 0.554. Non-home discharge by band: RAPT >9 = 1.63%, 6-9 = 9.95% (OR 4.87), <6 = 37.2% (OR 27.2). In ~18,000 TKA/THA patients (J Arthroplasty 2023) each 1-point RAPT increase gave OR 1.82 for home discharge, 0.22 days shorter LOS, OR 1.13 for POD-0 ambulation, and +0.25 AM-PAC points. For same-day discharge, cutoffs ≥9 (TKA) and ≥11 (THA) were only 66.7% accurate vs 91.7% for the surgeon's documented preoperative discharge plan — a caution against over-trusting RAPT for SDD.

> Sequeira SB et al., Utility of Frailty Index vs RAPT in ERAS THA/TKA, PMC12205658 (2025); Kugelman D et al., RAPT Score Predicts Discharge Destination, LOS, and Postoperative Mobility After TJA, J Arthroplasty 2023. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12205658/ ; https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(23)00479-5/fulltext

### mFI-5 — 5 binary items, good for disposition, poor for complications
*[strong]*

mFI-5 items: (1) non-independent functional status, (2) diabetes mellitus, (3) COPD or current pneumonia, (4) congestive heart failure within 30 days, (5) hypertension requiring medication; score 0-5. In the 858-patient ERAS TJA cohort: not frail (0-1) 63.8%, prefrail (2-3) 31.8%, frail (4-5) 4.4%. Non-home discharge 4.02% / 14.7% (OR 2.31) / 43.2% (OR 8.82); AUC 0.720 non-home discharge, 0.649 prolonged LOS, 0.525 any complication, 0.574 90-day readmission. Delirium rose 0.18% → 0.73% → 5.26% across bands. Other arthroplasty series: each mFI-5 point raises medical complication risk 66%, readmission 52%, adverse discharge 45%; mortality OR 2.66 per point; in patients >50 an mFI-5 of 1 vs 0 gave OR 3.27 for non-home discharge. mFI-11 (the older NSQIP 11-item version) has largely been replaced because several of its variables were dropped from NSQIP after 2015. Fully open.

> Sequeira SB et al., PMC12205658; Traven SA et al., New 5-Factor Modified Frailty Index Predicts Morbidity and Mortality in Primary Hip and Knee Arthroplasty, J Arthroplasty 2019. https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(18)30821-0/pdf

### ASA physical status — crude but the single most surgeon-recognized risk token
*[strong]*

1-5 scale (1 normal healthy → 5 moribund), +E for emergency. In 2,089,830 ACS-NSQIP outpatient cases, adjusted OR for 30-day mortality vs ASA II: ASA I 0.19 (0.13-0.29), ASA III 7.00 (5.28-9.29), ASA IV 40.7 (29.5-56.0), ASA V 223 (161-307). Univariate c-statistic 0.79 (0.78-0.80) for 30-day mortality; composite models reach 0.82. In a Swedish perioperative registry of hip arthroplasty 2013-2022, ASA 3-5 carried OR 3.3 for 30-day mortality, consistent in both osteoarthritis and hip-fracture subgroups. ASA ≥4 was a top-5 predictor in the best-performing ML PJI model. Caveat: interrater reliability is imperfect — anesthesiologist/surgeon ASA discordance is documented and itself correlates with adverse outcomes.

> Hackett NJ et al. ASA class is a reliable independent predictor of medical complications and mortality following surgery, Int J Surg 2015; BMC Surgery 2021 ambulatory ASA analysis (n=2,089,830). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25937154/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12893-021-01256-6

### Charlson Comorbidity Index — open weights; the best comorbidity index for complications/readmission in TJA (but still weak)
*[strong]*

Original Charlson 1987 (n=559, 19 conditions). Weight 1 — MI, CHF, peripheral vascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, dementia, chronic pulmonary disease, rheumatologic disease, peptic ulcer, mild liver disease, diabetes; weight 2 — diabetes with end-organ damage, hemiplegia/paraplegia, renal disease, any malignancy/leukemia/lymphoma; weight 3 — moderate/severe liver disease; weight 6 — metastatic solid tumor, AIDS. Age-adjusted variant adds 1 point per decade over 40. In ERAS TJA (n=858) CCI AUCs: non-home discharge 0.729, prolonged LOS 0.671, any complication 0.645, 90-day readmission 0.619 — and CCI was the ONLY independent predictor of complications (OR 1.18/point) and readmissions (OR 1.20/point); non-home discharge OR 1.23/point, prolonged LOS OR 1.27/point. Quan 2005 ICD-9/ICD-10 coding algorithms are published and free.

> Charlson ME et al. J Chronic Dis 1987;40(5):373-83; Quan H et al. Med Care 2005; Sequeira SB et al. PMC12205658. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12205658/

### Elixhauser Comorbidity Measure + van Walraven weights — outperforms Charlson and mFI in arthroplasty
*[strong]*

31 (commonly 30, or 29 when cardiac arrhythmia is unavailable) comorbidity flags. Van Walraven converted mortality-model coefficients to integer weights by dividing each by the smallest absolute coefficient and rounding; the 30- and 29-variable weighted summary scores gave c-statistics of 0.804 and 0.802 for in-hospital mortality on NIS data. In THA (2013 National Inpatient Sample), ECM out-discriminated CCI, mFI, age, BMI and sex for CMS procedure-specific complications, extended LOS, and discharge to a facility. In shoulder arthroplasty, demographics + full ECM variable set gave c = 0.867 (death), 0.752 (extended LOS), 0.81 (non-routine discharge), beating CCI. In total ankle arthroplasty both ECI and CCI hit AUC 0.88 for mortality, but ECI beat CCI for complications, extended LOS and adverse discharge. AHRQ publishes free ICD-10 Elixhauser software; a weighted Elixhauser index specific to 90-day readmission after TJA exists (J Arthroplasty 2019).

> Ondeck NT/Menendez ME et al., Discriminative Ability of Elixhauser's Comorbidity Measure is Superior… After Total Hip Arthroplasty, J Arthroplasty 2017 (PMID 28927567); van Walraven C et al., Med Care 2009; BMC Health Serv Res 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28927567/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7786470/

### LACE index — exact point table, thresholds, and modest discrimination
*[strong]*

van Walraven 2010 (CMAJ). L = length of stay: <1 d 0, 1 d 1, 2 d 2, 3 d 3, 4-6 d 4, 7-13 d 5, ≥14 d 7. A = emergent/acute admission: 3. C = Charlson score: 0→0, 1→1, 2→2, 3→3, ≥4→5. E = ED visits in prior 6 months: 0→0, 1→1, 2→2, 3→3, ≥4→4. Max 19. Derivation n=2,393, internal validation n=2,419, external validation n=1,000,000; c-statistic 0.7114 / 0.6935 / 0.684 for death or unplanned readmission within 30 days. Risk runs 2.0% at score 0 to 43.7% at score 19; the ≥10 high-risk cut is conventional, not derived. LACE+ tested in an orthopedic surgery cohort of 18,893 patients across a multi-center system 2016-2018 with Q4 vs Q1 significantly predicting 30-day readmission; a modified LACE+ externally validated in 368,154 patients gave c = 0.681 (0.678-0.684). Fully open.

> van Walraven C et al. Derivation and validation of an index to predict early death or unplanned readmission after discharge (LACE). CMAJ 2010;182(6):551-7. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2845681/

### HOSPITAL score — 7 open predictors, c=0.72, but derived in medical (not surgical) patients
*[moderate]*

Components at discharge: hemoglobin <12 g/dL, discharge from an oncology service, sodium <135 mmol/L, any procedure during the index admission, urgent index admission type, number of admissions in the prior 12 months, and length of stay ≥5 days. International validation on 117,065 consecutive adult medical discharges (2011): 30-day all-cause readmission 14.5% (16,992), 30-day potentially avoidable readmission 9.7% (11,307); c-statistic 0.72 (95% CI 0.72-0.72) with excellent calibration (Pearson chi-square p=0.89). Risk strata: low 62.4% (n=73,031), intermediate 23.6% (27,612), high 14.0% (16,422). Caveat: derived and validated on medical services — transfer to elective arthroplasty is unproven and the oncology-service term is inert here.

> Donzé JD et al. International Validity of the HOSPITAL Score to Predict 30-Day Potentially Avoidable Hospital Readmissions. JAMA Intern Med 2016. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26954698/

### CMS/MIPS #480 RSCR — the exact complication ontology and time windows to encode
*[strong]*

Risk-standardized complication rate after elective primary THA/TKA, Medicare FFS ≥65. Complication windows measured from index admission start: AMI, pneumonia, sepsis/septicemia/shock within 7 days; surgical site bleeding, pulmonary embolism, and death within 30 days; mechanical complication and periprosthetic joint infection/wound infection within 90 days. Complications count only if they occur during the index admission or a subsequent inpatient admission. Risk adjustment: age, sex, one vs two procedures, and selected clinical comorbidities present at index admission or in the preceding 12 months; in-hospital-acquired complications are NOT adjusted for. Exclusions: <90 days post-discharge Part A enrollment (unless died), hospital transfers, AMA discharges, >2 THA/TKA in one admission, unattributable cases, and COVID-19 (ICD-10-CM U07.1) on the index admission. RSCR = predicted/expected × national observed rate.

> CMS MIPS Quality Measure #480 (2024) Measure Information Form; CMS 2022 Procedure-Specific Complication Measure Updates and Specifications Report. https://mdinteractive.com/mips_quality_measure/2024-mips-quality-measure-480 ; https://p4qm.org/measures/1550

### PJI risk calculators (Tan / Del Toro / Bülow) — strong derivation, but a calibration cliff above 3-4% predicted risk
*[strong]*

Tan et al. 2018 (JBJS): 27,717 patients, 1,035 confirmed PJI, 2000-2014; 42 candidate factors, 17 retained, coefficients scaled to integer scores; most influential retained factors were previous open surgical procedure on the joint, drug abuse, revision procedure, and HIV/AIDS. AUC any PJI 0.83 internal / 0.84 external (independent institution n=29,252); antibiotic-resistant PJI 0.86/0.83; S. aureus PJI 0.86/0.73. Observed PJI ranged 0.56-0.61% in the lowest risk decile to 15.85-20.63% in the highest (~30× spread). Independent multinational external validation (Netherlands/Portugal/Spain, Jan 2020-Dec 2021; n=2,684, 1,528 primary THA, 60 PJI = 2.2%): Tan c=0.72 (0.65-0.78), calibration intercept -0.44 (-0.72,-0.17), slope 0.51 (0.33-0.68), overestimating above 4%; Del Toro c=0.69 (0.59-0.78), intercept 0.19, slope 0.74, underestimating above 3%; Bülow c=0.72 (0.62-0.81), intercept -0.35, slope 1.23 (0.76-1.69). All three were acceptably calibrated only below 3-4% predicted risk.

> Tan TL, Maltenfort MG, Chen AF, et al. J Bone Joint Surg Am 2018 (PMID 29715226); Predicting periprosthetic joint infection: external validation of preoperative prediction models, 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29715226/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11554715/

### Published ML models for TJA outcomes — names, datasets, sizes, AUCs
*[moderate]*

(a) PJI after primary THA: ACS-NSQIP 2013-2020, n=51,053; histogram-based gradient boosting AUC 0.88; top predictors LOS >3 days, weight >94.3 kg, ASA ≥4, preop platelets <249,890/mm3, preop sodium <139.5 mEq/L (internal validation only; several predictors are peri/post-operative). (b) THA all-cause complications within 2 years: stochastic gradient boosting, AUC 0.88, calibration intercept 0.1, slope 1.22, Brier 0.09; top features age, drug allergies, prior hip surgery, smoking, opioid use; deployed as a free open-access web app. (c) Navarro/Ramkumar 2018: naïve Bayes on 141,446 primary TKAs (2009-2016), AUC 0.7822 for LOS and 0.7382 for inpatient cost from age, race, sex and risk-of-disease/risk-of-morbidity scores. (d) Non-home discharge after primary TKA: ANN and histogram gradient boosting, internal AUC 0.83-0.84 rising to 0.88-0.89 on external validation (mean 0.89); strongest predictors LOS, age, BMI, sex. (e) Revision TKA discharge disposition: ANN AUC 0.78, calibration slope 0.93. (f) Non-home discharge after total shoulder arthroplasty: boosted decision tree AUC 0.788 (90.3% accuracy), ANN AUC 0.851 (89.9%). (g) Revision TKA 30-day unplanned readmission: histogram gradient boosting AUC 0.95 — internal only, treat skeptically. (h) SORG-MLA for prolonged opioid prescription after TKA, internationally validated on 3,495 Taiwanese patients.

> Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2025 (THA PJI ML, n=51,053) https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00402-025-05757-4 ; Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2022 THA complications ML https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35508549/ ; Navarro SM et al. J Arthroplasty 2018 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30243882/ ; J Arthroplasty 2023 non-home discharge int/ext validation https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36764409/

### The honest externally-validated ceiling for readmission and 90-day complications is AUC ~0.6-0.75
*[strong]*

NY SPARCS, 247,875 TJA procedures 2012-2016 (train 168,555 / val 37,182 / test 37,182) plus a 6,000-patient external validation set from a separate arthroplasty center, comparing logistic regression, AdaBoost, XGBoost, random forest, SVM, and 1- and 5-layer neural networks. Test → external AUROC: 30-day mortality 0.78 → 0.72 (RF); 30-day readmission 0.61 → 0.68 (RF); 90-day readmission 0.73 (XGBoost) → 0.72 (AdaBoost); 90-day composite complications 0.73 (RF) → 0.69 (AdaBoost). Consistent top predictors: age, hospital length of stay, Elixhauser Comorbidity Index, diseases of the circulatory system, surgical blood loss. Any claim of AUC >0.9 for readmission should be assumed overfit until externally validated.

> Comparable performance of machine learning algorithms in predicting readmission and complications following total joint arthroplasty with external validation, 2023. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10631030/

### PROM-improvement (MCID) prediction models — achievable accuracy is c ≈ 0.70-0.77 and baseline PROM dominates
*[moderate]*

KOOS-PS MCID after TKA: elastic-net penalized logistic regression best, c-statistic 0.77 (Katakam et al., J Orthop Res 2022). KOOS JR MCID and substantial clinical benefit after TKA: n=1,064 with 81 preoperative variables including KOOS JR and PROMIS-10; random forest and penalized logistic regression reached AUC at or above ~0.70 for all outcomes; the dominant signal is that HIGHER preoperative KOOS JR predicts FAILURE to achieve MCID (ceiling effect). Hip arthroscopy comparator (FIRST RCT + embedded cohort): iHOT-12 logistic regression AUC 0.724 at 12 months; HOS AUC 0.672-0.715 at 6 months, 0.665-0.699 at 12 months. Implication: baseline PROM is the single strongest feature and must be captured preoperatively or the model is not buildable.

> Katakam A et al. J Orthop Res 2022 (KOOS-PS MCID elastic net); Knee Surg Relat Res 2025, KOOS JR MCID/SCB ML (n=1,064). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jor.25125 ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43019-025-00289-y

### Modifiable preoperative risk factors — the exact thresholds surgeons enforce before offering surgery
*[strong]*

BMI: <40 kg/m² target; morbid obesity raises PJI ~3-4× (THA OR 4.332, 95% CI 2.943-6.375; meta-analytic OR 3.27, 95% CI 2.46-4.34); absolute 15-year PJI risk with no other risk factors is 1% at normal weight, 2% class III obesity, 4% class IV. HbA1c: ≤7.5% target — ≥7.5% carried OR 2.6 (1.9-3.4) for deep infection; competing data-driven thresholds are contested (7.7%, where PJI rose 0.8%→5.4%; 9.7% for TKA and 7.8% for THA; a 2025 continuous-variable analysis concluded HbA1c has limited standalone utility). HbA1c/albumin ratio >2.37 gave OR 14.6 (3.18-67.1) for periprosthetic infection. Albumin: ≥3.5 g/dL — in 49,603 elective primary TJA NSQIP patients hypoalbuminemia raised any complication to 7.3% vs 4.0% (unadjusted RR 1.8 [1.6-2.2]; adjusted RR 1.5 [1.2-1.7]); in revision arthroplasty it conferred 4.32× wound infection and 2.78× deep SSI at 30 days. Hemoglobin: >12 g/dL women, >13 g/dL men; preop anemia prevalence ~15% TKA, ~23% THA, and 46.6% in revision vs 18.7% primary; anemic revision patients stayed 5.3 days longer vs 1 extra day in primaries. Smoking: cessation ≥4 weeks preoperatively. Opioids: ≥50% reduction by 4 weeks preop.

> MacMahon A et al. Preoperative Patient Optimization in Total Joint Arthroplasty—The Paradigm Shift from Preoperative Clearance (narrative review of 69 studies), 2021; Cancienne JM et al. HbA1c threshold, J Arthroplasty 2017 (PMID 28662955); Bohl DD et al. Hypoalbuminemia Independently Predicts SSI, Pneumonia, LOS and Readmission After TJA, J Arthroplasty 2016. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28662955/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883540315007822

### S. aureus colonization/decolonization and social determinants — two feature families with hard effect sizes
*[strong]*

Decolonization: mupirocin-based nasal decolonization vs no-treatment/placebo reduced S. aureus SSI with RR 0.67 (95% CI 0.49-0.91) and nasal colonization with RR 0.22 (0.18-0.26); in orthopedic surgery specifically, overall SSI RR 0.80 (0.65-0.99), with larger effects when targeted to confirmed carriers and combined with chlorhexidine gluconate bathing; a 5-day mupirocin course eradicates >90% of nasal colonization. SDOH: high Area Deprivation Index after TKA — any medical complication 11.7% vs 11.0% (OR 1.05), respiratory failure 0.4% vs 0.3% (OR 1.28), AKI 1.7% vs 1.5% (OR 1.15); most-deprived quartile periprosthetic fracture OR 2.07 (1.21-3.54); social-deprivation cohorts had 30-day readmission HR 1.74 and ED-visit HR 1.83. Black patients had ED-visit OR 2.15 (1.10-4.20) and readmission OR 2.70. Medicare/Medicaid coverage and prior ED utilization were consistent independent predictors of postoperative ED visits.

> Mupirocin-based nasal decolonization to prevent S. aureus SSI: meta-analysis of RCTs, Int J Infect Dis 2026 (PMID 41862082); A Nationwide Analysis of the Impact of Socioeconomic Status on Complications After TKA Using the Area Deprivation Index, J Arthroplasty 2024 (PMID 38615971). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862082/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38615971/

### Methodological red flags to design around when citing or building ML risk scores
*[strong]*

Systematic reviews across TJA prediction models converge on the same findings: most models never undergo external validation, calibration is rarely reported, risk of bias is high, and models reporting AUC >0.9 are likely overfit. A 2025 review of ML for VTE after joint arthroplasty covered 34 prediction models from 9 studies and found significant heterogeneity and high risk of bias throughout. A systematic review of ML prognostic models for TKA/THA (databases searched to 20 Jan 2024) and a TRIPOD-based critical appraisal of shoulder-arthroplasty ML models both concluded reporting transparency and methodological conduct were inadequate. Practical consequence for a clinical product: always report discrimination AND calibration (intercept, slope, Brier), state the derivation population and date range, and never present a model outside its validated risk range.

> Supervised machine learning-based bias risk of prognostic models for total knee or hip arthroplasty patients: a systematic review, PMC12537099 (2025); Machine Learning for Predicting VTE After Joint Arthroplasty: Systematic Review, PMC12900511. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12537099/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12900511/


## Implications for backend

- Add a versioned `risk_profile` record computed once preoperatively, holding: RAPT (store the 6 raw item answers plus derived 1-12 score and band), mFI-5 (5 booleans + 0-5 score), ASA class 1-5 + emergency flag, Charlson (19 condition flags, weights 1/2/3/6, plus age-adjusted variant) and Elixhauser 31 flags with van Walraven integer weights. All five are open with published item weights and should be re-implemented natively in Python — no license, no external call, no LLM involvement. Expose each as a typed reason code the existing rule-based tier engine can cite (RAPT_HIGH_RISK, MFI5_FRAIL, CCI_GE_3, ASA_GE_4).
- Encode the modifiable-risk thresholds as first-class validated fields, each emitting an OPTIMIZATION_GAP reason code when breached: BMI ≥40 kg/m2, HbA1c >7.5% (store the raw value — surgeons argue 7.5 vs 7.7 vs 8.0), albumin <3.5 g/dL, hemoglobin <12 g/dL (F) / <13 g/dL (M), current smoker within 4 weeks of surgery, S. aureus carrier status plus decolonization-completed flag, prior PJI or prior open procedure on the same joint, active immunosuppression (chronic steroids, TNF inhibitors, other biologics), drug abuse, and HIV/AIDS. The last four carry the largest weights in the Tan PJI score.
- Re-anchor the composite deviation index to the CMS/MIPS #480 windows rather than a generic 'infection surveillance' number: weight AMI/pneumonia/sepsis signals through post-op day 7, surgical-site bleeding/PE/mortality through day 30, and mechanical complication/PJI-wound infection through day 90. Every alert then maps to a complication surgeons and payers already recognize, and the product can report an observed complication rate directly comparable to the RSCR denominator (excluding transfers, AMA discharges, >2 joints per admission, and U07.1 index admissions).
- Blend static preoperative risk into the dynamic signal as a prior rather than a replacement: use RAPT/mFI-5/CCI to set the starting risk tier for roughly the first 72 hours (while the coverage-confidence gate is still failing on thin sensor history), then let EWMA/CUSUM deviations dominate. The published numbers justify this split precisely — static indices hit AUC 0.72-0.77 for disposition but only 0.52-0.65 for complications and readmission, which is the gap longitudinal RTM data exists to close.
- Present PJI and readmission risk in calibrated bands, never as a point probability: externally validated PJI models are only trustworthy below 3-4% predicted risk (Tan calibration slope 0.51, Del Toro 0.74, Bülow 1.23) and the best externally validated readmission ML sits at AUC 0.61-0.72. Store calibration metadata (c-statistic, intercept, slope, Brier, derivation cohort, validation cohort) alongside every score the engine emits and surface it in the clinician-facing UI.
- Do not attempt to re-implement the ACS NSQIP Surgical Risk Calculator — its coefficients are unpublished. Deep-link to riskcalculator.facs.org instead, but capture all 21 of its input variables anyway (age, sex, functional status, emergency, ASA, steroids, ascites, sepsis, ventilator, disseminated cancer, diabetes, HTN on meds, CHF, dyspnea, smoker, severe COPD, dialysis, ARF, BMI, height/weight, CPT) because they simultaneously feed CCI, Elixhauser, mFI-5 and any future in-house model.
- Capture baseline preoperative PROMs — KOOS JR / KOOS-PS for knee, HOOS JR for hip, PROMIS-10 globally — because baseline PROM is the dominant feature in every MCID-prediction model (c 0.70-0.77) and higher preoperative scores predict FAILURE to achieve MCID. Add MCID and substantial-clinical-benefit achievement flags to the outcome schema so the engine reports expected-vs-achieved functional improvement, not just vitals deviation.
- Add SDOH features with concrete encodings: 9-digit-ZIP Area Deprivation Index national percentile, payer type (Medicare/Medicaid/commercial), rurality, prior 6-month ED visits (which also supplies LACE's 'E' term), and caregiver availability (already RAPT item 6). Published effects justify inclusion — periprosthetic fracture OR 2.07, 30-day readmission HR 1.74, ED HR 1.83 — and caregiver/support items are among the few risk levers a remote-monitoring product can actually move.
- Compute LACE at discharge (L from actual LOS using the 0/1/2/3/4/5/7 table, A=3 if emergent, C from Charlson bands 0/1/2/3/5, E from prior-6-month ED visits 0-4; max 19) as the readmission prior for the post-discharge window, and calibrate the alerting cut locally rather than defaulting to the conventional ≥10 threshold — external c-statistic is only 0.684, so it should shade the tier, not set it.

## Open questions

- Does MedPull capture any preoperative data today, or only post-discharge sensor streams? RAPT, mFI-5, Charlson and every optimization threshold require a preoperative intake form; without one, only LACE-at-discharge and the CMS complication ontology are immediately usable.
- Are the non-arthroplasty procedures in scope (ACL reconstruction, rotator cuff repair, lumbar fusion/decompression, ankle, meniscus) served by any validated risk tool? The web-search budget was exhausted before this could be researched. Partial known answers: mFI-5 predicts complications, admission and mortality after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (PMID 31901389), and the ACS NSQIP SRC has been validated (poorly) in geriatric lumbar surgery. Whether spine-specific discharge/readmission tools exist needs a follow-up pass.
- The exact 2024 AAOS dental-prophylaxis CPG recommendation wording and strength of evidence could not be retrieved (both AAOS URLs returned header-overflow or 403). Known: a 2024 J Arthroplasty study found no PJI difference between TJA patients who had a dental procedure within 2 years and those who did not, and AAOS published suggested time intervals between dental procedures and TJA. Confirm before encoding any dental gating logic.
- Does CMS publish the RSCR model's risk-adjustment coefficients and c-statistic in its annual specifications report? The variable list and complication windows are public; whether the coefficients are re-implementable was not confirmed (the CMS PDF returned 403).
- Is the Tan PJI integer score table (all 17 retained factors with point values) reproduced in the open-access JBJS 2018 supplement or paywalled? Re-implementation depends on it — only the four most influential factors were retrievable here.
- Should the product surface a PJI probability at all given documented miscalibration above 3-4%, or restrict itself to deviation-from-baseline surveillance plus a binary 'elevated PJI risk profile' flag? This is a clinical-liability decision as much as a technical one.
- Which comorbidity index should be canonical: Elixhauser (better discrimination in THA/shoulder/ankle, 31 flags, needs claims-grade coding) or Charlson (weaker overall but the only independent predictor of complications and readmission in the ERAS TJA cohort, and far easier to collect directly from patients)? Recommend computing both and displaying whichever the surgeon audience expects.
- What is the true fructosamine threshold sometimes cited (~292 µmol/L) as an HbA1c alternative in diabetic arthroplasty patients? It could not be verified in this session and must not be encoded until sourced.
- Do any RAPT/mFI-5 re-validations exist in same-day-discharge or outpatient TJA cohorts post-2021, now that CMS has removed TKA/THA from the inpatient-only list? Discharge-disposition base rates have shifted enough that published band probabilities (e.g., 37.2% non-home discharge at RAPT <6) may no longer hold.
