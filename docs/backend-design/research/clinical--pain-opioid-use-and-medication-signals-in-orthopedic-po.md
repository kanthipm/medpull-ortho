# Pain, opioid use, and medication signals in orthopedic post-operative recovery — expected NRS trajectories by procedure, catastrophizing and central sensitization predictors, MME consumption and weaning timelines, persistent opioid use, VTE/antibiotic prophylaxis adherence, sleep and HRV as pain proxies, and wearable/EHR prediction model performance

## Summary

Post-operative pain after orthopedic surgery does not decay monotonically, and this is the single most actionable finding for the engine: two matched prospective cohorts show TKA pain falls from mean NRS 5.8 on POD1 to 4.6 on POD8, then rebounds to 4.8 on POD9 when intensive rehab starts, before settling to 3.0 by POD29; THA follows the same shape roughly 2.5 NRS points lower (3.1 to 2.3, bump to 2.6 on POD9, 2.3 by POD12). A latent-class analysis of 227 TKA patients splits the population into a 'Moderate-High Persistent Pain' class (45.2%, NRS intercept 6.96, slope -0.494/day) and a 'Rapid Relief' class (54.8%, intercept 5.63, slope -0.631/day), with preoperative NRS (OR 3.55) and Pain Catastrophizing Scale (OR 1.09/point) as dominant predictors, giving a directly implementable two-curve mixture model plus a POD1-5 slope feature. Pain measured at POD3, POD7, POD14 and POD30 (not POD0-2) is what predicts chronic postsurgical pain, whose base rates are 8-34% after TKA, ~10% after THA, ~24% at 6 months after rotator cuff repair, up to 26% at 2 years after ACL reconstruction, and ~20% after lumbar fusion. On opioids, pooled meta-analytic persistence is 26% (TKA) and 20% (THA) at 3 months and 21%/16% at 12 months, with median cessation at POD30 (95% CI 29-31 days); the citable definitional constant is Brummett's 'fill between 90 and 180 days in an opioid-naive patient', incidence 5.9-6.5% versus 0.4% in non-surgical controls. Prescribed-versus-consumed anchors are well quantified per procedure (TKA 88.4 pills prescribed vs 65.0 consumed; THA 64.0 vs 29.8; ACLR median 15 vs 2; ARCR ~90 MME) and Michigan OPEN caps THA at 0-30 and TKA at 0-40 oxycodone 5 mg tablets. Best published prediction performance is modest for claims-based models (SORG-MLA AUROC 0.75 with AUPRC only 0.34; Karhade lumbar-spine c-statistic 0.70) but a combined preoperative wearable-plus-EHR stacking ensemble reached AUC 0.88 for severe acute pain and 0.90 for chronic opioid use, which is the strongest evidence that this product's exact data shape can support real prediction. Medication signals worth alerting on center on VTE prophylaxis over a 14-35 day window (aspirin 81 mg, rivaroxaban 10 mg, enoxaparin 40 mg, or the EPCAT II hybrid), where 17.0% of patients are non-adherent and a separate 3.1% actually over-take. Finally, a documented negative result should shape the sleep feature set: wearable total sleep time after TKA did not change from baseline at 30/60/90 days and did not correlate with VAS at any timepoint, so any sleep-based pain proxy must be built on fragmentation, efficiency, WASO and daytime-nap shift instead.

## Findings

### TKA acute pain decay is NOT monotonic — there is a reproducible day-9 rebound at the rehab transition
*[moderate]*

Prospective observational cohort (n=104 TKA, standardized pain protocol, Benditz et al., J Clin Med 2022;11:7204): mean NRS 5.8 ± 2.8 on POD1, steady decline to 4.6 on POD8, REBOUND to 4.8 on POD9, then continuous decline to 3.0 by POD29. Authors attribute the POD9 bump to the start of the intensive rehabilitation program. Significant predictors of higher pain across all phases: female sex (p=0.001), LOWER BMI (p=0.010 at T1; p=0.003 at T2/T3), preoperative valgus deformity (p=0.020). Age, ASA score, surgical duration, implant type and approach were NOT significant. Engineering consequence: a naive monotonic-decay expected curve will fire false alarms on days 8-12 after TKA.

> Benditz A et al. 'Pain Course after Total Knee Arthroplasty within a Standardized Pain Management Concept: A Prospective Observational Study.' J Clin Med 2022;11(23):7204. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9741301/

### THA pain curve sits ~2.5 NRS points below TKA and has its own day-9 to day-12 bump — procedure-specific curves are mandatory
*[moderate]*

Same group's THA cohort (n=103, Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2018): mean NRS 3.1 on POD1, 2.3 on POD8, temporary rise to 2.6 on POD9, back to 2.3 by POD12. Compare TKA: 5.8 / 4.6 / 4.8. A single cross-procedure 'concerning pain' threshold is therefore wrong by roughly 2.5 NRS points; NRS 5 on POD5 is normal after TKA and clearly abnormal after THA. A separate 1,249-patient THA PROM trajectory analysis found 91.6% of THA patients fall into a single 'swift recovery' class, meaning the abnormal-THA tail is small and high-yield to detect.

> Benditz A et al. 'Course of pain after total hip arthroplasty within a standardized pain management concept.' Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2018;138:1103-1110. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00402-018-3014-x ; 'Postoperative pain trajectories in total hip arthroplasty: an analysis of patient-reported outcomes.' https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10914419/

### TKA patients split into two latent pain-trajectory classes with quantified intercepts and slopes — usable directly as a two-curve mixture model
*[moderate]*

Latent class growth analysis, n=227 TKA (Front Pain Res 2025;6:1659917). Class C1 'Moderate-High Persistent Pain': 45.16% (n=101), NRS intercept 6.956, slope -0.494 NRS/day. Class C2 'Moderate-Low Rapid Relief': 54.84% (n=126), intercept 5.631, slope -0.631/day. Average posterior class probabilities 0.980 and 0.972. Independent predictors of C1 membership: preoperative NRS OR 3.546 (95% CI 1.819-6.914, p<0.001); Pain Catastrophizing Scale OR 1.092 per point (1.022-1.167, p=0.009); age OR 1.052/yr (1.004-1.101); Family Care Index OR 0.763 (0.601-0.970, protective). C1 membership predicted worse 3-month function (beta=0.32, t=5.64, p<0.001), adding 6.2% explained variance over baseline predictors. Practical rule: a slope shallower than about -0.5 NRS/day over POD1-5 marks the high-risk class.

> 'Acute postoperative pain trajectories and their impact on functional recovery following total knee arthroplasty.' Front Pain Res 2025;6:1659917. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pain-research/articles/10.3389/fpain.2025.1659917/full

### Pain at POD3/7/14/30 predicts chronic postsurgical pain; CPSP incidence by procedure gives the base rates to calibrate against
*[moderate]*

Rest pain on POD3, POD7, POD14 and POD30 was associated with chronic postsurgical pain (CPSP) at long-term follow-up after TKA; conversely acute pain intensity in the first two postoperative days does NOT predict CPSP at 6 months after THA. So POD3/7/14/30 are the load-bearing sampling points and POD0-2 should be down-weighted. CPSP base rates: TKA 8-34% (commonly cited ~20%); THA ~10% (range 7-23%); arthroscopic rotator cuff repair 24% at 6 months (broader literature 10-50%); ACL reconstruction — clinically significant knee pain in up to 26% at 2 years (MOON cohort, AJSM 2026), anterior knee pain 3-47%; lumbar fusion ~20% with persistent back or leg pain. Overall bone-and-joint surgery CPSP incidence ~20%.

> 'The Impact of Acute Postoperative Pain in Developing Chronic Pain after Total Knee Arthroplasty.' Neurol Int 2024;16(2):34. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11054509/ ; 'Factors associated with persistent postsurgical pain after total knee or hip joint replacement: a systematic review and meta-analysis.' 2023. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9833456/ ; Cantrell WA et al. 'Prevalence of Postoperative Knee Pain After ACL Reconstruction at 2, 6, and 10 Years: Data From the MOON Group.' Am J Sports Med 2026. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03635465251414661

### Rotator cuff repair: pain recovery is ~44% at 3 months, ~70% at 6 months, ~85% at 1 year — a much slower, strictly monotone curve
*[moderate]*

Prospective comparative analysis of the 'big 3' shoulder surgeries plus a systematic review of ARCR recovery: VAS pain recovery rate 43.7% at 3 months, 69.9% at 6 months, ~85% at 12 months; VAS recovers faster than functional scores (ASES, Constant), which cross 50% only at 6 months. Pain after ARCR follows a strictly decreasing pattern with no rehab rebound analogous to TKA POD9, but is substantial for the first 5 days. 85-90% report no or minimal shoulder pain by 1-2 years. Functional recovery achieved by 3 months is itself a documented prognostic factor for 1-year outcome after ARCR.

> 'What Is the Trajectory of Recovery in the Early Postoperative Period after the Big 3 Shoulder Surgeries? Comparative Analysis Using 3 Previous Prospective Studies.' 2024. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11276123/ ; 'Recovery pattern in the early postoperative period after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair: a systematic review.' 2025. https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2058524125000799

### Pain catastrophizing separates rotator cuff pain curves starting POD2, with a hard PASS threshold of VAS <= 3.0
*[moderate]*

Prospective cohort n=33 ARCR (2025). Median split: low PCS mean 4.6 (SD 4.3) vs high PCS mean 25.0 (SD 14.4), p<0.001. Median VAS by day (low vs high): D0 3.0 vs 4.5 (NS); D1 3.5 vs 5.0 (NS); D2 2.0 vs 5.0 (p=0.02); D3 2.0 vs 5.5 (p=0.03); D4 2.0 vs 4.5 (p=0.03); D5 1.5 vs 4.0 (p=0.04). Patient Acceptable Symptom State was defined as VAS <= 3.0: the low-PCS group reached PASS by D2 and stayed there; the high-PCS group NEVER reached PASS during the first week. Directly implementable: 'days-to-PASS (VAS <= 3)' is a clean derived metric, and >2 days after ARCR flags the catastrophizing phenotype.

> 'Higher pain catastrophizing scale is associated with more postoperative pain within the first week after rotator cuff repair.' 2025. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12255946/

### PCS and CSI cutoffs: usable as risk modifiers with named thresholds, but both are contested and neither is a standalone alerting rule
*[moderate]*

Pain Catastrophizing Scale (13 items, range 0-52): >30 = clinically relevant catastrophizing (75th percentile), the standard threshold for risk-targeted intervention; preop PCS >20/52 gives OR 3.5 for pain intensity >30/100 at 1 year post-arthroplasty; in knee OA specifically PCS >=16 predicted poor TKA outcome; a model using PCS >=8 gave sensitivity 71.4% and specificity 62.5% for moderate-severe post-TKA pain (too unspecific to gate on). Countervailing evidence: PCS and painDETECT were poor predictors of postoperative pain, length of stay and early complications after TJA. Central Sensitization Inventory (25 items, range 0-100): CS defined as >=40; revision TKA patients with CSI >=40 had 39.081-fold increased odds of dissatisfaction; primary TKA and UKA CS groups had significantly worse postoperative pain, function and satisfaction. CRITICAL CAVEAT raised repeatedly in 2024-2025 work: the >=40 threshold was derived in fibromyalgia/depression populations, not knee OA, and studies deriving optimal orthopedic cutoffs propose different, generally lower values. Treat both as expected-curve shifters; flag the 39.081 OR as implausibly large and small-sample.

> Kim SH et al. 'Central Sensitization Is a Risk Factor for Persistent Postoperative Pain and Dissatisfaction in Patients Undergoing Revision Total Knee Arthroplasty.' J Arthroplasty 2019. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30992238/ ; 'Preoperative Central Sensitization as a Predictor of Pain Outcomes After Hip and Knee Arthroplasty: A Systematic Review.' 2025. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12910524/ ; 'Pain catastrophizing and pre-operative psychological state are predictive of chronic pain after joint arthroplasty of the hip, knee or shoulder.' 2022. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9556350/ ; 'Preoperative Pain Catastrophizing and Neuropathic Pain Do Not Predict Length of Stay and Early Post-Operative Complications following TJA.' 2023. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9962732/

### Persistent opioid use after TKA/THA: pooled 26%/20% at 3 months, 21%/16% at 12 months — far higher than surgeons assume
*[strong]*

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 30 studies (17 THA, 19 TKA; Jan 2009 to May 2021; Am J Health-Syst Pharm 2022;79(3):147-164). TKA: 26% (95% CI 19-33) at 3 months, 20% (17-24) at 6 months, 21% (12-29) at 12 months. THA: 20% (13-26) at 3 months, 17% (12-21) at 6 months, 16% (15-16) at 12 months. Over 1 in 5 patients still on opioids beyond 3 months. Opioid-naive patients were significantly less likely to continue than preoperatively opioid-tolerant patients. Separately, among opioid-naive TJA patients, 12.3% (792/6,432) had at least one opioid prescription between 90 days and 1 year.

> 'Persistent postoperative opioid use after total hip or knee arthroplasty: A systematic review and meta-analysis.' Am J Health-Syst Pharm 2022;79(3):147-164. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513405/

### The canonical 'new persistent opioid use' definition: opioid fill 90-180 days post-op in a previously opioid-naive patient; incidence 5.9-6.5% vs 0.4% in non-surgical controls
*[strong]*

Brummett et al. (JAMA Surg 2017;152(6):e170504) defined new persistent opioid use as an opioid prescription fulfillment between 90 and 180 days after surgery in opioid-naive adults. Incidence: 5.9% after minor surgery and 6.5% after major surgery, versus 0.4% in a non-operative control cohort, i.e. surgery multiplies baseline risk roughly 15-fold. The ASER/POQI-4 joint consensus statement (Anesth Analg 2019) is the citable authority for encoding a definitional constant. Alternative operationalizations in the literature: '>90 days of opioid use beginning within 90 days of surgery' and 'at least one opioid prescription between 90 days and 1 year'. Pick one, name it explicitly in the schema, and store which one was applied to each patient record.

> Brummett CM et al. 'New Persistent Opioid Use After Minor and Major Surgical Procedures in US Adults.' JAMA Surg 2017;152(6):e170504. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28403427/ ; ASER/POQI-4 Joint Consensus Statement on Persistent Postoperative Opioid Use, Anesth Analg 2019. https://journals.lww.com/anesthesia-analgesia/fulltext/10.1213/ane.0000000000003941~american-society-for-enhanced-recovery-and-perioperative

### Prescribed-vs-consumed opioid envelopes by procedure, in pills and OME — the concrete per-procedure expected-consumption table
*[strong]*

J Arthroplasty 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis: TKA 88.4 pills prescribed vs 65.0 consumed by 6 weeks; THA 64.0 prescribed vs 29.8 consumed by 12 weeks; patients consume about 75% of prescription after TKA and 50% after THA; recommended prescription ranges 50-104 pills TKA and 30-45 pills THA. Michigan OPEN 2025 (opioid-naive patients): THA 0-30 tablets oxycodone 5 mg (mean consumption 17-31.9 pills); TKA 0-40 tablets (mean consumption 33.4-39.9 pills); implementation cut prescription size 76% with no change in patient-reported pain or satisfaction. Michigan Surgical Quality Collaborative: TKA mean 632 mg OME prescribed vs 416 mg consumed. ACLR with multimodal protocol: median 15 oxycodone 5 mg prescribed, median 2 consumed — 38% consumed zero, 74% consumed <=5, 96% consumed <=15; adolescents averaged 0.4 pills with 81% taking none; opioid use and pain both drop sharply between POD3 and POD7. Shoulder: ARCR mean 90 MME (about 12 tablets oxycodone 5 mg); shoulder arthroplasty mean 67.5 MME (about 9 tablets). Conversions: oxycodone 5 mg = 7.5 OME, hydrocodone 5 mg = 5 OME. Note that sports-medicine expert consensus (30/45/60 pills for knee arthroscopy / meniscal repair / ACLR-or-ARCR) substantially overshoots measured consumption.

Fifteen Oxycodone 5-mg Tablets Are Sufficient.' Arthrosc Sports Med Rehabil 2023. https://www.arthroscopysportsmedicineandrehabilitation.org/article/S2666-061X(23)00009-3/fulltext ; 'Opioid consumption following orthopedic shoulder surgery: a retrospective analysis.' ### Expected weaning timeline: median opioid cessation about POD30 (95% CI 29-31 days) after TJA; still on opioids at 6 weeks is the actionable deviation
*[moderate]*

Medicare TKA/THA cohort: median time to discontinuation of prescription opioids = 30 days (95% CI 29-31 days). A prospective TJA cohort reported a longer median of 54 days, showing strong population dependence (payer mix, discharge disposition, opioid-tolerance status). With perioperative opioid counseling, median time to cessation shifted to POD 8-14 versus control. A wearable remote-patient-monitoring TKA pilot (Ramkumar et al., J Arthroplasty 2019) found opioid use typically stopped by postoperative day 5 in a fast-track cohort, with home-exercise-program compliance averaging 62% (range 0-99%) — that compliance range is itself a useful realism anchor for adherence targets. Suggested alert ladder: daily opioid use beyond POD30 = yellow; beyond POD42 = orange; any fill in the 90-180 day window = formal persistent-use flag.

> 'Association of occupational and physical therapy with duration of prescription opioid use after hip or knee arthroplasty: A retrospective cohort study of Medicare enrollees.' https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8263496/ ; 'Preoperative Versus Perioperative Risk Factors for Delayed Pain and Opioid Cessation After Total Joint Arthroplasty: A Prospective Cohort Study.' Pain Ther 2023. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40122-023-00543-9 ; Ramkumar PN et al. 'Remote Patient Monitoring Using Mobile Health for Total Knee Arthroplasty.' J Arthroplasty 2019. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883540319304954

### Published risk-factor odds ratios for persistent/prolonged opioid use, plus MME-inflation multipliers usable directly as prior weights
*[moderate]*

Effect sizes from arthroplasty and general surgical literature: depression OR 1.27-1.59 (in a TJA-specific study, mild depression OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.65-10.18); tobacco use OR 1.34-2.09; preoperative benzodiazepine use OR 1.59-4.83; preoperative opioid fill OR up to 4.34. MME-consumption multipliers after hip/knee arthroplasty (J Arthroplasty 2018): preoperative narcotic users filled 86% more MME, tramadol-only users 38% more, benzodiazepine users 81% more, and smokers 90% more than non-users. Discharge to inpatient rehabilitation was a strong independent predictor of persistent opioid use at 90 days in a prospective observational study.

> 'Preoperative Smoking and Narcotic, Benzodiazepine, and Tramadol Use are Risk Factors for Narcotic Use After Hip and Knee Arthroplasty.' J Arthroplasty 2018. https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(18)30345-0/abstract ; 'Rate and Risk Factors Associated With Prolonged Opioid Use After Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.' https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7317603/ ; 'Discharge to inpatient rehabilitation following arthroplasty is a strong predictor of persistent opioid use 90 days after surgery.' https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9840321/

### Best published model performance for predicting pain/opioid outcomes — including a wearable+EHR model at AUC 0.88-0.90
*[moderate]*

(a) SORG-MLA for prolonged postoperative opioid prescription after TKA, external validation on 3,495 Taiwanese patients: AUROC 0.75, AUPRC 0.34, Brier 0.029 vs null-model Brier 0.032 — discrimination is modest and calibration barely beats null because of the low event rate. (b) Karhade et al., prolonged opioid prescription in opioid-naive lumbar spine surgery: best model c-statistic 0.70. (c) All of Us Research Program (n approx 347 across 8 surgical specialties including orthopedics; at least 80% Fitbit wear required), combining 6 weeks of PREOPERATIVE Fitbit features (lightly active minutes, very active minutes, sedentary minutes, time asleep, deep sleep duration, time awake during main sleep) with EHR features (BMI, cancer, diabetes, myocardial infarction, anxiety/depression, preoperative opioid use, albumin, procedure type): a stacking ensemble (random forest + XGBoost base learners with logistic-regression meta-model) achieved acute postoperative pain (severe 7-10 vs other, within 6 weeks) AUC 0.88 (95% CI 0.85-0.91) with accuracy/precision/recall/F1 all 0.68; and chronic opioid use (6-12 months, dichotomized at 50 MME per CDC) AUC 0.90 (0.88-0.92), accuracy 0.89, precision 0.91. Logistic regression alone reached 0.74 AUC and random forest 0.81. Pain was trichotomized mild 0-3, moderate 4-6, severe 7-10.

> 'Predicting Postoperative Pain and Opioid Use with Machine Learning Applied to Longitudinal Electronic Health Record and Wearable Data.' 2024. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290948/ ; 'The Skeletal Oncology Research Group Machine Learning Algorithm (SORG-MLA) for predicting prolonged postoperative opioid prescription after total knee arthroplasty: an international validation study using 3,495 patients from a Taiwanese cohort.' BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2023. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10320986/ ; Karhade AV et al. 'Predicting prolonged opioid prescriptions in opioid-naive lumbar spine surgery patients.' ### CDC 2022 guideline removed hard MME/day and day-supply caps, but about half of US states retain a statutory 7-day initial acute-pain limit
*[strong]*

CDC Clinical Practice Guideline for Prescribing Opioids for Pain — United States, 2022 (MMWR Recomm Rep 2022;71(3):1-95, published November 2022) removed the 2016 guideline's specific day limits for acute pain and no longer places MME/day thresholds inside the formal recommendations; supporting text retains 'carefully reassess benefits and risks before increasing total opioid dosage to >50 MME/day' and extra caution above 90 MME/day. Approximately half of all states have passed legislation limiting the initial opioid prescription for acute pain to a 7-day supply or less, and many insurers, pharmacy benefit managers and pharmacies enforce equivalent policies independently. Consequence for a national product: MME/day and days-supply must be evaluated against a per-state rule table, and 50/90 MME/day should be encoded as advisory review triggers with provenance labels, never as hard blocks.

> Dowell D et al. 'CDC Clinical Practice Guideline for Prescribing Opioids for Pain — United States, 2022.' MMWR Recomm Rep 2022;71(3):1-95. https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/71/rr/rr7103a1.htm

### VTE prophylaxis after TKA/THA: exact regimens, durations, and the live aspirin-vs-DOAC controversy a surgeon expects the product to know
*[strong]*

EPCAT II (NEJM 2018;378:699-707, n=3,424): rivaroxaban 10 mg once daily until POD5, then randomized to continue rivaroxaban or switch to aspirin 81 mg daily for a further 9 days after TKA or 30 days after THA — aspirin was non-inferior for symptomatic VTE and cheaper. CRISTAL (JAMA 2022, cluster-randomized, n=9,711): aspirin 100 mg monotherapy was INFERIOR to enoxaparin 40 mg daily for 90-day symptomatic VTE, the difference largely driven by distal DVT. The 2022 International Consensus Meeting on VTE hip/knee delegates recommended low-dose aspirin 81 mg twice daily over LMWH for routine chemical thromboprophylaxis in standard-risk patients. Guideline duration: minimum 14 days and up to 35 days, with THA at the longer end and TKA shorter. AAOS commentary (2026) explicitly frames aspirin as 'not one-size-fits-all' and risk-stratified. Net: the adherence window to model is 14-35 days, regimen- and procedure-dependent.

> Anderson DR et al. 'Aspirin or Rivaroxaban for VTE Prophylaxis after Hip or Knee Arthroplasty.' N Engl J Med 2018;378:699-707. https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1712746 ; CRISTAL trial commentary, ASH The Hematologist 2022. https://ashpublications.org/thehematologist/article/doi/10.1182/hem.V19.6.202264/486920/CRISTAL-Clear-Results-Enoxaparin-or-Aspirin-for ; 'Aspirin as Venous Thromboembolism Prophylaxis in Arthroplasty Patients: An Analysis of Clinical Practice Guidelines Recommendations.' J Arthroplasty 2024. https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(24)00522-9/fulltext

### Real-world post-discharge VTE prophylaxis non-adherence is about 17% (including 3.1% OVER-taking), and antibiotic prophylaxis has hard encodable constants
*[moderate]*

Post-discharge DVT prophylaxis adherence study: 17.0% of arthroplasty patients were non-adherent — 13.9% missed at least one dose and 3.1% took MORE medicine than prescribed (over-anticoagulation is a distinct bleeding-risk alert class, not an adherence miss). A large comparison (n=846) found no difference between DOAC (53% adherent) and LMWH injection (59% adherent) after THA/TKA; a smaller study (n=130) reported 97% adherence to rivaroxaban, 100% to dabigatran and 100% to fondaparinux — estimates are entirely dependent on measurement method. Route of delivery, cost and occupation did not significantly affect compliance. Antibiotic prophylaxis constants: cefazolin IV within 60 minutes of skin incision; weight-based dosing 1 g if under 80 kg, 2 g if 80-120 kg, 3 g if 120 kg or greater; discontinue within 24 hours of the procedure (1-day equals 3-day regimens for infection rate, and a single preoperative cefazolin dose equaled 1 day of cefuroxime). Routine extended postoperative oral antibiotics are not standard and remain contested.

> Sidhu V et al. 'Post-discharge patient-reported non-adherence to aspirin compared to enoxaparin for venous thromboembolism prophylaxis after hip or knee arthroplasty.' ANZ J Surg 2023. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ans.18284 ; 'Patient compliance with deep vein thrombosis prophylaxis after total hip and total knee arthroplasty.' https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6589531/ ; 'Antimicrobial Prophylaxis in Joint Arthroplasty.' US Pharmacist. https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/antimicrobial-prophylaxis-in-joint-arthroplasty

### Sleep disruption after TKA/THA is near-universal subjectively, but wearable TOTAL SLEEP TIME is a documented FAILURE as a pain proxy
*[moderate]*

Sleep disturbance occurs in more than 50% of THA and TKA patients. PSQI 'fairly bad' or 'very bad' sleep: 20.8% preoperatively, rising to 44.4% at 30 days, then 26.5% at 60 days and 20.2% at 90 days — subjective sleep is worst at about 1 month and normalizes by 90 days. Objective actigraphy/wearable studies consistently show reduced consolidated nocturnal sleep, increased fragmentation, decreased sleep efficiency and a shift toward daytime sleep in the immediate postoperative period with gradual recovery over days to weeks; deep and restorative sleep hours were significantly reduced in the first postoperative week and returned to baseline after that week. CRITICAL NEGATIVE RESULT (CCJR Gerard A. Engh Award paper, J Arthroplasty 2024, plus a separate 12-week Fitbit TKA study): recorded total sleep DURATION did not change from baseline at 30, 60 or 90 days and did NOT correlate with VAS pain at ANY timepoint, while subjective PSQI worsened at 30 days and improved beyond baseline at 90 days. Build any sleep-based pain proxy on fragmentation, efficiency, WASO, awakenings and daytime-nap shift — never on total sleep time.

> 'The CCJR Gerard A. Engh Excellence in Knee Research Award: Remote Monitoring of Sleep Disturbance Following Total Knee Arthroplasty: A Cautionary Note.' J Arthroplasty 2024. https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(24)00307-3/fulltext ; 'Sleep Disturbances Following Total Knee Arthroplasty.' J Arthroplasty 2023. https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(23)00086-4/fulltext ; 'Objective Sleep Disturbance After Total Hip and Knee Arthroplasty: A Systematic Review of Wearable-Derived Metrics and Targeted Interventions.' Arthroplast Today 2026. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352344126000622

### HRV-pain relationship exists but direction is inconsistent; multimodal analgesia is now the default standard and defines what 'expected medications' means
*[weak]*

HRV: a combined model using the Insomnia Severity Index plus HRV plus demographics predicted higher pain at PACU arrival with AUC 0.852 (Pain Practice 2024). Pre-surgical HRV indices inversely predicted post-operative pain, with high-frequency HRV the strongest single predictor, accounting for an additional 18% of variance. However other work found HIGHER parasympathetic indices (RMSSD, pNN50, HF) before end of surgery associated with HIGHER postoperative pain scores, and a 2022 scoping review concluded the link between postsurgical pain and HRV parameters is not well established. Since this engine already computes HRV RMSSD, an HRV-pain coupling feature is feasible but must be validated internally and not assumed directional. Multimodal analgesia: the AAHKS membership survey shows current standard components are NSAIDs (98%), acetaminophen (80%) and gabapentinoids (32%), plus regional nerve blocks and local infiltration analgesia; ERAS pathways report greater than 50% reductions in postoperative morphine equivalents with equal or better pain relief. This defines the medication list an adherence module should expect a TJA patient to be taking.

## Implications for backend

- Replace any single generic pain-decay curve with per-procedure expected NRS trajectories that explicitly model the rehab-transition rebound. Concrete seeds: TKA mean NRS 5.8 (POD1) -> 4.6 (POD8) -> 4.8 (POD9, rebound) -> 3.0 (POD29); THA 3.1 (POD1) -> 2.3 (POD8) -> 2.6 (POD9) -> 2.3 (POD12). Encode a per-procedure 'rebound window' (TKA/THA POD8-POD12) during which the deviation threshold widens, so the engine does not fire false alarms on physiologic rehab pain. ARCR should use a strictly monotone decay parameterized to recovery fractions of about 44% at 3 months, 70% at 6 months and 85% at 12 months rather than a 30-day curve.
- Compute and store two derived pain features that are individually predictive and fully deterministic (no LLM needed): (1) NRS slope over POD1-POD5 — shallower than -0.5 NRS/day is the published 'Moderate-High Persistent Pain' latent class (45.2% of TKA patients, intercept 6.96) versus -0.63/day for the rapid-relief class (54.8%, intercept 5.63); (2) days-to-PASS, the first day pain reaches 3 or below and stays there — benchmark POD2 after ARCR, with failure within week 1 flagging the high-catastrophizing phenotype.
- Anchor the chronic-pain risk feature to POD3, POD7, POD14 and POD30 specifically, the days with published association to chronic postsurgical pain after TKA, and down-weight POD0-2 which does not predict CPSP after THA. Encode the alert grammar in absolute terms too: severe 7-10, moderate 4-6, mild 0-3 (the categorization used by the AUC 0.88 wearable+EHR model), and treat a 2-point-or-greater NRS increase, or failure to achieve a 30% relative reduction week-over-week, as the clinically meaningful deviation (acute-pain MCID is roughly 1.5-2 NRS points or 30%).
- Build a first-class opioid module storing cumulative MME, MME/day, and days-since-last-opioid-dose. Encode conversions (oxycodone 5 mg = 7.5 OME; hydrocodone 5 mg = 5 OME) and per-procedure expected-consumption envelopes: TKA about 65 pills / 416 mg OME over 6 weeks (prescribed 88.4 pills / 632 mg OME); THA about 29.8 pills over 12 weeks; ACLR median 2 pills with 96% consuming 15 or fewer; ARCR about 90 MME; shoulder arthroplasty about 67.5 MME. Alert on consumption exceeding the procedure's upper envelope (Michigan OPEN caps: THA 0-30, TKA 0-40 oxycodone 5 mg tablets).
- Encode the weaning timeline as explicit day-indexed thresholds: median cessation POD30 (95% CI 29-31) after TJA; still using daily opioids at POD42 is a yellow flag; and any opioid fill in the 90-180 day post-op window in a previously opioid-naive patient is the formal 'new persistent opioid use' event (Brummett definition; base rate 5.9-6.5% vs 0.4% in non-surgical controls; pooled 26% TKA and 20% THA still on opioids at 3 months). Name the chosen definition in the data model so clinicians can audit it.
- Add a preoperative risk-modifier vector that SHIFTS the expected pain and opioid curves rather than generating alerts on its own: preoperative NRS (OR 3.546 for the persistent-pain class), PCS (OR 1.092 per point; thresholds 16 in knee OA, 20, 30), CSI at or above 40 (flagged as a fibromyalgia-derived cutoff), depression (OR 1.27-1.59; mild depression OR 4.11 in TJA), tobacco (OR 1.34-2.09), preoperative benzodiazepine (OR 1.59-4.83), preoperative opioid fill (OR up to 4.34), and discharge to inpatient rehab. The same factors give MME multipliers usable as priors: prior narcotic use +86%, benzodiazepine +81%, smoking +90%, tramadol-only +38%.
- Build a medication-adherence subsystem distinct from the existing 14-day activity adherence rate, with per-drug expected-duration windows. VTE prophylaxis spans 14-35 days post-op (THA long end, TKA short end) and must cover aspirin 81 mg daily or twice daily, rivaroxaban 10 mg daily, enoxaparin 40 mg daily, and the EPCAT II hybrid (rivaroxaban to POD5, then aspirin for 9 more days after TKA or 30 more days after THA). Alert on missed doses (baseline non-adherence 17.0%, with 13.9% missing at least one dose) AND separately on over-taking (3.1%, a bleeding-risk alert class). Model antibiotic prophylaxis as a perioperative event rather than a home medication: cefazolin within 60 minutes of incision, weight-tiered 1 g / 2 g / 3 g, discontinued within 24 hours.
- Do NOT use wearable total sleep time as a pain proxy. A CCJR-award TKA study and a 12-week Fitbit study both found total sleep duration unchanged from baseline at 30/60/90 days and uncorrelated with VAS at every timepoint. Derive fragmentation features instead: sleep efficiency, WASO, awakening count, deep-sleep fraction (reduced only in postoperative week 1, recovering after), and daytime-nap fraction. Expect subjective sleep to be worst at about 30 days (44.4% rating sleep bad versus 20.8% pre-op, normalizing to 20.2% at 90 days) — a 30-day trough is physiologic and must not alert.
- For any predictive model added, calibrate expectations to published ceilings and log the metric alongside each output so surgeons can see it: SORG-MLA prolonged opioid prescription after TKA AUROC 0.75 with AUPRC only 0.34 and Brier 0.029 versus null 0.032; Karhade lumbar-spine c-statistic 0.70; best wearable+EHR ensemble AUC 0.88 for severe acute pain (accuracy only 0.68) and 0.90 for chronic opioid use. Low AUPRC at low event rates means precision will be poor, so surface a calibrated probability with an explicit calibration statement rather than a bare binary.
- Make opioid regulatory limits a per-state configuration table rather than hardcoded CDC constants: the 2022 CDC guideline (MMWR RR-71(3)) removed absolute day-supply and MME/day caps, but roughly half of states retain statutory 7-day initial acute-pain limits and PBMs enforce their own. Keep 50 MME/day and 90 MME/day as advisory review triggers with provenance labels. This is a compliance surface that pairs naturally with the existing RTM CPT 98975/98977/98980/98981 billing module.
- Since the engine already computes HRV RMSSD and sleep duration, add an internal validation harness for an HRV-pain and sleep-fragmentation-pain coupling feature before shipping it. Published direction is inconsistent (HF-HRV inversely predicts postoperative pain and adds 18% of variance in one study; higher RMSSD/pNN50/HF associates with HIGHER pain in another; a scoping review calls the link unestablished), so the coupling coefficient must be learned from the product's own cohort and gated behind the existing coverage-based confidence gate.

## Open questions

- No published day-by-day NRS trajectory with means and SDs was found for ACL reconstruction, lumbar fusion/decompression, ankle or meniscus procedures at the granularity available for TKA and THA — the lumbar fusion literature reports only POD1-4 and 6-month timepoints. These curves may need to be constructed empirically from the product's own cohort or elicited via surgeon consensus, and should be labeled as lower-evidence in the UI.
- The magnitude and duration of the TKA/THA day-8-to-day-12 rehab rebound comes from two single-center cohorts (n=104 and n=103) under one standardized pain protocol; it has not been independently replicated at scale, and its size will vary with local rehab timing (inpatient rehab versus home PT versus same-day discharge). The rebound window should be a per-site configurable parameter, not a hardcoded constant.
- Whether a pain SPIKE (day-over-day increase) is more specific than an absolute NRS level for detecting infection, DVT, arthrofibrosis or wound complication is unresolved — no published sensitivity/specificity for a pain-delta-based alert in orthopedic RTM was found. This matters directly because the existing composite deviation index is intended as infection surveillance and pain would be the natural additional channel.
- Direction of the HRV-pain relationship is inconsistent across studies (HF-HRV inversely predicting pain and adding 18% of variance versus higher RMSSD/pNN50/HF associating with higher pain), and the supporting work is intraoperative or PACU-based in non-orthopedic populations. Whether home wearable RMSSD in post-op weeks 1-4 carries any pain signal at all in orthopedic patients is untested.
- The optimal CSI cutoff for orthopedic rather than fibromyalgia populations is actively contested; 2024-2025 papers propose alternative, generally lower thresholds for knee OA and osteotomy patients. Relatedly, the revision-TKA dissatisfaction OR of 39.081 is implausibly large and almost certainly reflects a small, highly selected sample, so it must not be used as a model coefficient.
- No source was located specifying a validated NRS threshold for triggering clinical escalation in a remote-monitoring product (for example 'NRS 7 or above for 2 consecutive days'). Escalation thresholds will therefore be product-defined and should be exposed as configurable parameters with explicit stated rationale, not presented as evidence-based.
- Non-adherence detection method for oral medications is unaddressed by the literature: published post-discharge VTE prophylaxis adherence ranges from 53% to 100% depending entirely on measurement method (self-report versus pill count versus pharmacy fill). Whether patient-reported medication logging in this product will be accurate enough to alert on is an empirical question needing internal validation.
- NSAID use after lumbar fusion and its relationship to nonunion/pseudarthrosis (dose- and duration-dependence, ketorolac in particular) could not be resolved within the search budget. This is directly relevant because a multimodal-analgesia adherence module would otherwise recommend scheduled NSAIDs to fusion patients — it needs a dedicated follow-up review before any NSAID guidance is surfaced.
- Michigan OPEN publishes tablet-count recommendations only for THA (0-30 oxycodone 5 mg) and TKA (0-40) among the procedures in scope; ACLR, rotator cuff repair, spine, ankle and meniscus numbers came from individual studies and expert-consensus panels that demonstrably overshoot measured consumption. A single authoritative per-procedure table covering the full procedure list does not exist and will have to be assembled from heterogeneous sources with per-row citations.
- The relationship between second-opioid-refill rate and downstream complications after arthroplasty — whether an early refill is a usable early-warning signal for infection or mechanical failure rather than merely uncontrolled pain — was not retrievable within budget and remains an open modeling question.
