# Extracting structured signal from patient conversation text and fusing it with wearable time series and tabular data (MedPull Recovery Copilot)

## Summary

For a 10-1000 patient post-op orthopedic RTM product with rare outcome labels, the right architecture is a three-layer text pipeline plus deliberately boring fusion. Layer 1 is a deterministic red-flag matcher (regex + medspaCy ConText for negation/uncertainty/experiencer) that runs on the raw utterance and can fire an escalation with no LLM in the loop; Layer 2 is an LLM extractor constrained to a Pydantic/JSON schema that emits value + certainty + verbatim evidence span per field (the exact prompt pattern that got 0.89 average accuracy on lung-cancer pathology extraction vs 0.51 for keyword search and 0.76 for BioClinicalBERT NER in npj Digital Medicine 2024); Layer 3 is a validation/abstention gate (schema retry, self-consistency over k=3 samples on red-flag fields only, evidence-span verification by string containment) that routes anything uncertain to a human. Full ontology pipelines (MedCAT, QuickUMLS, full UMLS) are over-engineered at your scale and carry a UMLS license burden — a curated ~200-400 concept ortho symptom dictionary with synonyms covers essentially all check-in vocabulary. Amazon Comprehend Medical is HIPAA-eligible and turnkey but costs $0.01 per 100 characters ($100/M chars), roughly 20-50x an equivalent Groq/Bedrock LLM call for the same text, and it does not produce your product's fields (pain NRS, PT completed, meds taken) — use it, if at all, only as an offline second opinion on a gold set. For fusion, do NOT build an end-to-end multimodal deep model: with single-digit-percent event rates and <1000 patients you will have 10-50 positive events, below the sample size where even GBDT is honest. Engineer text into ~10-15 scalar features, concatenate with wearable-derived features into one tabular row, and keep your existing deterministic composite index as the shipping model; hold a Firth-penalized logistic regression or a heavily-constrained LightGBM in reserve until you accumulate >=100 events. Published multimodal gains are real but modest (6.4% average accuracy increase across 128 studies; 6-33% AUC gains in HAIM at N=34,537) and were obtained at sample sizes 30-3000x yours.

## Findings

### LLM structured extraction with evidence-and-certainty JSON beats traditional clinical NLP by a wide margin — and the exact prompt schema is published
*[strong]*

Huang et al., npj Digital Medicine 2024 (PMC11063058) extracted TNM stage and histology from 774 lung cancer pathology reports with ChatGPT-3.5. Final prompt forced JSON with four components per attribute: extracted value, estimated value, degree of certainty, and supporting evidence; plus forced multiple-choice answer sets, an explicit 'Unknown' option, chain-of-thought intermediate questions (extract tumor size before assigning pT), and worked exemplars. Accuracy: pT 0.87, pN 0.91, overall stage 0.76, histology 0.99, average 0.89. Baselines on the same data: WordPiece keyword search 0.51, BioClinicalBERT NER 0.76. Validation on 191 pediatric osteosarcoma cases: grade 98.6%, margin 100%. GPT-4 was >5% better than GPT-3.5 on nearly all metrics. Reproducibility across two months: 0.913 equivalence rate, MAE 0.051 on the model's own confidence scores. Copy this four-field-per-attribute schema directly.

> Huang J. et al. A critical assessment of using ChatGPT for extracting structured data from clinical notes. npj Digit Med 2024. PMC11063058

### The dominant LLM failure mode is rule application and hallucination-on-empty-input, not entity recognition
*[strong]*

Same study: the model extracted the correct tumor size but applied the AJCC rule incorrectly (T1→T2 in 8.9% of cases); counted positive nodes instead of reading their anatomic location (N1→N2 in 32 cases); and propagated 22 of 56 stage errors from upstream fields. Critically, on blank reports and poor-quality scans it fabricated confident, deterministic (temperature=0) answers rather than returning unknown — only 5 of 15 missing-form cases correctly returned 'unknown'. Direct implication: never let the LLM compute a derived score or tier; have it extract atomic observations only and compute pain NRS deltas, red-flag logic, and tiers in Python. And explicitly test the empty/garbage/silence input path — an empty or off-topic check-in must not produce a plausible-looking pain score.

> PMC11063058 (error analysis section)

### Fine-tuned small encoder models still beat zero/few-shot GPT on nuanced clinical-text classification
*[strong]*

Guevara et al., npj Digital Medicine 2024 (PMC10781957) on social determinants of health in EHR notes: fine-tuned Flan-T5 XL reached macro-F1 0.71 for any-SDoH mention and 0.70 for adverse mentions, outperforming zero- and few-shot ChatGPT-family models in most scenarios. Read this as: LLMs win when you have no labels (your situation today); a small fine-tuned model wins once you have a few thousand labelled check-ins. Do not fine-tune anything before you have a gold set — but design the gold set now so it can become fine-tuning data later.

> Guevara M. et al. Large language models to identify social determinants of health in EHRs. npj Digit Med 2024. PMC10781957

### Prompting strategy matters more than model choice for extraction; heuristic prompts hit 0.94-0.96 zero-shot
*[moderate]*

Sivarajkumar et al., JMIR Medical Informatics 2024 (PMC11036183): GPT-3.5 zero-shot with heuristic prompts reached 0.96 accuracy on clinical sense disambiguation and 0.94 on biomedical evidence extraction; prompt ensembles capitalized on complementary strengths of different prompt styles. Practical read: build a small prompt-variant suite (heuristic/instruction, few-shot, CoT) and ensemble by majority vote on the red-flag booleans rather than reaching for a bigger model.

> Sivarajkumar S. et al. An empirical evaluation of prompting strategies for LLMs in zero-shot clinical NLP. JMIR Med Inform 2024. PMC11036183

### The clinical-LLM literature almost never measures calibration — you will have to build this yourself
*[strong]*

Bedi et al., JAMA 2025 (PMC11480901): systematic review of 519 healthcare LLM studies (Jan 2022-Feb 2024). 95.4% measured accuracy; only 1.2% measured calibration or uncertainty; only 4.6% addressed deployment; only 5% used real patient data. There is no off-the-shelf published calibration curve you can borrow for check-in extraction. Corollary: your abstention threshold must be tuned empirically on your own gold set, and reported per-field, not globally.

> Bedi S. et al. Testing and evaluation of health care applications of large language models. JAMA 2025. PMC11480901

### medspaCy ConText is the correct negation/uncertainty/experiencer layer, and it is free, local, deterministic and auditable
*[strong]*

medspaCy (Eyre et al., AMIA 2021, arXiv 2106.07799) implements the ConText algorithm on spaCy v3 (tested through 3.8.2), plus a clinical sentence splitter, rule-based TargetMatcher, sectionizer, and pre/post-processors. ConText assigns modifiers — NEGATED_EXISTENCE, POSSIBLE_EXISTENCE, HISTORICAL, FAMILY/experiencer, HYPOTHETICAL — with directionality and scope termination on conjunctions ('but', 'however', 'except'). Original NegEx (Chapman 2001) reported sensitivity 94.5%, specificity 77.8%, PPV 84.5% on discharge summaries; ConText (Harkema 2009) reported per-modifier F around 0.9 on several clinical report types. For patient-authored text ('no fever but the knee is hot', 'my mom had a clot') this single component removes the largest class of naive-keyword false positives. pip install medspacy.

> medspaCy GitHub + Eyre et al. AMIA 2021 (arXiv:2106.07799); Chapman et al. NegEx JBI 2001; Harkema et al. ConText JBI 2009

### Amazon Comprehend Medical is HIPAA-eligible but priced ~20-50x above an LLM call for the same text, and it does not emit your product's fields
*[strong]*

Verified pricing: 1 unit = 100 characters, 1-unit minimum per request. NERe (medical entity + relation extraction) $0.01/unit for the first 1M units, $0.005/unit for the second million, $0.001/unit above 2M. SNOMED CT linking $0.0075 / $0.00375 / $0.00075 per unit on the same tiers. ICD-10-CM, RxNorm, PHI detection also offered. Free tier: 85,000 units (8.5M characters) in month one. That is $100 per million characters at tier 1. A 500-character check-in costs $0.05 in NERe alone; 500 patients x 90 days = 45,000 check-ins ≈ $2,250, versus single-digit dollars for the same volume through a 70B-class LLM. AWS's HIPAA-eligible services reference explicitly lists 'Amazon Comprehend Medical', 'AWS Lambda', 'Amazon Translate', and 'Amazon Bedrock [excluding Fable and Mythos models]'; all require an executed BAA. Comprehend Medical also cannot give you pain NRS, PT-completed, or medication-adherence booleans — those are not entity types.

> https://aws.amazon.com/comprehend/medical/pricing/ ; https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/hipaa-eligible-services-reference/

### Full UMLS/SNOMED linking (MedCAT, QuickUMLS, scispaCy linker) is the wrong tool at your scale and adds a license burden
*[strong]*

MedCAT (Kraljevic et al., Artif Intell Med 2021) does self-supervised NER+L against SNOMED-CT/UMLS, trained on MIMIC-III with a ~35K-entity MedMentions base and ~800K-word Wiktionary vocabulary; MetaCAT adds negation/experiencer meta-annotations. It is Elastic License 2.0 and model packs require UMLS authentication via NIH (free but requires an accepted UMLS Metathesaurus License Agreement, annual renewal, and restricts redistribution). QuickUMLS uses SimString approximate matching, default Jaccard threshold 0.7, window 5 tokens, needs a local UMLS install and 5-30 minutes to build its index. Both are designed for millions of clinical notes with unbounded vocabulary. Your check-ins have bounded vocabulary: pain, swelling, redness, drainage, fever, chills, calf pain, shortness of breath, stiffness, numbness, falls, medications. A hand-curated 200-400 concept dictionary with lay synonyms ('gunk', 'weeping', 'charley horse', 'pins and needles') mapped to SNOMED codes will outperform any of these on your data and is fully auditable. Keep the SNOMED codes on your own concepts so you are interoperable later without running a linker.

> MedCAT GitHub (Elastic License 2.0, UMLS auth required); QuickUMLS GitHub (SimString, Jaccard 0.7)

### Constrained decoding guarantees valid JSON but can degrade reasoning — use reason-then-format, not raw grammar constraints
*[moderate]*

Outlines (dottxt-ai) enforces JSON Schema / Pydantic / regex / CFG at the token level across transformers, llama.cpp, vLLM, Ollama, OpenAI and Gemini backends, giving guaranteed-valid structure. Instructor (567-labs) takes the API-side approach: Pydantic model + field validators, automatic re-prompting with the validation error on failure (max_retries=3), streaming Partial[Model], and one from_provider() interface across OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini and Ollama. Published evidence (Tam et al., 'Let Me Speak Freely?', EMNLP Findings 2024) shows that hard format restriction degrades reasoning quality on some tasks relative to free-form generation followed by parsing. The reconciliation used in the pathology paper above is the right one: keep a free-text 'evidence' and 'reasoning' string field inside the schema, ordered before the typed fields, so the model reasons inside the JSON. Prefer Instructor for API-hosted models (Bedrock/Groq); Outlines only if you self-host.

> https://github.com/dottxt-ai/outlines ; https://github.com/567-labs/instructor ; Tam et al. EMNLP Findings 2024

### Multimodal fusion helps, but by single-digit percentages and only at sample sizes you do not have
*[strong]*

Kline et al., npj Digital Medicine 2022 (s41746-022-00712-8): scoping review of 128 articles, 2011-2021. Taxonomy — early/data-level (concatenate features, one classifier), intermediate/joint (per-modality encoders, merged at intermediate layers, jointly trained), late/decision-level (per-modality models, ensemble/vote). Early fusion was the most common. Studies comparing multimodal to unimodal reported an average increase of 6.4% in predictive accuracy. Most common pairing was imaging + EHR (n=52). The review's stated limitations map exactly onto your risks: single-site cohorts, small samples, poor handling of missing data and class imbalance, no deployment strategy. Soenksen et al., npj Digit Med 2022 (PMC9489871), the HAIM framework, fused tabular + time-series + text + images on MIMIC (N=34,537) and beat single-source models by 6-33%. Note the N: 34,537 patients for a 6-33% gain. At 10-1000 patients with 1-5% event rates you have 10-50 events; a fusion architecture cannot be validated there.

> Kline A. et al. npj Digit Med 2022, 5:171; Soenksen L. et al. npj Digit Med 2022. PMC9489871

### On small tabular clinical data, gradient-boosted trees beat deep learning; below ~100 events, penalized regression beats both
*[strong]*

Grinsztajn et al. (NeurIPS 2022 Datasets & Benchmarks, 45 datasets, truncated to 10k samples) and Shwartz-Ziv & Armon (Information Fusion 2022) both find tree ensembles outperform tuned deep tabular models on medium-sized tabular data, with far lower tuning cost. Below that regime, classical prediction-model sample-size rules bind: Peduzzi's events-per-variable heuristic (>=10 events per candidate predictor) and Riley et al. (Stat Med 2019) minimum-sample-size criteria imply that with 20 events you can honestly fit about 2 pre-specified predictors. Firth's penalized likelihood logistic regression (Heinze & Schemper) is the standard remedy for rare events and separation; Python implementation: firthlogist (pip install firthlogist), or statsmodels Logit with an L2 penalty plus bootstrap optimism correction. Do not report an AUC from a model with fewer than ~10 events per feature without a nested-CV / bootstrap-corrected estimate and a wide CI.

> Grinsztajn et al. NeurIPS 2022; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon, Information Fusion 2022; Riley et al. Stat Med 2019; Heinze & Schemper 2002

### Text embeddings as fusion features are a trap at your N — use a dozen engineered scalars instead
*[moderate]*

A 384-dim MiniLM or 768-dim BGE embedding concatenated to a tabular row with 30 patients and 8 events is guaranteed overfit; the model will memorize phrasing idiosyncrasies of individual patients. Correct approach at your scale: derive 10-15 interpretable scalars from the extraction JSON — pain_nrs, pain_nrs_delta_vs_7d_median, worst_pain_24h, swelling_ordinal(0-3), wound_appearance_ordinal, drainage_type(none/serous/purulent), fever_reported_bool, systemic_symptoms_count, pt_sessions_completed_ratio, opioid_doses_vs_prescribed, new_symptom_count, red_flag_count, distress_score, message_length_z, response_latency_hours. These are the same shape as your existing vitals features and drop straight into your composite index. Revisit embeddings only above ~300 events. If you must use text vectors earlier, project to <=3 dims with PCA fit on the training fold only.

> Design inference from Riley et al. 2019 sample-size criteria + Kline et al. 2022 limitations

### Sentiment/linguistic markers carry real but weak signal; treat them as context, never as a driver
*[moderate]*

ECLIPPSE (Schillinger et al., PLoS One 2019, PMC6386302): NLP-derived literacy profiles from 283,216 secure messages by 6,941 diabetes patients, C-statistics 0.61-0.74; patients flagged as limited-literacy had poorer medication adherence, worse glycemic control, more hypoglycemia and higher utilization. Longitudinal text-therapy study (JMIR 2021, PMC8319778, 10,000 patients): LIWC categories — emotional tone, pronoun rates, sadness/health/biology words — were strongly associated with PHQ-9 scores. Counter-evidence on naive sentiment: JAMA Netw Open 2022 (PMC9713605, 609 physicians, 1.45M inbasket messages) found no significant association between message sentiment scores and the outcome studied. Al-Mosaiwi & Johnstone (Clin Psychol Sci 2018) found absolutist-word frequency elevated in anxiety/depression/suicidal-ideation forums vs controls. Net: use a distress/affect score as a low-weight contextual feature and as a nudge to schedule a human call — not as an input to the clinical risk tier. LIWC-22 is commercially licensed; free alternatives are NRC-EmoLex, the empath library, and VADER (vaderSentiment, not clinically validated).

> Schillinger D. et al. PLoS One 2019 PMC6386302; JMIR 2021 PMC8319778; JAMA Netw Open 2022 PMC9713605; Al-Mosaiwi & Johnstone 2018

### Realistic inter-annotator agreement targets and gold-set sizes for clinical NLP are published and achievable
*[strong]*

Reported IAA in comparable clinical annotation projects: named entity annotation 0.697-0.750 agreement in a comprehensive JAMIA syntactic/semantic corpus (PMC3756257) — i.e. entity spans are HARD; medication names 0.87 F-measure, medication types 0.73, attribute linking 0.96 on 1,042 double-annotated clinical trial announcements (JMIR 2013, PMC3636329); mobility-functioning extraction achieved 92.3% exact-mention F1 and Cohen's kappa 96.6% on attributes over 400 notes / 14,281 mentions (Int J Med Inform 2021, PMC8104034); an Italian cardiology event corpus used just 75 documents (60 train / 15 test) with 4,365 event mentions and published its guidelines (PMC6948016). Practical targets for you: whole-field values (pain NRS, PT completed, red-flag boolean) should reach kappa >= 0.80; free-span symptom mentions realistically land at F1 0.70-0.85 and you should not treat span mismatch as a model failure if the normalized concept matches. Publish your annotation guideline document alongside the gold set.

> PMC3756257; PMC3636329; PMC8104034; PMC6948016

### Post-op orthopedic red-flag content: the deterministic checklist should be built from NHSN SSI criteria, VTE decision rules, and compartment/neurovascular signs — none of which should be delegated to a model
*[moderate]*

CDC NHSN surgical site infection surveillance defines superficial incisional, deep incisional, and organ/space SSI using explicit sign/symptom criteria — purulent drainage, localized pain or tenderness, localized swelling, erythema, heat, fever >38.0C, deliberate opening or spontaneous dehiscence — within a 30-day surveillance window for most procedures and a 90-day window for implant procedures including HPRO (hip prosthesis) and KPRO (knee prosthesis). VTE screening should mirror Wells DVT items (unilateral calf swelling with >3 cm circumference difference, localized deep-vein tenderness, pitting edema confined to symptomatic leg) and PE presentation (new dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, hemoptysis, syncope, tachycardia). Compartment syndrome: pain out of proportion, pain on passive stretch, paresthesia, tense compartment. Neurovascular: new foot drop, cold/pale/pulseless limb, saddle or progressive numbness. Mechanical: inability to bear weight after a pop or fall, sudden leg-length or rotation change (dislocation). Arthrofibrosis/MUA: TKA flexion stalling below ~90 degrees at 6 weeks. Medication: opioid oversedation, anticoagulant-associated bleeding. Encode each as a boolean over normalized extracted concepts plus a severity/duration qualifier, with a fixed priority order and a hard-coded escalation action — never as an LLM judgment. Note: I could not retrieve the CDC NHSN PDF in this session (HTTP 403); verify the exact criterion wording and windows against the current chapter before encoding.

> CDC NHSN Patient Safety Component Manual, Chapter 9 (SSI) — not fetched this session; Wells criteria for DVT/PE

### Published evidence for chatbot-based post-op orthopedic triage is thin — you are ahead of the literature and must self-validate
*[moderate]*

A targeted Europe PMC query for conversational agents in postoperative orthopedic triage returned no papers reporting symptom item sets, escalation rules, completion rates, or accuracy/safety outcomes for arthroplasty follow-up. The nearest evidence is virtual arthroplasty follow-up without a conversational agent: Platt & Bolland 2025 (Eur J Orthop Surg Traumatol, PMC12380640) managed 92.2% of total hip replacement patients remotely with 86% satisfaction, using Oxford Hip Scores and radiographs — but no systematic red-flag criteria. Implication: there is no external benchmark to point at. Your safety argument has to rest on your own prospectively-collected gold set and a documented sensitivity floor for red-flag detection, plus a designed fail-open path (uncertain -> human, never uncertain -> reassure).

> Europe PMC search, July 2026; Platt NA, Bolland B. Eur J Orthop Surg Traumatol 2025. PMC12380640

### Self-consistency and semantic-entropy give you a usable confidence signal when logprobs are unavailable
*[strong]*

Wang et al. (ICLR 2023) self-consistency: sample k reasoning paths at temperature ~0.5-0.7, marginalize by majority vote over the final answer; consistently improves accuracy over greedy decoding on reasoning tasks. Farquhar et al., Nature 2024 (PMC11186750) semantic entropy: cluster k sampled generations by bidirectional entailment and compute entropy over meaning-clusters rather than token sequences; detects confabulations without task-specific training data or labels. For a discrete extraction schema, semantic clustering collapses to exact-value matching, which makes this cheap: sample k=3-5 completions, compute per-field agreement rate, and treat agreement < 1.0 on any red-flag boolean as an abstention trigger. This matters practically because the Anthropic API does not expose token logprobs, so logprob-based confidence is not available on a Bedrock-Claude path.

> Wang X. et al. Self-Consistency, ICLR 2023; Farquhar S. et al. Nature 2024. PMC11186750


## Implications for backend

- Add a three-layer text module under app/engine that mirrors the existing vitals engine's shape: engine/text/rules.py (medspaCy ConText + curated lexicon, deterministic, unit-tested), engine/text/extract.py (Instructor + Pydantic schema against Bedrock/Groq), engine/text/gate.py (abstention triggers). The LLM must never compute a number that feeds the tier — it emits atomic observations with evidence spans, and Python computes pain deltas, red-flag booleans, and the composite index. This preserves the existing invariant that 'an LLM writes narrative only, never numbers' and extends it to 'an LLM extracts observations, never scores'.
- Compliance is the gating item, not accuracy. Bedrock is HIPAA-eligible under BAA (excluding Fable and Mythos models) and Lambda/Translate/Comprehend Medical are too — but the current memory note says the LLM chain is Groq-first. Sending patient check-in text to Groq is PHI disclosure and requires an executed BAA or de-identification first. Either move the extraction call to Bedrock, or add a de-identification step (Comprehend Medical DetectPHI, or a local regex+NER scrubber) before any non-BAA provider, and gate it in code so the Groq path physically cannot receive raw text. Also note Anthropic's API does not expose logprobs, so build confidence from self-consistency sampling, not logprobs.
- Persist the extraction as an immutable, versioned artifact: raw transcript, schema_version, prompt_version, model_id, dictionary_version, per-field value + certainty + evidence span, retries_used, self_consistency_agreement, and needs_human_review. Without prompt_version and model_id on every row you cannot explain why a patient's pain trend shifted when you edited a prompt, and you cannot run the drift canary. SQLite handles this fine at 1000 patients; the JSON blob column is the natural shape and migrates cleanly to Postgres jsonb later.
- Extend the existing coverage-confidence gate to text. Today the gate is >=3 of 6 metrics present per day and low if <40% of the last 7 days. Text needs its own arm: a check-in within 48h, extraction not abstained, and >=N key fields non-unknown. If the text arm fails, text features contribute zero weight to the composite index rather than contributing a default — a missing check-in must never read as 'no pain'. Surface the text coverage separately in the console so a clinician can see whether the number is thin.
- Do not build or promise a learned multimodal risk model in the current release. With 10-1000 patients and single-digit-percent event rates you will have 10-50 events; that is below the sample size where any fusion model is identifiable. Ship the deterministic composite index with clinician-set weights and the deterministic red-flag ladder, and write the sample-size trigger into the roadmap explicitly (>=40 events -> Firth logistic on 3-5 features; >=100-200 events -> constrained LightGBM with isotonic calibration). This is also the honest answer to any investor or clinician who asks whether the model is 'validated'.
- Budget the text pipeline as an LLM cost, not a Comprehend Medical cost. Comprehend Medical NERe is $0.01 per 100 characters ($100/M chars); a 500-char check-in is $0.05, so 500 patients x 90 days is roughly $2,250 versus single-digit dollars for the same text through a 70B-class model. Reserve Comprehend Medical for offline second-opinion runs on the gold set and for DetectPHI in the de-identification path, where its HIPAA eligibility and turnkey nature actually earn the price.
- Build the gold set and the pytest regression fixture in the same PR as the extractor, not after. Pin 200 double-annotated check-ins as a fixture the way golden tiers are already pinned; make red-flag recall and human-review rate assertions part of CI so no prompt edit can silently degrade safety. Add a monthly 30-item live canary for drift — the pathology study saw 0.913 month-to-month equivalence on a frozen model/prompt, which means ~9% of extractions can change under you with no code change at all.

## Recommended stack

- **Deterministic red-flag screening on every check-in utterance (safety layer, runs before and independently of the LLM)** — Rule-based target matching with ConText negation/uncertainty/experiencer scoping over a curated ortho symptom lexicon; fixed-priority escalation ladder emitting typed reason codes into your existing tier engine via `medspacy>=1.3.1 (pip install medspacy) on spacy>=3.7,<3.9 with en_core_web_sm; plus plain Python regex for numerics (temperature, NRS, dose counts). No UMLS license required.`
  - params: medspacy.load(enable=['medspacy_pyrush','medspacy_target_matcher','medspacy_context']). ConText: default rule set plus custom modifiers for lay speech — negation triggers ('no','none','nothing','haven't had','denied','went away','not anymore'), uncertainty ('maybe','kind of','a little','I think','not sure','might be'), experiencer ('my mom','my husband','my friend'), historical ('before surgery','last year','pre-op'). max_scope=None but termination on {'but','however','except','although','though'}; use_context_window=False. TargetMatcher rules as (literal, category, pattern) triples over ~250 curated concepts, each carrying a SNOMED CT code. Escalation ladder priority: (1) PE/compartment/neurovascular -> immediate; (2) SSI/PJI per NHSN signs (purulent drainage OR fever>=38.0C with wound signs OR dehiscence OR spreading erythema OR new pain increase after POD5) -> same-day; (3) DVT (unilateral calf pain+swelling) -> same-day; (4) fall/dislocation/inability to bear weight -> same-day; (5) opioid oversedation or anticoagulant bleeding -> same-day; (6) ROM stall (TKA flexion <90 at 6wk) -> routine PT escalation. Fire on ANY match that is not NEGATED, not FAMILY, not HISTORICAL, not HYPOTHETICAL.
  - needs: Zero labelled patients to build. Needs ~150-250 real or realistically-simulated check-in utterances (including adversarial phrasings and negations) to tune the lexicon, and a 200-utterance held-out gold set to measure recall. Target red-flag recall >=0.95 with an explicit accepted false-positive rate.
  - why: Recall-first, fully auditable, zero inference cost, no PHI leaves the process, and it cannot hallucinate. medspaCy's ConText is the descendant of NegEx (sens 94.5% / spec 77.8% / PPV 84.5% on discharge summaries) and removes the dominant naive-keyword false-positive class. Because it is deterministic you can unit-test every rule and pin the tests, which is how you defend the safety claim to a clinician.
- **Converting a free-text conversational check-in into the structured comparable record (pain NRS, swelling, wound appearance, meds taken, PT completed, new symptoms, red flags)** — Schema-constrained LLM extraction with per-field {value, certainty, evidence_span} objects and mandatory 'unknown' option; validator-driven retry; self-consistency vote on safety-critical booleans only via `instructor (pip install instructor) + pydantic>=2.7, over anthropic-bedrock (Amazon Bedrock is HIPAA-eligible under BAA, excluding Fable and Mythos models) as the primary path, with Groq as fallback ONLY if a BAA is in place or the text is de-identified. Do not use Outlines unless you self-host weights.`
  - params: temperature=0.0 for the primary pass; max_retries=3 with Pydantic field_validators that reject out-of-range NRS, evidence spans not found verbatim in the source (substring check after whitespace normalization), and any field marked present without an evidence span. Schema field order: reasoning:str first, then per-attribute objects. Numeric fields: pain_rest_nrs / pain_activity_nrs / worst_pain_24h as Optional[conint(ge=0,le=10)]; swelling and wound_appearance as Literal ordinals; pt_completed as Literal['none','partial','full','unknown']; meds as list of {name, taken:bool, doses:int}; new_symptoms as list of curated concept enums plus a free_text_other; red_flags as list of enum. Every attribute also carries certainty:Literal['high','medium','low'] and evidence:str. Self-consistency: for red_flags and pain fields only, resample k=3 at temperature=0.3 and require unanimity; any disagreement sets needs_human_review=True. Prompt must include: forced multiple-choice answer sets, explicit 'if the patient did not say it, answer unknown — do NOT infer', 2-4 worked exemplars including one empty/off-topic message that must return all-unknown, and an instruction never to compute derived scores.
  - needs: Zero labelled patients to start (zero-shot). To claim per-field accuracy you need a gold set of ~200 double-annotated check-ins, stratified 100 random + 100 enriched for red flags and negations. To detect a per-field accuracy of 0.90 with a +/-0.05 half-width 95% CI you need ~140 items per field; 200 covers the common fields and under-powers the rare ones, which is acceptable if you report the CIs honestly.
  - why: This is the exact prompt architecture that produced 0.89 average accuracy vs 0.51 (keyword) and 0.76 (BioClinicalBERT NER) in npj Digital Medicine 2024, with 0.913 month-to-month reproducibility. The evidence-span requirement plus substring validation converts the hardest failure mode (confident fabrication on empty input — only 5/15 handled correctly in that study) into a mechanically detectable error. Instructor's validator-retry loop means malformed output never reaches your database.

- **Confidence, abstention, and human-in-the-loop routing for the extraction pipeline** — Multi-signal abstention: schema-validation failures, evidence-span verification failure, self-consistency disagreement, ConText ambiguity, and OOV concepts — any one triggers human review; nothing autonomously reassures a patient via `instructor validators + a small Python rule module; optional semantic-entropy style clustering over k samples (exact-match clustering suffices for discrete schemas)`

Semantic entropy (Nature 2024) shows sampling-based disagreement detects confabulation without labels, which is exactly the regime you are in. Anthropic's API does not expose token logprobs, so sampling-based agreement — not logprob confidence — is the available mechanism on a Bedrock-Claude path.
