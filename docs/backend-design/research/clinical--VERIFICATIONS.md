### Added detail
- Post-episode spending guardrail (42 CFR 512.550(f)): if a participant's average 30-day post-episode spending exceeds 3 standard deviations above the regional average, the excess is subtracted from the NPRA and is NOT protected by stop-loss/stop-gain limits — hospitals cannot game the 30-day window by pushing costs to day 31+; a monitoring platform's day-31-60 utilization data is still financially relevant
- Episode start quirk (42 CFR 512.525): if an anchor hospitalization begins the same day as or within 3 days after an outpatient procedure of the same episode category, the episode start date is the outpatient procedure date — matters for correctly timestamping episode day 0 in analytics
- PYs 2-5 default track assignment is Track 3; hospitals must affirmatively request and receive CMS approval for Track 1 (safety-net only, through PY3) or Track 2 (specified hospital types) before each performance year — 42 CFR 512.520(b)
- The TEAM regulations were amended by the FY2026 IPPS final rule effective Aug 4, 2025 (90 FR 37204-37207) — any product logic built from FY2025-final-rule summaries (especially the quality measure set and target-price sections 512.540/512.545/512.550) must be rebuilt from the amended text
- CQS scoring method (512.547(b)): raw measure scores are converted to scaled scores by percentile against a national cohort (TEAM + non-TEAM hospitals) in a measure-specific CQS baseline period (CY2025 for HWR/PSI-90/PRO-PM; CY2026 for the PY2 harm measures; CY2027 for Information Transfer PRO-PM) — it is percentile benchmarking, not fixed point weights

- **CLAIM:** Claim 3: 'Class C1 ... 45.16% (n=101)' and 'practical rule: a slope shallower than about -0.5 NRS/day over POD1-5 marks the high-risk class'
  - **CORRECTION:** All the model parameters check out verbatim against the Frontiers full text (intercepts 6.956/5.631, slopes -0.494/-0.631, posteriors 0.980/0.972, all four ORs and CIs, beta=0.32/t=5.64/6.2% variance), but two things are wrong. ### Added detail
- The 39.081 OR in claim 7 is even weaker than the researcher's own caveat suggests: 95% CI 6.926-220.504 (a 32-fold span) from n=68 total — 20 in the CS group vs 48 controls, with 15% satisfaction in the CS group vs 91.7% in controls. With cell counts that small the point estimate is meaningless; if you surface it at all, surface the CI, not the OR.
- The TKA and THA cohorts in claims 1 and 2 are both n=103 from the same Regensburg group under the same standardized pain protocol. That is convenient for a head-to-head offset, but they are two separate nonrandomized single-center cohorts, not a comparative trial. The offset the researcher rounds to '~2.5 NRS points' is actually 2.7 at POD1 and 2.3 at POD8 — it is not constant across the curve, so a single additive procedure offset will be wrong at the ends.
- Claim 3's classes are 45%/55% — this is not a rare high-risk tail. Any alerting rule built on 'C1 membership' will flag nearly half your TKA population. The clinically actionable framing is the predictor set (preop NRS is by far the strongest, OR 3.5), not class membership itself.
- Claim 17 undersells one positive finding: patient-reported sleep DID track pain. Patients reporting 'very bad' sleep had significantly worse VAS than those reporting 'bad' sleep at 30 days. So the correct build is: drop objective total sleep time as a pain proxy (confirmed dead), but keep subjective sleep-quality items — they carry signal that the wearable duration metric does not.
- Two 2026 papers now govern the wearable-sleep question and neither is cited: Gonzalez et al., J Arthroplasty 2026;41(S1):S66-S73 (prospective wearable sleep patterns in TKA) and Ferrero et al., Arthroplast Today 2026;39:102013 (systematic review of wearable-derived sleep metrics and targeted interventions after THA/TKA). Read the systematic review before choosing which fragmentation/efficiency/WASO metrics to compute.
- Related and newer than claim 13(c): Soley N, Rattsev I, Speed TJ, et al., 'Predicting postoperative chronic opioid use with fair machine learning models integrating multi-modal data sources,' J Am Med Inform Assoc 2025 (PMID 40152140) — same group, adds fairness auditing, which matters for a clinical product.
