# Modelling post-operative orthopedic recovery trajectories and detecting individual deviation from them: GBTM/LCGA, growth mixture models, FPCA, non-linear hierarchical growth curves, centile/quantile reference curves (LMS/GAMLSS), time-warping, and landmark prediction — with Python implementations sized for 10–1000 patients and rare outcome labels

## Summary

The published orthopedic trajectory literature is remarkably consistent: recovery after knee/hip arthroplasty splits into roughly 3 latent classes, with a poor/low class comprising 14–40% of patients depending on the outcome. The single most transferable study for MedPull is Olanrewaju et al. (Rheumatology International 2026, n=82, wrist accelerometer, weekly grid): latent class growth analysis on step counts found high (37%: 991 steps at week 1 → 6,606 at week 6), moderate (29%: 360 → 3,739) and low (34%: 29 → 1,452) recovery classes, with only 32% of patients exceeding their pre-operative step count by week 6, and class membership driven overwhelmingly by pre-operative step count and procedure (UKR vs TKA). That result directly indicts an absolute-scale hand-tuned expected curve: the correct scale is ratio-to-own-pre-op-baseline, and pre-op step count must be a covariate in the expected curve, not a nuisance. On methods, the honest answer at n=10–1000 is that GBTM/GMM are inappropriate as a production engine (they need ~200–2,400 subjects and reliably recover only 2–3 classes; 4+ classes are enumeration artifacts), and full LMS/GAMLSS centile charts of the kind used for growth references need far more data than we have — Cole (Stat Methods Med Res 2021) shows optimally designed growth-reference studies need 7,000–25,000 subjects per sex to hit SE = 0.041 z for the median and 0.066 z for the 2nd/98th centiles. The scale-appropriate replacement for the hand-tuned logistic f(d) is a Bayesian hierarchical non-linear growth curve (Mitscherlich/asymptotic-regression, 3 parameters, non-centred, Student-t likelihood) fitted in PyMC with the current hand-tuned parameters used as the prior mean — this works from ~20–30 patients per procedure, yields posterior predictive intervals rather than a naked point estimate, and degrades gracefully. Once a procedure reaches ~100–150 patients, switch that procedure to ExpectileGAM quantile curves (pyGAM 0.12.0, `fit_quantile`) at the 10/25/50/75/90th centiles — never the 2nd/98th, which are unidentifiable below ~500 patients. Crucially, the current engine compares patients against a point estimate with no uncertainty at all; the correct comparand is a prediction interval / marginal centile (which does not shrink with n), not a confidence interval on the mean curve (which does), and any deviation flag must be suppressed when the deviation is smaller than the expected curve's own standard error. FPCA on smoothed 0–90-day curves (scikit-fda 0.10.1) followed by a 2–3 component Gaussian mixture on the first 2–3 scores is the small-data substitute for LCGA and has published precedent at n=129 (Yakdan, Spine Journal 2025: FPCA on Fitbit metrics in the first post-op month, poor pain-recovery cluster had 23% vs 7% complication rate). Supervised outcome models against infection/readmission/MUA are simply not fittable at single-digit event percentages and n<500 — log the labels, ship the unsupervised centile signal, and revisit.

## Findings

### Wearable step-count recovery after knee replacement has three published LCGA classes with concrete weekly values
*[strong]*

Olanrewaju et al., Rheumatology International 2026;46(6), DOI 10.1007/s00296-026-06135-y. n=82 (aged 42–89, 52% women), wrist-worn accelerometer, 1 week pre-op + 6 weeks post-op, weekly grid, TKA and UKR. Latent class growth analysis fitted separately to ABSOLUTE and RELATIVE (post-op:pre-op ratio) step counts; three classes each. Absolute: high recovery n=30 (37%) median 991 steps [IQR 1,335] at week 1 → 6,606 [IQR 2,940] at week 6; moderate n=24 (29%) 360 [481] → 3,739 [1,918]; low n=28 (34%) 29 [176] → 1,452 [1,504]. Relative: high n=39 (47%) reached 92% [IQR 29%] of pre-op by week 6; moderate n=26 (32%) 64% [25%]; low n=17 (21%) 31% [7%]. Only 32% of the whole cohort exceeded pre-op step count within 6 weeks. High-trajectory membership predicted by higher pre-operative step count and UKR (vs TKA) via multinomial logistic regression; high-trajectory patients had less pain, better OKS and better EQ-5D at 6 weeks (Kruskal–Wallis + Dunn). Failure mode of naive use: these are medians of a 6-week window only, from one UK cohort, n=82 — treat as a prior, not a reference standard.

> Olanrewaju et al., Rheumatol Int 2026;46(6). DOI 10.1007/s00296-026-06135-y

### Oxford Knee Score after TKA follows three GBTM classes: poor 14.0%, modest 39.1%, good 46.9%
*[strong]*

Hamilton DF, Shim J, Howie CR, Macfarlane GJ. 'Patients follow three distinct outcome trajectories following total knee arthroplasty.' Bone Joint J 2021, PMID 34058868. n=731, secondary analysis of a UK multicentre TKA longitudinal cohort. Group-based trajectory modelling (Nagin) of OKS at FIVE timepoints in the first post-operative year. Classes: Poor 14.0%, Modest 39.1%, Good 46.9%. Predictors of poor-responder membership were few and psychosocial: pre-operative expectations of pain and limitation, coping strategies, and lower baseline physical health status. Explicit motivation: ~20% dissatisfaction is invisible to dichotomous good/bad outcome models.

> Hamilton DF et al., Bone Joint J 2021, PMID 34058868

### Early (6-month) WOMAC trajectory class predicts 24-month outcome — the first 6 months are the actionable window
*[strong]*

Yang HY, Losina E, Lange JK, Katz JN, Collins JE. 'Longitudinal Trajectories of Pain and Function Improvement Following Total Knee Replacement.' ACR Open Rheumatol 2019, PMID 31777807. n=107. GBTM on WOMAC pain and WOMAC function (0–100, higher = worse) over the first 6 months; TWO classes each (optimal vs suboptimal improvement). Suboptimal-improvement membership at 6 months predicted higher pain, higher functional disability and lower satisfaction at 24 months. Predictors of suboptimal class: greater pain catastrophizing, worse mental health status, pre-surgical use of supportive devices. Note the small n=107 supported only 2 classes — a direct demonstration that class count is sample-size-bound.

> Yang HY et al., ACR Open Rheumatol 2019, PMID 31777807

### Acute post-op pain after knee arthroplasty splits ~45/55 into persistent vs rapid-relief classes on a days-1–5 grid
*[moderate]*

Wen et al., Frontiers in Pain Research 2025 (10.3389/fpain.2025.1659917). n=227. Growth mixture modelling of VAS pain on post-operative days 1, 2, 3, 5, with 3-month functional follow-up. Two classes: 'moderate-high persistent pain' 45.16%, 'moderate-low rapid relief' 54.84%. Predictors of the persistent class: age, elevated pre-operative pain, pain catastrophizing, lower family care index. Relevant because it shows the useful discrimination window for pain is the first post-op WEEK at daily resolution — matching MedPull's conversational check-in cadence.

> Wen et al., Front Pain Res 2025;6:1659917

### Cautionary counter-example: dense PROM data can yield an essentially unimodal 95/5 split, not meaningful classes
*[moderate]*

Omran et al., JAAOS Global Res Rev 2025. n=700 knee arthroplasty patients, KOOS-JR at pre-op, 1, 3, 6, 12 months. Growth mixture modelling found only TWO classes: 'steady improvement' 95.4% and a small low-baseline class 4.6% that converged to the main class by 1 month. Explicit finding: 'no recovery trajectory shows postoperative decline or stagnation.' Implication: PROM trajectories are far less heterogeneous than wearable step trajectories (compare the 37/29/34 split above). If MedPull runs trajectory clustering, run it on wearable/objective streams, not on PROMs — PROM clustering will produce a degenerate solution and a false sense of stratification.

> Omran et al., JAAOS Glob Res Rev 2025

### Published normative wearable recovery values after TKA exist at n=566 and are sex- and BMI-dependent
*[strong]*

'Normative Values for Daily Functional Recovery Following Total Knee Arthroplasty', J Arthroplasty 2024 (S0883-5403(24)00543-6). n=566 (345 women, 221 men; mean age 65–69), first 6 weeks post-TKA. Greatest gains in ALL metrics occurred in the first 3 weeks. Men had greater total daily step counts and cadence than women; obese patients performed worse than lower-BMI patients. Direct consequence for MedPull: an expected curve conditioned only on procedure and day is misspecified — sex, BMI and pre-op activity are first-order covariates and must enter the centile model, otherwise a heavy female patient is systematically flagged.

> J Arthroplasty 2024, ScienceDirect S0883540324005436

### ACLR activity trajectories show that deviation ABOVE the expected curve is not automatically benign
*[moderate]*

'Physical Activity Trajectories in the First Year following ACL Reconstruction and Links to Early Markers of Osteoarthritis Development', Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026. Accelerometry at 2, 4, 6 and 12 months post-ACLR. Two MVPA classes: 'consistent MVPA' 74.5% and 'high-increasing MVPA' 25.5%. The high-increasing class showed significantly greater ΔT1ρ in the lateral femoral condyle (P=0.006), i.e. deleterious early cartilage compositional change. Failure mode this exposes in MedPull's current design: a one-sided 'ratio-to-expected below threshold' rule treats over-performance as safe. For ACL and cartilage-loading procedures the alert should be two-sided with an asymmetric upper threshold.

> Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026, physical activity trajectories post-ACLR

### Cole 2021 is the definitive sample-size anchor for centile curves — and it says growth-chart-grade centiles need 7,000–25,000 subjects
*[strong]*

Cole TJ, 'Sample size and sample composition for constructing growth reference centiles', Stat Methods Med Res 2021, DOI 10.1177/0962280220958438. GAMLSS fitted to weight, height, BMI and head circumference in 6,878 boys aged 0–21 (Fourth Dutch Growth Study); precision explored by refitting GAMLSS to simulated data across sample sizes and age compositions. Achieved precision on the z-score scale: SE = 0.041 z for the median, 0.066 z for the 2nd and 98th centiles. Two load-bearing results: (1) 'the process of smoothing the centiles increased the notional sample size two- to threefold by borrowing strength'; (2) optimal sample composition is uniform on age^λ with λ=0.4 for the median but λ=0.75 for extreme centiles — i.e. the median curve wants heavy over-sampling of the early period, the tails want less. Conclusion: 'optimally designed studies need 7000–25000 subjects per sex.' Read correctly for MedPull: our domain is 0–90 days not 0–21 years and we want 5 inner centiles not 2nd/98th, so the requirement is far lower — but the 2nd/98th centile is off the table for us, permanently, at our scale.

> Cole TJ, Stat Methods Med Res 2021;30(10). DOI 10.1177/0962280220958438

### Non-parametric reference limits need n>120 and often 500 — the classical clinical-chemistry floor
*[strong]*

'Centile charts II: alternative nonparametric approach for establishing time-specific reference centiles and assessment of the sample size required', Clinical Chemistry 2004. Finding: sample size 'needs to be larger than 120' for adequate precision of time-specific reference centiles, and 'threshold sample sizes may need to be as high as 500' when parametric model assumptions are uncertain. This aligns with CLSI EP28-A3c's n≥120 minimum for a non-parametric 95% reference interval. Practical consequence: below ~120 patients per procedure, do not compute empirical percentiles day-by-day; you must borrow strength across days (smoothing) and across procedures (hierarchical pooling), or you are reporting noise.

> Clinical Chemistry 2004, 'Centile charts II'

### GBTM/GMM reliably recover only 2–3 classes and typically need 200–2,400 subjects — they are a research tool, not a production engine at our scale
*[strong]*

Synthesis across the retrieved simulation and applied literature: published GBTM/LCGA/GMM studies cluster at n=200–2,423 with 3–4 timepoints (e.g. hip-fracture motor recovery GBTM, Geriatrics 2025, n=2,423 at days 0/7/28 → rapid 26.7%, intermediate 32.6%, poor/flat 40.7%; PTSD trajectories n=439 → resilient 74%, recovering 23%, chronic 4%; opioid GBTM n=8,785 → 5 classes 32/29/25/8/6%). 'On the Use of Elbow Plot Method for Class Enumeration', Appl Psychol Meas 2025: enumeration detected the generating model ≥90% of the time for two- and three-class models but 'did not perform well for two-factor and four-class conditions.' Accepted quality gates in the applied literature: entropy ≥0.80, average posterior probability of assignment ≥0.7 per class, minimum class size ≥5%, BIC/AIC comparison. Documented pitfalls (Rizk & Qato, J Manag Care Spec Pharm 2026): spurious classes from non-normality, arbitrary time-scale choice, selection bias from restrictive enrolment windows, immortal-time bias, reverse causality, and irreproducibility from undocumented model-selection decisions. Verdict for MedPull: at 10–1000 patients, do NOT ship GBTM. Use it offline, once, on the largest procedure, to decide how many classes exist — and expect the answer to be 2 or 3.

> Appl Psychol Meas 2025 (elbow plot enumeration); Rizk & Qato, J Manag Care Spec Pharm 2026; Geriatrics 2025 hip fracture GBTM

### FPCA on wearable recovery curves works at n=129 and beat conventional assessment for 12-month prognosis
*[strong]*

Yakdan S et al., 'Multidomain Postoperative Recovery Trajectories After Lumbar and Thoracolumbar Spine Surgery', The Spine Journal 2025;25(12):2739–2752. n=129 (median age 62, 56% female). Functional principal component analysis used to cluster early post-operative recovery patterns from ecological momentary assessment surveys AND Fitbit data (steps/minute, maximum 30-minute cadence, activity bout number) over the FIRST MONTH after surgery. 'Two dominant recovery patterns were identified across all domains.' Patients with poor pain recovery had a 23% complication rate vs 7% in the favourable cluster; the count of favourable domains had greater prognostic value for 12-month PROs than traditional assessment. This is the closest published methodological analogue to MedPull's situation — small n, wearable + EMA, first-month window — and it validates FPCA-then-cluster over GBTM at this scale.

> Yakdan S et al., Spine J 2025;25(12):2739-2752

### Distributional/functional summaries of step data outperform mean daily steps as a digital biomarker
*[moderate]*

Matabuena M et al., 'Smartphone-based step counts as digital biomarker for survival in ALS', Front Digit Health 2025;7:1705368. n=31 with 16 deaths, median 3-year follow-up. Comparing simple MEAN daily step count against DISTRIBUTIONAL representations of physical activity: C-index 0.68 (distributional) vs 0.55 (mean), median over 1,000 bootstrap replicates, significant at the 90% level. Failure mode acknowledged by the authors: n=31, 'larger studies with larger sample sizes are required.' Implication for MedPull: the daily aggregate mean is throwing away signal. Compute per-day quantiles of intraday activity, or the fraction of the day above an activity threshold, and feed those as additional functional channels.

> Matabuena M et al., Front Digit Health 2025;7:1705368

### Normative-model transfer learning lets a big pretrained centile model be adapted to a new site/procedure with ~50 subjects
*[moderate]*

Elleaume et al., eLife 2026 (normative modelling sample-size study): model performance improved with sample size and 'demographic alignment of the covariates, particularly in age, was essential'; ~200 healthy controls sufficed when training directly within a dataset, BUT transfer-learning-adapted models pretrained on UK Biobank (n=42,747) reached comparable performance with only 50 individuals. Corroborating: Little et al., Imaging Neuroscience 2025, needed 3,276 controls across 21 sites for robust de novo normative models. The Python implementation of this paradigm is PCNtoolkit, which provides Bayesian Linear Regression, warped/SHASH-likelihood Hierarchical Bayesian Regression, a NormData class, model transfer/extension to new sites, and outputs per-subject Z-scores and centiles. Direct translation for MedPull: fit ONE cross-procedure recovery normative model on all patients, then transfer/adapt it per procedure with as few as ~50 — this is exactly the small-data escape hatch we need.

> Elleaume et al., eLife 2026; Little et al., Imaging Neurosci 2025; https://pcntoolkit.readthedocs.io/

### Joint models beat landmarking at our scale, but both require far more events than we will have
*[strong]*

BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 2025 landmarking-gradient-boosting simulation study at n=300, 650, 1000, 1500: the joint model was superior when the longitudinal-hazard relationship was linear; the ML landmarking approach only won 'under conditions involving larger sample sizes, higher censoring rates, and later landmark times'. BMC Med Res Methodol 2026 (COPD, n=344 with 111 adverse events) compared Joint Latent Class Model, Shared Random-Effects Model and Landmark model: SREM achieved higher AUC and lower Brier score at all landmark times with near-diagonal calibration. NPJ Precis Oncol 2025 review of 174 dynamic prediction models: joint models 28.2%, two-stage 32.2%, Cox landmarking 8.6%. Clin Epidemiol 2026 review of 171 articles: reported discrimination depends heavily on how the time horizon is defined (57% sliding window, 21% fixed final horizon) — so published AUCs are not comparable across studies. Verdict for MedPull: with ~3% event rates and n≤1000 we would have ~30 events; Riley-style minimum sample size for a 5-predictor model at 3% prevalence implies well over 1,000 patients. Do not fit dynamic outcome prediction yet.

> BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 2025; BMC Med Res Methodol 2026; NPJ Precis Oncol 2025; Clin Epidemiol 2026

### Python GAMLSS is immature; pyGAM ExpectileGAM is the production-ready quantile-curve path
*[strong]*

gamlss.com documents the canonical centile methodology: LMS = Box-Cox Cole–Green (BCCG) distribution for skewed-but-not-kurtotic data; BCPE (Box-Cox power exponential) and BCT (Box-Cox t) for kurtosis; functions lms(), quantSheets(), gamlss(). All of this is R. Python options are all partial: scikit-normod (GAMLSS for normative modelling), PyNM (LOESS/GP/centile normative modelling), xi2pi/gamlss (an in-progress R-to-Python port). By contrast pyGAM 0.12.0 (released 18 Dec 2025, Python ≥3.10 <3.15) ships ExpectileGAM with a fit_quantile() method, penalized B-spline terms s()/f()/te() with n_splines, spline_order, lam, and monotonic/convex constraints, gridsearch for lam, plus prediction_intervals() and confidence_intervals(). statsmodels QuantReg gives linear quantile regression with Greene (2008) asymptotic covariance using logistic or gaussian kernels and Bofinger/Chamberlain/Hall–Sheather bandwidths. Failure modes: ExpectileGAM's fit_quantile is an iterative expectile-to-quantile search that can fail to converge at extreme quantiles; separately-fitted quantile curves can CROSS and must be post-hoc rearranged (sort across the quantile axis); QuantReg is linear-in-covariates so it needs an explicit spline basis for day.

> https://www.gamlss.com/centile-estimation/ ; https://pypi.org/project/pygam/ ; statsmodels QuantReg docs

### The confidence-interval / prediction-interval distinction is the single biggest statistical defect in the current engine
*[strong]*

The engine's f(d) = floor + (1-floor)/(1+exp(-r(d-d50))) is a POINT with no uncertainty, and 'ratio-to-expected' is compared against fixed thresholds. Three separate uncertainty sources are being conflated: (a) uncertainty in the population mean curve — a confidence interval, width ∝ σ_between/√n, shrinks to zero as patients accrue; (b) between-patient variability — the marginal prediction interval / centile band for a NEW patient, which does NOT shrink with n and is the correct comparand for a first-week patient with no history; (c) within-patient day-to-day noise — which is what makes a single low day meaningless. Additionally, because the curve is hand-parameterized rather than fitted, (a) is not merely unknown but unbounded. Correct handling: emit a centile/z-score against the marginal predictive distribution, attach SE_curve(d) from the fit, and suppress any deviation flag where |z| < 2·SE_curve(d) — i.e. never flag a deviation smaller than the curve's own ignorance. For a patient with ≥10 days of their own history, switch to the INDIVIDUALIZED predictive interval conditional on their posterior random effects, which is materially narrower and is what a hierarchical model gives for free.

> Standard mixed-model theory; Cole TJ Stat Methods Med Res 2021 for the z-score precision framing

### Derived sample-size requirements for MedPull's own centile curves
*[moderate]*

Asymptotic SE of an estimated centile at standardized position z_p, in z units, is ≈ √(1 + z_p²/2)/√n_eff. Factors: median 1.00; 25th/75th (z=0.674) 1.11; 10th/90th (z=1.282) 1.35; 2nd/98th (z=2.054) 1.76. Cole's achieved SEs (0.041 median, 0.066 at 2nd/98th; ratio 1.61) are consistent with this after smoothing. n_eff is NOT n_patients: with daily observations and a ~14-day smoothing neighbourhood, intraclass correlation ρ≈0.75 gives design effect 1+(14-1)(0.75)≈10.8, so each patient contributes ≈14/10.8 ≈ 1.3 effective observations per smoothing window; Cole's 2–3× 'borrowing strength' from smoothing across day partially offsets this. Targeting SE(z) ≤ 0.15 (the centile line known to within 0.15 SD): median needs n_eff≈44 → ~35 patients per procedure; 10th/90th needs n_eff≈81 → ~65 patients; 2nd/98th needs n_eff≈138 → ~105 patients. Targeting the tighter SE(z) ≤ 0.10: 10th/90th needs ~140 patients; 2nd/98th needs ~240. Practical staging: <30 patients → hierarchical Bayesian curve only, no centiles; 30–100 → median curve + a single pooled dispersion; 100–250 → 10/25/50/75/90 centiles per procedure; 250–500 → 5th/95th; ≥500 → consider 2nd/98th, and only then. Also require ≥60% day-coverage per patient before that patient contributes to the fit, or missingness will bias the low centiles downward.

> Derived from Cole TJ Stat Methods Med Res 2021 precision framework + standard order-statistic SE and design-effect algebra


## Implications for backend

- The expected-recovery curve must stop being a hard-coded function and become a VERSIONED ARTIFACT. Create an `expected_curve` table/artifact keyed by (procedure, model_version, fit_date) storing, per day 0–90 and per covariate stratum: the median, the 10/25/75/90 centiles, the standard error of each centile, the n it was fitted on, and the method that produced it ('hand_tuned_v0' | 'pymc_hierarchical_v1' | 'expectile_gam_v2'). Every emitted deviation reason code must carry the model_version and the SE at that day. This is the change that makes everything else on this list shippable incrementally — without it, there is nowhere to put a fitted curve.
- Pre-operative baseline capture becomes a hard enrolment requirement, not a nice-to-have. The published LCGA classes separated far better on the RELATIVE (post-op ÷ pre-op) scale than the absolute one, and pre-op step count was THE dominant predictor of trajectory class. The backend needs a `preop_baseline` record per patient per metric, computed over a 7–14 day pre-surgery window with an explicit coverage count, and every trajectory metric should be stored and served on both the absolute and the ratio scale. Patients enrolled post-operatively (no baseline) must be routed to an absolute-scale model with an explicitly wider band and a documented lower confidence — not silently given the same thresholds.
- Wear-time / coverage must be a first-class column on every daily aggregate, and the existing coverage gate (≥3 of 6 metrics; low if <40% of last 7 days) needs to be extended to per-metric wear-hours. Low step counts are indistinguishable from a device left on a nightstand, and this is the dominant failure mode for a step-count centile system: uncorrected non-wear will drag the fitted 10th centile down and then mask genuine deterioration. Recommend: exclude patient-days with <10 wear-hours from BOTH the fitting set and the scoring path, and require ≥60% covered days before a patient contributes to any curve fit.
- Split the compute: model FITTING never runs in Lambda, model SCORING always does. PyMC/NUTS, GAMLSS-via-rpy2 and pyGAM gridsearch are minutes-to-hours offline jobs. Export a Parquet snapshot from SQLite, fit offline (a scheduled job or a manual notebook is fine at this stage), and ship the result as a precomputed lookup table — day × covariate-stratum × centile, a few thousand floats — that Lambda loads at cold start and interpolates. This keeps the Lambda bundle free of pymc/arviz/scikit-fda (which would blow the deployment size limit anyway) and keeps p99 latency flat. Only numpy/scipy/pandas need to be in the runtime.
- Replace the fixed composite weights (RHR .25, temp .25, HRV .20, RR .15, steps .15) with a common z-score / centile scale before weighting. Right now the weights are combining quantities on incommensurable scales; once every metric is expressed as a centile against its own fitted expected curve, the weights become a genuine clinical judgement about relative importance rather than an accidental unit conversion. This also makes the composite index directly interpretable ('this patient is at the 8th centile overall') and lets a single deviation threshold apply across procedures.
- Add a flag-suppression guard driven by model uncertainty: never emit a trajectory-deviation reason code when the magnitude of the deviation is smaller than roughly twice the expected curve's own standard error at that day, and never before post-op day 7. This is the operational form of the confidence-vs-prediction-interval distinction, and it is what stops a curve fitted on 30 patients from generating confident-sounding alerts it cannot support. It also makes the alert rate automatically increase as the model earns it.
- Ship the new engine in SHADOW MODE first. Run the fitted centile path alongside the existing hand-tuned path for every scored patient-day, log both outputs and their disagreement, and change no risk tier until you have a few hundred patient-days of disagreement data to inspect. The golden-tier tests pinned in the existing test suite are an asset here — they define the current behaviour precisely, so the shadow comparison can be expressed as a diff against them rather than a vague 'seems better'.
- Start logging ground-truth outcome labels (infection, readmission, MUA, ED visit) NOW with dates, in a dedicated table, even though nothing can be fitted on them for a long time. At single-digit event percentages, a 5-predictor supervised model needs well over 1,000 patients to avoid catastrophic overfitting; every month of unlogged outcomes is a month added to that timeline. In the interim the centile deviation is an unsupervised anomaly signal and should be described as such in the UI and in any clinical documentation — not as a validated risk prediction.
- Store daily aggregates in a long/tidy layout (patient_id, day_index, metric, value, wear_minutes, source, imputed_flag) rather than a wide one. Every method on this list — FPCA, GAMLSS, quantile GAMs, PyMC, DTW — consumes long format, and the wide layout will otherwise need reshaping in every fitting job. SQLite is fine for this until roughly 100k patient-days; the trigger for Postgres is when the offline fitting export starts taking minutes, not when patient count crosses a threshold.
- Expose 'days behind expected' as a first-class output alongside the centile. The DTW lag against the median curve is cheap (needs only a fitted median and 14 days of patient data), it needs no additional cohort size, and it is dramatically more actionable for both clinicians and patients than a unitless deviation index. It also decomposes deviation into timing versus magnitude, which have different clinical meanings — a patient tracking the right shape 9 days late is a different problem from one who has plateaued.

## Recommended stack

- **PRIMARY REPLACEMENT for the hand-tuned logistic expected curve, usable from the very first cohort (20–30 patients per procedure). Produces a population curve, patient-specific curves, and calibrated predictive intervals in one model.** — Bayesian hierarchical non-linear growth curve, Mitscherlich / asymptotic-regression form (3 parameters — more identifiable at small n than 4-parameter logistic or Gompertz), fitted on the RATIO scale to each patient's own pre-op baseline. Model: r_ij = A_i·(1 − exp(−k_i·(d_ij − t0_i))) with likelihood StudentT(nu=4, mu=r_ij, sigma). Non-centred parameterization is mandatory. The current hand-tuned floor/r/d50 values enter as the PRIOR MEAN on the population hyperparameters, so the model starts exactly where the engine is today and moves only as data accrues. via `pymc>=5.16 (with arviz>=0.18 for diagnostics; nutpie>=0.13 for 5–20× faster sampling). Fit OFFLINE only — never inside Lambda.`
  - params: Priors: mu_logA ~ Normal(log(1.0), 0.30) [asymptote ~100% of pre-op]; log A_i ~ Normal(mu_logA, sigma_A), sigma_A ~ HalfNormal(0.40). mu_logk ~ Normal(log(1/21), 0.50) [recovery time constant ~21 days, matching the J Arthroplasty 2024 finding that gains concentrate in the first 3 weeks]; log k_i ~ Normal(mu_logk, sigma_k), sigma_k ~ HalfNormal(0.50). t0_i ~ Normal(mu_t0, sigma_t0) with mu_t0 ~ Normal(2, 2) days, sigma_t0 ~ HalfNormal(2). sigma (residual) ~ HalfNormal(0.25). Covariate effects on log A and log k: Normal(0, 0.25) coefficients for standardized pre-op step count, sex, BMI, and procedure (TKA/UKR/THA) — procedure enters as a hierarchical offset so rare procedures shrink toward the global mean. Sampler: NUTS, 4 chains, tune=1500, draws=1500, target_accept=0.95. Gates: r_hat<1.01, ESS_bulk>400, zero divergences. Deviation score = the patient's day-d value evaluated against the POSTERIOR PREDICTIVE distribution, reported as a percentile, with the model's own SE(d) exported alongside.
  - needs: 20–30 patients per procedure with ≥14 days each for stable population hyperparameters; 10 patients if procedures are pooled with a procedure-level hyperprior. Individual-level A_i/k_i become meaningful at ≥10 observed days for that patient; below that the patient is essentially reading the population curve, which is correct behaviour and should be surfaced as low confidence.
  - why: It is the only method on this list that is honest at n=20–30, degrades gracefully as n grows, encodes the existing hand-tuned curve as a prior instead of discarding it, and emits a genuine prediction interval rather than a bare point estimate. Partial pooling means a patient with 4 days of data borrows the population curve, while a patient with 40 days gets a genuinely individual curve — which is exactly the behaviour the deviation index needs and currently lacks.
- **Data-derived recovery CENTILES per procedure, once a procedure crosses ~100–150 patients. This is the direct analogue of a paediatric growth chart and the eventual production surface for 'is this patient behind schedule'.** — Quantile regression curves via ExpectileGAM's quantile-fitting mode, fitted independently at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th centiles, on penalized B-splines in transformed day, with monotonic-increasing constraint on the day term. Post-hoc monotone rearrangement (np.sort across the quantile axis at each day) to eliminate quantile crossing. Response: log1p(steps) or logit of ratio-to-pre-op; predictor 1: sqrt(day) or log1p(day) (compresses the fast early phase, matching Cole's λ<1 sample-composition insight); predictors 2..k: standardized pre-op activity, sex, BMI, procedure. via `pygam==0.12.0 (`from pygam import ExpectileGAM, s, l, f`; `ExpectileGAM(...).fit_quantile(X, y, quantile=q, max_iter=50, tol=1e-4)`). Fallback for linear-in-covariates checks: statsmodels>=0.14 QuantReg (`.fit(q=0.1, vcov='robust', kernel='epa', bandwidth='hsheather')`). If/when a procedure exceeds ~500 patients and skew/kurtosis matter, escalate to true GAMLSS BCCG/BCPE/BCT via rpy2>=3.5 calling R's gamlss::lms() — there is no mature native-Python GAMLSS.`
  - params: Term spec: s(0, n_splines=8, spline_order=3, lam=0.6, constraints='monotonic_inc') + l(1) + l(2) + f(3). Choose lam by `gam.gridsearch(X, y, lam=np.logspace(-3, 3, 11))` using GCV, then FREEZE lam and refit all five quantiles with the same lam so the centile bands stay parallel and cannot cross pathologically. n_splines=8 over a 0–90 day domain gives ~11-day effective knot spacing — deliberately coarse to avoid chasing noise at n<200. Do NOT fit the 2nd/98th centiles. Report SE per centile per day by patient-level bootstrap (200 resamples, resample PATIENTS not rows).
  - needs: ~100–150 patients per procedure for the 10th/90th centiles at SE(z)≈0.15 (derived above); ~35 patients for the median alone; ≥250 before adding the 5th/95th. Require ≥60% wear-day coverage per patient for inclusion. Below 100 patients per procedure, do not ship per-procedure centiles — pool procedures and use the PyMC model above.
  - why: Centiles are exactly what clinicians already reason with (growth charts), they require no distributional assumption about the residuals, they handle the strong right-skew of step counts natively, and unlike a mean curve they directly answer 'what fraction of comparable patients are below this patient today'. The monotonic constraint encodes the clinically non-negotiable fact that expected recovery does not go backwards, which is what stops small-n wiggle from producing absurd expected curves.
- **Small-data substitute for GBTM/LCGA: discovering whether MedPull's own cohort really contains distinct recovery classes, at n=80–200 where GBTM is not licensed.** — Smooth each patient's 0–90-day curve onto a common grid, project onto a functional basis, run FPCA, then fit a small Gaussian mixture on the first 2–3 FPC scores. This reproduces the Yakdan (Spine J 2025, n=129) design and is far more stable than direct GBTM because the clustering happens in a 2–3 dimensional score space rather than in a high-dimensional likelihood with a mixture over random effects. via `scikit-fda==0.10.1 (`skfda.FDataGrid`, `skfda.representation.basis.BSplineBasis`, `skfda.preprocessing.smoothing.BasisSmoother` or `KernelSmoother`, `skfda.preprocessing.dim_reduction.FPCA`, plus `skfda.preprocessing.registration` for landmark and elastic/Fisher-Rao alignment, and `skfda.exploratory.outliers` for depth-based functional outlier detection). Clustering via scikit-learn>=1.4 `GaussianMixture`. For sparsely/irregularly observed curves scikit-fda has no PACE-style sparse FPCA — use R's fdapace via rpy2, or impute onto the grid with the PyMC model first.`
  - params: Pre-smooth with a 7-day centred rolling median before basis projection (kills wear-time dropouts). Basis: BSplineBasis(n_basis=7, order=4, domain_range=(0, 90)). FPCA(n_components=3, centering=True); expect PC1 (overall level) + PC2 (early-vs-late slope) to explain 80–90% of variance — report the actual explained-variance ratio and stop at the number of components reaching 90%. GaussianMixture(n_components=k for k in 2..4, covariance_type='diag', n_init=50, reg_covar=1e-4, random_state=0), select k by BIC AND require every class ≥5% of the cohort AND mean posterior assignment probability ≥0.7 — the same gates the GBTM literature uses. Expect the answer to be k=2 or 3; treat k≥4 as an artifact.
  - needs: ≥50 patients with ≥21 days of ≥60%-coverage data for 2 stable FPCs; ≥100 before publishing class prevalences; ≥150 before attempting to model predictors of class membership (multinomial logistic with ≥10 patients per predictor per class).
  - why: It gets the clinically meaningful output of GBTM (a small number of interpretable recovery archetypes plus a per-patient soft membership) at one-tenth the sample size, with a published orthopedic-adjacent precedent at n=129 where the poor-recovery cluster carried a 23% vs 7% complication rate. The FPC scores are also directly usable as two continuous features in any later model, which is more information-preserving than a hard class label.
- **Distribution-free prediction intervals with a finite-sample coverage guarantee, wrapped around whichever centile model is in production — so the engine can state '90% of comparable patients fall in this band' and have it be true.** — Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR): take the fitted 5th/95th (or 10th/90th) quantile curves, compute conformity scores on a held-out calibration set, and inflate/deflate the band by the empirical quantile of those scores. Because our observations are clustered within patients, use CLUSTERED/block conformal: split by PATIENT, and use one summary conformity score per calibration patient rather than one per patient-day. via `mapie==1.4.1 (requires Python ≥3.9, numpy ≥1.23, scikit-learn ≥1.4). Use the conformalized-quantile-regression estimator for regression, with a GroupKFold-style patient-level split. Also supports split conformal, jackknife+, jackknife+-after-bootstrap and CV+ if a point model is preferred.`
  - params: confidence_level=0.90 (alpha=0.10). Calibration set: minimum n_cal ≥ 1/alpha − 1 = 19 PATIENTS for 90% coverage; target ≥60 calibration patients for a stable band, and split 60/20/20 train/calibrate/test by patient ID. Conformity score: the CQR score max(q_lo(x) − y, y − q_hi(x)), aggregated per patient as the max across that patient's days (conservative) or the 90th percentile of their days (less conservative — pick one and document it). Validate empirical coverage on the held-out patients and report it; if measured coverage is <85% or >95% the exchangeability assumption is broken, most likely by a cohort/site shift.
  - needs: ≥19 calibration patients absolutely; ≥60 for a usable band; ≥100 held-out patients to measure coverage to within a few percentage points. Must be patient-disjoint from the training set.
  - why: It converts a heuristic band into one with a stated, checkable guarantee, and it is the only cheap way to be honest about coverage when the underlying quantile model is fitted on 100–200 patients. It also gives an automatic monitoring signal: when measured coverage drifts, the expected curve has gone stale and needs refitting.
- **Aligning individual recoveries that differ in TIMING rather than magnitude, and converting 'how far behind' into days rather than an abstract index — much more actionable for both clinician and patient.** — Constrained Dynamic Time Warping between a patient's smoothed curve and the fitted median (50th centile) curve, with the warping path used to read off a per-patient TIME LAG in days. Pre-smooth both series with a 7-day rolling median and z-normalize per-series ONLY if you want shape-not-level comparison (usually you do NOT — level is the clinical signal, so leave unnormalized and warp on the ratio scale).   - why: 'You are about 9 days behind the typical recovery for your procedure' is a far better clinical and patient-facing output than 'composite deviation index 1.4', and it decomposes deviation into a timing component and a magnitude component that have different clinical implications (slow-but-tracking vs genuinely plateaued). DTW failure modes must be respected: it is not a metric (no triangle inequality), it is O(n·m) so cap series length, unconstrained DTW will align almost anything, and it is very sensitive to noise — hence the mandatory pre-smoothing and Sakoe-Chiba band.
- **Cross-procedure normative model with transfer/adaptation to a new procedure or new clinical site that has only ~50 patients — the escape hatch for launching a new procedure without a cold start.** — Hierarchical Bayesian Regression normative modelling with a flexible (SHASH) likelihood to capture skew and kurtosis in the recovery distribution, trained across all procedures, then transferred/extended to the new procedure or site with a small local sample. Outputs per-patient Z-scores and centiles directly. via `pcntoolkit (Predictive Clinical Neuroscience toolkit) — provides BLR, warped BLR, GPR and HBR with Normal/SHASH/Beta likelihoods, a NormData class, model transfer and extension APIs, cluster/federated fitting, and a CLI.   - params: Covariates: day (with a spline or polynomial basis, degree 3 over 0–90 days), pre-op baseline, age, sex, BMI. Batch/site effect = procedure × clinic. Likelihood: SHASH (four-parameter, handles the right-skew of step counts without a log transform). For transfer to a new procedure, freeze the shared/global parameters and re-estimate only the batch-effect parameters on the local ~50 patients. That is the difference between being able to launch a new procedure in month 1 versus month 18. It also gives Z-scores on a common scale across all metrics and procedures, which is precisely the interface the existing composite deviation index needs in order to stop using arbitrary fixed weights.
- **INTERIM method for the period before any procedure reaches 30 patients — i.e. what to do literally today, and what to stop doing.** — (1) Keep the hand-tuned f(d) but immediately attach an explicit, documented uncertainty band to it, derived from the published literature rather than from our own data. (2) Re-express every ratio-to-expected on the ratio-to-own-pre-op-baseline scale rather than absolute. (3) Replace fixed composite weights with a simple pooled empirical percentile across ALL procedures combined, with James–Stein / hierarchical shrinkage of each procedure's mean toward the global mean. (4) Widen all thresholds so the false-positive rate is deliberately low while the curve is unvalidated. via `numpy>=1.26, scipy>=1.11 (scipy.stats.norm, scipy.ndimage.uniform_filter1d for smoothing), pandas>=2.1, statsmodels>=0.14 (lowess for a nonparametric pooled curve).   - params: Literature-anchored prior band for TKA/UKR step recovery at weeks 1–6, taken from Olanrewaju 2026 (n=82): week-1 median ~360 steps with a 10th–90th spread of roughly 29–991; week-6 median ~3,739 with spread ~1,452–6,606; expect only ~1 in 3 patients to exceed pre-op by week 6; on the relative scale, expect week-6 values of ~31% / 64% / 92% of pre-op for the low / moderate / high thirds. Shrinkage: procedure mean_shrunk = w·procedure_mean + (1−w)·global_mean with w = n_p/(n_p + 25), so a procedure needs ~25 patients before it is trusted half as much as the pool.   - why: It is defensible, it is cheap, and it fixes the two defects that are actively harmful right now — the absolute scale (which the LCGA evidence shows is dominated by pre-op fitness, so absolute thresholds systematically flag deconditioned patients and miss deteriorating athletic ones) and the total absence of any uncertainty statement. It also builds the exact data structures (ratio scale, coverage flags, pre-op baseline window) that every downstream method on this list requires.

## Open questions

- What fraction of MedPull patients will actually have a usable 7–14 day pre-operative wearable baseline? The entire relative-scale approach — which is the one the published LCGA evidence supports — collapses without it. If the answer is under ~60%, the architecture needs a principled dual path (relative for those with baselines, absolute-plus-demographics for those without) and the two paths will have materially different alert rates that must be reconciled before anyone trusts the tiers.
- What is the actual procedure mix? The recommendations assume a dominant procedure (likely TKA) reaching 100–150 patients well before the others. If the mix is flat across 6+ procedures, no single procedure reaches centile-viability for years, and the PCNtoolkit-style cross-procedure model with per-procedure transfer becomes the primary path rather than a fallback — which is a bigger engineering commitment and should be decided early.
- What is the true within-patient day-to-day intraclass correlation for step counts in the first 6 weeks post-op? The sample-size derivation above assumes ρ≈0.75, which drives the design effect and therefore every 'how many patients do we need' number. This is measurable from the first 30 patients and should be measured before committing to a centile rollout schedule — if ρ is closer to 0.9, all the sample-size requirements roughly double.
- Which specific wearables and which step-counting algorithms are in play? Step counts are not comparable across Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin and Whoop, particularly at the low cadences typical of week-1 post-op patients where most consumer algorithms systematically undercount. A device covariate (or per-device calibration offset) may be mandatory in the centile model, and mixing devices without it will inflate the apparent between-patient variance and widen the bands to uselessness.
- Is a low step count in week 1 a signal or an artifact of prescribed rest? Post-operative protocols differ by surgeon and procedure, and some low trajectories are protocol-compliant rather than pathological. Without a structured record of the prescribed weight-bearing/activity protocol, the low-recovery class will be contaminated with correctly-recovering patients — this is the most likely source of clinician-perceived false positives.
- What alert rate is clinically tolerable? This determines the centile threshold far more than any statistical criterion. A 10th-centile threshold alerts on 10% of patient-days by construction — with 200 patients that is ~20 alerts/day, which may be unworkable. The threshold should be set from operational capacity and then the achieved sensitivity honestly reported, rather than set from a statistical convention and the workload discovered later.
- Do we have any access to an external cohort for pretraining? The transfer-learning result (50 patients when pretrained on a large cohort vs 200 de novo) is the single biggest available accelerator, but it needs a source cohort. Worth investigating whether any public arthroplasty accelerometry dataset, or a partner institution's retrospective data, could serve as the pretraining base — this could compress the timeline to credible per-procedure centiles by a year or more.
- Should the trajectory classes (once found) be surfaced to clinicians at all, or used only internally? Hard class assignment at n≈100 with entropy around 0.8 means roughly 1 in 5 patients is assigned to a class they only weakly belong to. Surfacing a continuous centile plus a lag-in-days may be both more honest and more actionable than a class label — but clinicians may strongly prefer the label, and that tension should be resolved with users before building the UI.
