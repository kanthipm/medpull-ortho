# Post-operative complication surveillance in orthopedic surgery: incidence, presentation windows, earliest detectable physiologic/behavioral signatures, and current diagnostic criteria

## Summary

The single most defensible remote-monitoring signal in orthopedics today is not a vital sign — it is patient-reported wound drainage. In a 1,019-patient multicentre telemonitoring cohort, any drainage still present in postoperative week 2 gave 88% sensitivity / 88% specificity for acute PJI, moderate-to-heavy drainage in week 3 gave OR 103 and PPV 83%, and complete absence of drainage gave NPV >98% (1 PJI in 467 patients). Acute PJI itself presents at a median of 14 days (IQR 10–18) — squarely inside an RTM window. Persistent wound drainage lasting >3 days is independently predictive (OR 39.4/day, 62.9% sens / 90.6% spec). Vital signs give much shorter lead times: the best-quantified figure is a median 14 hours for continuous HR+temperature telemetry flagging 78% of infectious complications, and 7–11 hours in a prospective wearable postoperative cohort (HR +14 bpm, RR +5 bpm, SpO2 −4%, skin temp +1.2°C). Activity data predicts course rather than crisis: POD1 step count ≥1,000 (OR 0.63 for prolonged LOS), return-to-baseline activity AUROC 0.76 for 30-day readmission. For the diagnostic layer, encode MSIS 2018 (score >5 = infected, 3–5 inconclusive) and EBJIS 2021 (<1500 leukocytes/µL or <65% PMN = unlikely; >3000/µL or >80% PMN = confirmed) verbatim — surgeons will check. Timing windows differ sharply by procedure: 76.8% of THA PJIs are diagnosed by day 90 versus only 57.7% of TKA PJIs, symptomatic PE clusters at median POD 3 (IQR 2–7), dislocation 91% within 6 weeks (posterior approach), MUA decisions at 6–12 weeks on a <90° flexion threshold, cyclops lesions at 2–3 months, and rotator cuff retear peaking between week 6 and month 3. Several commonly-cited entities are weak or contested: CRPS after TKA (0 cases in 100 prospectively Budapest-screened patients), and single-timepoint CRP (cannot discriminate PJI in the first 6 postoperative days).

## Findings

### PJI incidence and the 90-day blind spot differs by joint — TKA infections declare late, THA early
*[strong]*

In 136,491 patients, PJI diagnosed by 1 year was 1.33% overall. By procedure: primary THA 0.94%, primary TKA 1.05%, revision THA 4.39%, revision TKA 5.33%. Proportion diagnosed within 90 days: 76.78% primary THA, 74.12% revision THA, but only 57.67% primary TKA and 53.78% revision TKA — i.e. ~42% of primary TKA PJIs present between day 91 and day 365. Separately, ~60% of SSIs occur within 30 days and 74% within 90 days. Engineering consequence: an infection surveillance window closing at day 90 misses roughly a quarter of THA and over 40% of TKA infections; TKA cohorts need a longer, lower-intensity tail.

> Weinstein/Rothman Institute et al., 'Limiting the Surveillance Period to 90 Days Misses a Large Portion of Infections in the First Year After Total Hip and Knee Arthroplasty', J Arthroplasty 2022, PMC9160673 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9160673/

### MSIS 2018 PJI definition — exact point weights and score cutoffs (encode verbatim)
*[strong]*

Minor criteria scoring: serum CRP >1 mg/dL = 2 points; serum D-dimer >860 ng/mL = 2 points; ESR >30 mm/h = 1 point; synovial WBC >3,000 cells/µL = 3 points; synovial alpha-defensin signal-to-cutoff ratio >1.0 = 3 points; leukocyte esterase ++ = 3 points; synovial PMN% >80% = 2 points; synovial CRP >6.9 mg/L = 1 point. Total >5 = INFECTED; 3–5 = INCONCLUSIVE (requires intraoperative findings); <3 = NOT INFECTED. Major criteria (either alone = infected): two positive periprosthetic cultures of the same organism, or a sinus tract communicating with the joint/prosthesis. Reported performance: sensitivity 97.7%, specificity 99.5%.

> Parvizi J, Tan TL, Goswami K, et al., 'The 2018 Definition of Periprosthetic Hip and Knee Infection: An Evidence-Based and Validated Criteria', J Arthroplasty 2018;33(5):1309-1314.e2, PMID 29551303 — https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(18)30226-2/abstract

### EBJIS 2021 PJI definition — three-tier thresholds differ from MSIS and are the European standard
*[strong]*

EBJIS 2021 uses infection unlikely / likely / confirmed. Synovial fluid: total leukocyte count <1,500 cells/µL OR PMN <65% ⇒ infection unlikely (sensitive rule-out, if no other positive feature); >1,500/µL or >65% PMN ⇒ suggestive; >3,000 cells/µL OR PMN >80% ⇒ specific enough to CONFIRM. Serum CRP >10 mg/L is only a 'suggestive' feature. Independent validation: optimal synovial WBC 1,470 cells/µL (AUC 0.94), PMN 62.5% (AUC 0.91), synovial CRP 2.7 mg/L (AUC 0.90), adenosine deaminase 60 U/L (AUC 0.82), alpha-2-macroglobulin 420 mg/L (AUC 0.76). MSIS/EBJIS discordance: MSIS treats synovial WBC >3,000/µL as a 3-point minor criterion, EBJIS treats it as confirmatory — any display of 'PJI criteria met' must name the system used.

> McNally M, Sousa R, Wouthuyzen-Bakker M, et al., 'The EBJIS definition of periprosthetic joint infection', Bone Joint J 2021;103-B(1):18-25; validation: Sigmund IK et al., J Bone Joint Infect 2023;8:109 — https://jbji.copernicus.org/articles/8/109/2023/

### Normal CRP kinetics after arthroplasty and time-decaying PJI cutoffs — a single CRP value is uninterpretable without the day
*[moderate]*

Uncomplicated THA/TKA: CRP rises sharply, peaks around postoperative day 2–3, then declines slowly toward baseline by the end of the second postoperative week. Time-dependent PJI cutoffs: within 30 days, 50.7 mg/L (sens 85%, spec 83%); within 45 days, 44.9 mg/L (sens 86%, spec 89%); within 90 days, 39.8 mg/L (sens 91%, spec 95%). For THA specifically: within 6 weeks suspect early PJI at CRP ≥100 mg/L; between 6 and 12 weeks CRP ≥33 mg/L diagnoses PJI with high accuracy. CONTESTED/NEGATIVE: CRP measured during the first 6 postoperative days after THA cannot predict early periprosthetic infection — do not build a POD 0–6 CRP alarm.

> 'C-reactive protein during the first 6 postoperative days after total hip arthroplasty cannot predict early periprosthetic infection', PMC10191946 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10191946/ ; 'Serum CRP and Procalcitonin Kinetics in Elective THA', PMC4026950

### Patient-reported wound drainage is the strongest quantified remote predictor of acute PJI — week-by-week ORs, PPV/NPV, plus ICM day-thresholds and the hematoma link
*[strong]*

Multicentre prospective cohort, n=1,019 (46% knee, 54% hip), daily self-reported wound status for 30 postoperative days on a 5-point scale (no/minimal/mild/moderate/heavy). PJI in 16 (1.6%); early PJI at median 14 days (IQR 10–18); 2 late-early cases at days 71 and 77. Drainage prevalence non-PJI vs PJI: week 1, 50% vs 63%; week 2, 12% vs 88%; week 3, 8% vs 64%; week 4, 3% vs 25%. Moderate-to-heavy drainage: week 2 OR 51.22 (95% CI 15.84–165.65); week 3 OR 103.23 (26.08–408.57), PPV 83%. New-onset drainage week 2: OR 80.71 (9.12–714.52). Cumulative >5 drainage days weeks 1–3: OR 9.20 (3.37–25.14), sens 63%, spec 87%. ANY drainage week 2: sens 88%, spec 88%. >10 drainage days: sens 27%, spec 97%. NO drainage: NPV >98% (1 of 467). The study's own naive alerts fired for only 6 of 16 PJI patients — direct evidence flat rules underperform week-conditional logic. ICM defines persistent wound drainage as >2×2 cm on the dressing beyond 72 h, mandates monitoring past 72 h and reoperation if draining >5–7 days; inpatient data give OR 39.4 per additional drainage day for acute high-grade PJI (PWD >3 days: 62.9% sens, 90.6% spec) and preop×POD2 leukocyte product >100 OR 2.1 (96.9% spec), while glucose, Hb, erythrocytes, platelets and CRP had NO early predictive value. Hematoma: 13/271 (4.7%) had PWD >72 h, 8/13 (61.5%) with associated superficial hematoma; wound complications quadruple 5-year PJI risk after TKR. Published ladder: days 1–3 anticoagulation adjustment + dressing changes, days 3–5 incisional NPWT, beyond day 7 debridement above the capsule.

> Wouthuyzen-Bakker M et al., 'Wound drainage after arthroplasty and prediction of acute prosthetic joint infection: prospective multicentre cohort using a telemonitoring app', 2023, PMC10015257 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015257/ ; early predictors PMC10439017 ; Leggieri et al., Knee Surg Sports Traumatol Arthrosc 2026, doi:10.1002/ksa.70077

### VTE after TKA/THA: incidence, and the tight POD 2–7 window in which symptomatic PE presents
*[strong]*

Contemporary symptomatic rates: 90-day symptomatic VTE after elective THA on aspirin prophylaxis 1.11% (DVT 0.59%, PE 0.53%; n=8,885). NSQIP trend 2009→2022: 30-day VTE fell 0.8%→0.5% (THA) and 1.8%→0.9% (TKA). Timing of symptomatic PE: median postoperative day 3 (IQR 2–7) with non-warfarin anticoagulants, 90-day symptomatic PE 0.71%; other series report median day 7 (California discharge data) and median day 12 after TKR — spread reflects detection method. Asymptomatic screening DVT up to 2 weeks reaches 27.3% overall (2.8% proximal femoral) — do not use screening-DVT incidence as an alert base rate. Historic untreated rates: 40–60% after THA, 40–85% after TKA.

> 'The Timing of Symptomatic Pulmonary Embolism in Patients With Nonwarfarin Anticoagulants Following Elective Primary TJA', J Arthroplasty 2020 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883540320300425 ; PMC10117228 ; 'Current Rates and Trends of VTE After THA and TKA: Updated NSQIP Analysis', Arthroplasty Today 2025

### SpO2 is a real but low-yield PE signal after TKA — 80% of detected PE is asymptomatic
*[moderate]*

Prospective pulse-oximetry protocol, n=1,645 TKA. PE detected in 20 (1.2%); symptomatic PE only 4 (0.24%) — 80% of CT-confirmed PE asymptomatic. CTPA triggers: persistent SpO2 <95%, a sudden SpO2 decrease, or a decrease after POD3 with symptoms; SpO2 ≥95% used to exclude hypoxemia. Mean day of CTPA: 2.4 days (persistent-low), 2.6 days (sudden-drop), 8.3 days (late symptomatic); routine monitoring ran to POD2. Yield once triggered: 34.5% (20/58). The study did NOT report sensitivity, specificity, AUC, heart-rate change or lead time — a −4% SpO2 rule is defensible as a screening trigger, not a validated detector. Corroborating wearable data: SpO2 −4% and HR +14 bpm preceded clinical recognition by 7–11 hours.

> 'Efficacy of pulse oximetry for early diagnosis of pulmonary embolism after total knee arthroplasty', 2024, PMC10801930 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801930/

### Arthrofibrosis and the MUA decision: <90° flexion at 6–12 weeks is the operative trigger
*[moderate]*

Prevalence of stiffness after TKA 4.54%; MUA performed in ~4% of TKA patients. Standard indication: failure to achieve >90° of flexion by 6–12 weeks postoperatively. Patients presenting in the 6–12 week window generally receive MUA; later presentation usually requires arthroscopic lysis of adhesions. Outcome benchmarks: mean flexion gain 28° at 6 weeks post-MUA, 37° at 1 year; in patients with <70° flexion before MUA, mean flexion 6 weeks after MUA was 100.0° ± 7.68°. Product implication: trend ROM weekly from week 2 and alert on a PROJECTED failure to cross 90° by week 6, raised at week 3–4 while intensified physiotherapy can still change the outcome. (Primary anchor is 2011 — flag as older; a 2024 Ranawat Award multicenter RCT exists but was paywalled.)

> Pfitzner T et al., 'Arthrofibrosis after TKA — Influence factors on the absolute flexion and gain in flexion after manipulation under anaesthesia', BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2011;12:184, PMC3175211 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3175211/ ; Ranawat Award MUA multicenter RCT, J Arthroplasty 2024

### THA dislocation is an early, front-loaded event concentrated in the first 6 weeks
*[moderate]*

Primary THA dislocation rates by approach (n=13,335): posterior 1.1%, direct anterior 0.7%, lateral 0.5%. Anterior approach revision-for-instability hazard in the 0–3 month window HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.83) versus posterior. Timing: with the posterior approach ALL dislocations occurred within 6 months and 91% within 6 weeks; in a direct-anterior cohort 75% of first-time dislocations occurred in the first 3 weeks; across broader populations 38% occur within 3 months of the index THA. Behavioral signature: an acute mechanical event with no physiologic prodrome — the detectable signature is a same-day order-of-magnitude activity collapse plus an acute pain spike, not a drifting trend. Precaution adherence and deep-flexion/adduction exposure are the only true leading indicators.

> 'Dislocation incidence and risk factors following direct anterior primary THA: a consecutive, single-surgeon cohort', 2025, PMC12051311 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12051311/ ; 'A comparison of cemented femoral fixation via anterior versus posterior approach THA: 60,739 THAs', PMC11264548

### Periprosthetic fracture: low early incidence, Vancouver classification drives management
*[moderate]*

Periprosthetic femoral fracture incidence ~1.7% after primary THA and 6.2% after revision THA at 10 years; rates up to 15% in some major registries; one recent series found 38/768 hip surgeries (4.5%). Vancouver classification: type A (trochanteric — AG greater, AL lesser), type B (around/just distal to the stem: B1 stable stem, B2 loose stem, B3 loose stem plus poor bone stock), type C (well distal to the stem); B2/B3 require stem revision, B1/C are fixed. Signature: like dislocation, a discrete traumatic event — an acute fall plus abrupt loss of weight-bearing and step count is the only monitorable precursor, so fall detection plus a step-cliff rule is the right mechanism, not a physiologic trend.

> 'Periprosthetic hip fractures: an update into their management and clinical outcomes', EFORT Open Rev 2021;6(1):75-92, PMC7845569 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7845569/ ; 'A Retrospective Study on the Incidence of Periprosthetic Fractures Related to THA', PMC11941720

### CRPS after TKA is likely over-diagnosed — Budapest-criteria prospective data found zero cases in 100 patients
*[moderate]*

Legacy literature claims CRPS in up to 21% of TKA patients. A prospective study applying the Budapest (IASP) criteria to 100 consecutive TKA patients identified NO cases and warned misdiagnosis is potentially hazardous because it delays workup for infection and loosening. A longitudinal Budapest-criteria study found steeply decaying prevalence: 19.1% at 2 weeks, 9.1% at 6 weeks, 1.8% at 12 weeks — most early 'CRPS-like' pictures are resolving normal post-TKA inflammation. Budapest criteria require continuing pain disproportionate to the inciting event, PLUS ≥1 reported symptom in 3 of 4 categories (sensory hyperesthesia/allodynia; vasomotor temperature or color asymmetry; sudomotor edema/sweating asymmetry; motor-trophic weakness, tremor, dystonia, trophic changes) AND ≥1 observed sign at evaluation in 2 of 4 categories. Risk factors: advanced age, longer duration of preoperative OA symptoms; sex not associated. Implication: do not surface a CRPS flag before week 12, and never as the primary hypothesis for disproportionate early pain — rank infection above it.

> 'CRPS after Total Knee Arthroplasty is Rare and Misdiagnosis Potentially Hazardous — Prospective Study of the New Diagnostic Criteria in 100 Patients with No Cases Identified', PMID 29294499 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29294499/ ; 'The Prevalence of CRPS after Primary TKA — A Longitudinal Study', J Orthop Case Rep 2026

### ACL reconstruction: cyclops lesion and extension loss present at 2–6 months, with a wide imaging-vs-symptom gap
*[moderate]*

Cyclops lesion is the second most common cause of extension loss after ACLR. Radiographic prevalence far exceeds symptomatic prevalence: 33.0–46.8% on MRI and 14.5–35.4% on arthroscopy, versus symptomatic 'cyclops syndrome' (extension loss >5°) in 0.9–3.6% after double-bundle ACLR and 1–9.8% overall. Timing: lesions typically become symptomatic 2–3 months after surgery; patients with early clinical cyclops syndrome had a mean extension deficit of 19° at a mean 5.9 months post-index. Typical extension loss ~10°, enough to disrupt daily activity. Monitorable signature: failure of terminal extension to reach 0° by week 4–6, then plateau or regression between weeks 6 and 12, with a clunk on terminal extension and anterior knee pain. Arthroscopic excision resolves swelling and progressively restores extension.

> 'Cyclops lesion — The entity causing loss of knee extension after ACL reconstruction surgery: A case report', Ann Med Surg 2021, PMC8577123 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8577123/ ; 'Etiology and effects of cyclops lesions in double-bundle ACL reconstruction: a case-control study', J Orthop 2025

### Rotator cuff retear clusters between postoperative week 6 and month 3 — exactly when sling weaning happens
*[moderate]*

On 6-month postoperative MRI after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (n=124): Sugaya type I/II 76 (61%), type III 33 (27%), type IV/V (full-thickness retear) 15 (12%). Retear rates across techniques/populations range ~12–57%. Retear is defined as Sugaya IV/V — full-thickness discontinuity on both coronal and oblique sagittal MRI. Timing: the highest retear rate occurred between 6 weeks and 3 months postoperatively, with equivalent lower rates within the first 6 weeks and from 3 to 6 months; early-group retears had significantly larger Sugaya-classified defects. Late progression: on final ultrasound, new retears appeared in 1/76 (1.3%) of Sugaya I/II versus 4/33 (12.1%) of Sugaya III (significant) — Sugaya III at 6 months is a genuine at-risk state, not healed. Retear patients had significantly worse pain and functional scores. Monitorable signature: reversal of the pain-improvement trajectory and loss of previously-gained ACTIVE elevation between weeks 6 and 12 with preserved PASSIVE ROM, typically after sling discontinuation.

> 'Timing of retears after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair and associated factors: a retrospective analysis', J Shoulder Elbow Surg 2023 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1058274623001222 ; 'Type III Sugaya Classification after Rotator Cuff Repair: Preoperative Risk Factors and Progression to Retear', Clin Orthop Surg, PMC13047394

### Lumbar fusion: pseudarthrosis and adjacent segment disease — different timelines, very different reoperation rates
*[moderate]*

Adjacent segment disease (ASD) incidence 5–27% depending on criteria (a 2014 systematic review narrowed it to 2–14%); cumulative clinical ASD at L5-S1 after an L4-L5 operation was 10%. ASD reoperation 9–36% at 5–10 year follow-up. Pseudarthrosis is defined on postoperative CT as screw loosening and/or non-bridging bone; head-to-head, reoperation occurred in 29% of pseudarthrosis patients versus 5% of ASD patients, and fusion was achieved in only 71% after a first revision for pseudarthrosis. Timelines: pseudarthrosis is judged at 12–24 months (fusion is not expected before ~6–12 months, so persistent pain at 3 months is NOT evidence of nonunion); ASD is a years-scale cumulative hazard. Monitorable signatures: for pseudarthrosis, failure of the axial/leg-pain curve to keep improving after month 3 plus mechanical (activity-dependent) pain; for ASD, a NEW pain pattern emerging after a sustained plateau of good function — a re-rise after recovery, not a failure to recover.

> Kim et al., 'Outcomes of Revision Surgery Following Instrumented Posterolateral Fusion in Degenerative Lumbar Spinal Stenosis: A Comparative Analysis between Pseudarthrosis and Adjacent Segment Disease', PMID 28670415 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28670415/ ; 'Adjacent segment disease after MIS-TLIF: incidence and risk factors', PMC9661740

### Post-op delirium after TJA: 13.6% pooled, onset POD 1–3, screened with CAM/4AT
*[moderate]*

Pooled incidence of postoperative delirium after primary total joint arthroplasty in older adults: 13.6% (95% CI 12.2–15.0%), from a 2025 meta-analysis of 35 studies and >29,000 patients. Wide between-study range: 0% in one 462-patient elective TJA series to 21.0% after THA for hip fracture. Standard screening cadence is CAM twice daily for the first 3 postoperative days (hip-fracture protocols screen morning and evening on days 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7) — the onset window is essentially POD 1–3. Preoperative frailty dominates: delirium in 42.3% of frail versus 7.8% of non-frail hip-fracture patients; other associations are advanced age, dementia, hypertension, diabetes, prior stroke. Monitorable signature: nocturnal activity fragmentation and sleep-wake reversal — a collapse in measured sleep duration and a rise in overnight movement on POD 0–1 — precede the diagnostic CAM screen; a strong candidate use for the existing sleep-duration channel.

> Ni et al., 'Incidence and factors associated with postoperative delirium after primary total joint arthroplasty in older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis', 2025, PMC12586022 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12586022/ ; frailty cohort PMC10830592

### Medical complication base rates after TJA: AKI, ileus, urinary retention, pneumonia, cardiac
*[moderate]*

Overall systemic complication rate 5.1% (95% CI 4.3–5.8) after hip replacement and 6.9% (95% CI 5.0–8.8) after knee replacement. AKI: reported 2–15% depending on criteria; specific series 6.2% (n=7,406), 14.7% (n=911), 16.2% (n=197), 19.9% by strict KDIGO creatinine criteria. KDIGO definition to encode: serum creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 h, OR ≥1.5× baseline, OR urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h. Postoperative ileus: 0.7% of 4,567 TJA patients — rare but with severe consequences. Urinary retention: 0.07% as a coded complication, but straight-catheterization rates of 24–37% (TKA, age <60) and 18–30% (THA, age >65) in protocol-driven series — a ~300-fold definitional spread, so any threshold must state its definition. Hospital-acquired pneumonia 1–2%; MI/arrhythmia 0.5–1.53%; VTE 1.12–3.0%; AKI and UTI grouped 0–2%. Preoperative comorbidity burden is the single most important risk factor for both postoperative mortality and systemic medical complications.

> 'Systemic medical complications following joint replacement: a review of the evidence', 2023, PMC9974346 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974346/ ; AKI incidence PMC7068025 ; 'Postoperative ileus after total joint arthroplasty', PMID 18358373 ; POUR meta-analysis PMC11256004

### Readmission after TJA: 3.3–4.5% at 30 days, and the surgical/medical cause split inverts between hip and knee
*[strong]*

Unadjusted 30-day readmission after primary TKA and THA is ~4–5%, trending down from 4.5% (2011) to 3.3% (2018). More than 70% of readmissions and complications occur within the first 30 days, though 90-day windows are considered better quality measures. Cause split inverts by joint: after TKA, 52.6% of readmissions are MEDICAL and 37.7% surgical; after THA, 50.7% are SURGICAL and 38.5% medical. Leading medical causes: diseases of the circulatory system, abnormal laboratory values, diseases of the digestive system. Leading surgical causes for both TKA and THA: surgical site infection/wound disruption first, venous thromboembolism second. NOTE a documented data-source discordance: CMS-derived and NSQIP-derived 30-day readmission rates for the same procedures disagree materially, so any benchmark shown to a surgeon must name its source.

> 'Hospital Readmissions After Total Joint Arthroplasty: An Updated Analysis and Implications for Value-Based Care', J Arthroplasty 2023 — https://www.arthroplastyjournal.org/article/S0883-5403(22)00859-2/abstract ; 'Discordance in Published 30-Day Readmission Rates Following Primary THA and TKA: CMS Versus NSQIP', PMC11195884

### Quantified lead times: vital signs buy hours, wound reports and activity buy days
*[weak]*

The only directly quantified vital-sign lead time found: continuous heart-rate + temperature telemetry correctly flagged 78% of infectious complications a median of 14 hours before standard chart documentation (single 2023 feasibility study — weak evidence). A prospective wearable postoperative cohort reported mean detection 7–11 hours earlier across complications, with abnormal HR +14 bpm, RR +5 bpm, SpO2 −4%, skin temperature +1.2°C observed before clinical recognition; overall early complication detection 30% vs 13% standard care, SSI detection 14% vs 7%, LOS 5.6±1.8 vs 7.4±2.1 days, ICU transfers 6% vs 14%, readmissions 8% vs 16%. Activity-based models (colorectal, transferable in form): POD-1 step-count spline AUROC 0.78 for prolonged LOS, ≥1,000 steps on POD1 OR 0.63 (95% CI 0.45–0.84); return-to-baseline activity AUROC 0.76 (sens 75%, spec 69%) for 30-day readmission; distance + HRV + skin temperature AUROC 0.82 for pulmonary complications; preop inactivity + NSQIP AUROC 0.71; each additional 100 m walked lowered pulmonary-complication odds ~6%. Arthroplasty activity benchmarks: 86.7% of preoperative step count at week 6 and 100% at week 7 in a favorable (UKA) group versus 59.4% and 73% in a slower comparison group; absolute daily steps moved ~4,970→6,185 in men but 5,532→4,652 in women. Device accuracy bound: wearable bias −1.6 bpm for HR and +0.12 °C for skin temperature.

> 'Continuous Wearable-Sensor Monitoring After Colorectal Surgery: A Systematic Review of Clinical Outcomes and Predictive Analytics', 2025, PMC12428718 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12428718/ ; 'Prospective Assessment of the Efficacy of Wearable Technology in Postoperative Patient Monitoring and Early Complication Detection', Eur J Cardiovasc Med ; 'Preoperative Decline and Postoperative Recovery of Wearable-Derived Physical Activity in TKA and THA', Sensors 2026, doi:10.3390/s26113319


## Implications for backend

- Add a daily patient-reported wound channel as a first-class signal alongside vitals — a 5-point drainage scale (none/minimal/mild/moderate/heavy) plus erythema and dehiscence flags, captured for at least postoperative days 0–30. Score it with WEEK-CONDITIONAL logic, not a flat threshold: any drainage in postoperative week 2 (sens 88%/spec 88%), moderate-to-heavy drainage in week 3 (OR 103, PPV 83%), new-onset drainage appearing in week 2 (OR 80.7), cumulative >5 drainage days across weeks 1–3 (sens 63%/spec 87%). Encode the ICM definition (>2×2 cm on the dressing beyond 72 h) as the 'persistent' flag with escalation steps at >3 days (OR 39.4/day), >5 days, and >7 days. Absence of drainage across all 30 days should actively LOWER the infection composite: NPV >98%.
- Make every complication rule time-conditional on postoperative day, because identical values mean different things on different days. Concretely: suppress CRP-based infection logic entirely for POD 0–6 (proven non-discriminative) and apply decaying cutoffs 50.7 mg/L (≤30 d), 44.9 mg/L (≤45 d), 39.8 mg/L (≤90 d), ≥100 mg/L (≤6 weeks THA), ≥33 mg/L (6–12 weeks); weight the PE/VTE channel most heavily on POD 2–7 (median POD 3, IQR 2–7); keep the dislocation/mechanical channel at maximum sensitivity for the first 6 weeks after THA (91% of posterior-approach dislocations); suppress CRPS before week 12; and make the infection surveillance horizon a per-procedure config value — TKA must run to day 365 since only 57.7% of primary TKA PJIs declare by day 90 versus 76.8% for THA.
- Implement MSIS 2018 and EBJIS 2021 as explicit, separately-labeled scoring functions the clinician can inspect, since they disagree. MSIS: CRP >1 mg/dL=2, D-dimer >860 ng/mL=2, ESR >30 mm/h=1, synovial WBC >3000/µL=3, alpha-defensin S/CO >1.0=3, leukocyte esterase ++=3, synovial PMN >80%=2, synovial CRP >6.9 mg/L=1; >5 infected, 3–5 inconclusive, <3 not infected; plus major criteria (sinus tract, two matching cultures) as auto-infected. EBJIS: synovial WBC <1500/µL or PMN <65% = unlikely; >3000/µL or PMN >80% = confirmed. Store which system produced a verdict and never blend them — these artifacts are what make the product credible to a surgeon in ninety seconds.
- Split the alert taxonomy into TREND complications and EVENT complications, because they require different detectors. Trend (EWMA/CUSUM on vitals plus activity is appropriate): infection/PJI, VTE, delirium, AKI, general deterioration. Event (a step-change detector, not a control chart): dislocation, periprosthetic fracture, acute graft or repair failure — the correct rule is a same-day step-count cliff (e.g. >70% drop versus the patient's own 7-day median) plus an acute pain spike and, ideally, a fall signal. Running EWMA over these is a category error and will always fire late.
- Add ROM and functional-milestone trajectory modeling as a separate engine from the vitals engine, with per-procedure milestone curves and forward projection. TKA: weekly flexion from week 2, alert when projected week-6 flexion falls below 90° (the MUA threshold) with enough runway (week 3–4) to intervene; benchmark post-MUA gains of 28° at 6 weeks and 37° at 1 year. ACLR: terminal extension must reach 0° by week 4–6 — flag plateau or regression between weeks 6 and 12 as possible cyclops. Rotator cuff: flag ACTIVE elevation regression with preserved PASSIVE ROM between week 6 and month 3, the peak retear window. Lumbar fusion: flag failure of the pain curve to keep improving after month 3 (pseudarthrosis) and a NEW rise after a sustained good plateau (adjacent segment disease).
- Calibrate alert expectations to real base rates and store them as per-procedure priors: primary THA PJI 0.94%/yr, primary TKA 1.05%/yr, revision THA 4.39%, revision TKA 5.33%; 90-day symptomatic PE ~0.71%, 90-day symptomatic VTE after THA 1.11%; MUA ~4% of TKA; THA dislocation 0.5–1.1% by approach; delirium 13.6%; AKI 2–15%; ileus 0.7%; 30-day readmission 3.3–4.5%. At a 1% PJI base rate an 88%/88% rule yields a PPV near 7%, so the UI must present a tiered watch/contact/escalate ladder rather than a binary alarm, and reason codes should carry the interval-specific PPV the literature supports (e.g. 83% for moderate-to-heavy week-3 drainage).
- Set honest lead-time expectations in product copy and in the risk model. Vital-sign deviations buy hours, not days: median 14 h for HR+temperature telemetry, 7–11 h in prospective wearable cohorts, with typical magnitudes HR +14 bpm, RR +5 bpm, SpO2 −4%, skin temp +1.2°C. Days-scale lead time comes from wound reports and activity trajectories, not vitals. Encode persistent SpO2 <95% (or a sudden drop) as a PE screening trigger while labeling it screening-grade — the source study reported no sensitivity, specificity or AUC, and 80% of the PE it found was asymptomatic. Guard against device noise using the published wearable bias bounds (−1.6 bpm HR, +0.12 °C skin temp).
- Add a nocturnal/sleep-fragmentation detector aimed specifically at delirium in the POD 0–3 window for patients over 65 or flagged frail (frail 42.3% versus non-frail 7.8%), and pair it with a caregiver-facing CAM or 4AT prompt rather than an autonomous verdict. This is a high-value, currently-unused application of the sleep-duration channel already in the engine.
- Sex-stratify and procedure-stratify the expected activity recovery curves. Published data show men rising from ~4,970 to ~6,185 daily steps post-op while women fell from ~5,532 to ~4,652, and week-6 recovery of 86.7% versus 59.4% of preoperative steps depending on procedure — a single pooled logistic curve will systematically mislabel women and TKA patients as under-recovering. Also capture 4 weeks of PREOPERATIVE activity where possible, since preoperative activity cluster predicted both early recovery rate and long-term return to baseline.
- Build a procedure-specific readmission-risk surface rather than one generic model: after TKA weight medical causes (52.6% — circulatory, abnormal labs, digestive) and after THA weight surgical causes (50.7% — SSI/wound disruption first, VTE second). Return-to-baseline activity alone reached AUROC 0.76 (sens 75%, spec 69%) for 30-day readmission in a comparable surgical population, so the existing activity channel can carry real weight. Always label the benchmark's data source, since CMS and NSQIP rates are documented to disagree.

## Open questions

- No study found quantifies lead time in DAYS for continuous heart rate or skin temperature preceding PJI specifically in arthroplasty patients — the 14-hour and 7–11-hour figures come from mixed and colorectal surgical populations and one small feasibility study. Is there an arthroplasty-specific wearable-to-PJI lead-time study? If not, the product should not claim day-scale physiologic lead time for infection.
- Transfusion and post-op anemia rates for contemporary primary TKA/THA were not retrieved (the systemic complications review explicitly lacked them). Need current tranexamic-acid-era transfusion rates (likely 1–3%), the hemoglobin trigger in use, and whether resting HR elevation reliably tracks post-op anemia and at what Hb level.
- The Ranawat Award multicenter randomized MUA trial (J Arthroplasty 2024) was paywalled (HTTP 403). Its exact randomization threshold, timing arms and outcome deltas should be obtained — it is the most current high-quality evidence for the MUA decision rule and would directly parameterize the ROM projection alert.
- No source quantified how many days before clinical presentation a step-count collapse occurs for PJI, dislocation, or periprosthetic fracture. The step-cliff rule is proposed on mechanistic grounds; its sensitivity, specificity, and false-positive rate (ordinary bad days, travel, intercurrent illness) are unvalidated and should be measured on the platform's own data before it drives escalation.
- Wound photographs versus a 5-point self-reported drainage scale: the telemonitoring study used the scale, and its own automated alerts caught only 6 of 16 PJIs. Would photo-plus-scale capture improve PPV, and what is the regulatory posture of any image-based inference?
- The MSIS/EBJIS discordance (synovial WBC >3000/µL is confirmatory under EBJIS but a 3-point minor criterion under MSIS) means the same patient can be classified differently. Which system do the target surgeons actually use, and should the product default to one or always show both?
- AKI incidence spans 2–15% and urinary retention spans 0.07%–37% purely because of definitional differences. Which operational definitions do partner institutions use, so displayed base rates match their own experience rather than contradicting it?
- Post-op day-level timing data for wound dehiscence specifically (as distinct from persistent drainage) and for hematoma requiring evacuation was thin — only a 4.7% PWD-associated superficial hematoma figure from a 271-case series. Larger contemporary series would firm up these thresholds.
- Whether the composite deviation index should include the patient-reported wound channel at all, or whether wound findings should drive a SEPARATE, higher-precedence infection pathway. Given that drainage vastly outperforms any vital sign for PJI, diluting it inside a weighted composite may destroy the signal.
- ACL graft failure (rupture/revision) rates and their timing distribution, and lumbar fusion pseudarthrosis raw incidence at 1–2 years, were not directly retrieved — only reoperation rates and ASD incidence. These two numbers should be filled in before the ACL and spine risk models are calibrated.
