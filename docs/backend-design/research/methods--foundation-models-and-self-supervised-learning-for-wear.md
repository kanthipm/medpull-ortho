# Foundation models and self-supervised learning for wearable time series and health data — off-the-shelf vs. fine-tune vs. train, for MedPull Recovery Copilot (post-op orthopedic RTM, 10–1000 patients, rare outcome labels)

## Summary

The headline finding is a hard gate: every wearable foundation model that would actually help you (Google LSM/LSM-2, Apple's Wearable Behavior Model, PH-LLM) is closed — no weights, no API, explicitly withheld for consent/privacy reasons — and every wearable foundation model you CAN download requires an input modality you probably do not have. PaPaGei needs 125 Hz raw PPG in 10-second segments; Oxford SSL-Wearables needs 30 Hz tri-axial raw accelerometry in 10-second windows and is licensed academic-use-only (commercial use requires negotiating with Oxford University Innovation, a real blocker for a startup); Pulse-PPG likewise needs raw field PPG. If your aggregator API returns daily aggregates — resting HR, RMSSD, wrist temp, SpO2, RR, sleep minutes, steps — then literally zero downloadable wearable foundation model accepts your input format, and this is the single most important fact in this report. The closest architectural analogue to your data is Apple's WBM (27 behavioral metrics at hourly resolution, 168h windows, bi-directional Mamba-2, contrastive InfoNCE), which reports infection/illness detection at AUROC 0.749 and pregnancy at 0.864 — but it was pretrained on 161,855 participants and 2.5B hours and the authors state they cannot release weights or code. You cannot replicate that: LSM-2 used 3.58M person-days from 60,440 people; you will have ~0.1% of that. General time-series foundation models (Chronos-Bolt, TimesFM 2.0, TTM, Lag-Llama) ARE downloadable under Apache-2.0 and CPU-runnable, but they are univariate forecasters designed for long series, and with 30–90 daily points per patient they will not beat your existing per-patient EWMA/CUSUM residual machinery — and NeurIPS 2024 ablation work shows LLM-based forecasters do no better than the same architecture with the LLM deleted. For medical LLMs the evidence is equally deflationary: a systematic head-to-head with per-model prompt optimization found medical-adapted LLMs beat their own general base models in only 12.1% of 3-shot test cases and were worse in 38.2%. Verdict: keep classical statistics for deviation detection and risk tiering, use a good general instruction-tuned LLM with tight prompting and schema-constrained output for narrative and symptom extraction, do NOT fine-tune anything in year one, and spend the engineering budget on hierarchical Bayesian partial pooling and conformal calibration instead — those are the methods that genuinely work at n=10–1000 with single-digit-percent event rates.

## Findings

### Google LSM-2 is the closest technical match to your data and it is unobtainable
*[strong]*

LSM-2 (Xu, Narayanswamy et al., arXiv:2506.05321) pretrains a 25M-param 1D ViT (384-d hidden, 12 encoder / 4 decoder layers, patch = 10 minutes, input = 1440 minutes x 26 minutely-aggregated features -> 3744 tokens) on 3,581,748 person-days (~40M hours) from 60,440 people. Objective is Adaptive and Inherited Masking (AIM): union of real ('inherited') missingness with artificial masks — 80% random token imputation, 50% temporal-slice (device-off), 50% signal-slice (sensor-off), plus a fixed 50% token dropout for compute. Linear-probe results: hypertension AUROC 0.754 (vs LSM-1 0.739), anxiety 0.758, 20-class activity F1 0.474, age Pearson r 0.722, BMI r 0.673. Its big win is robustness to missingness: with PPG removed, hypertension AUROC drops only 6% vs 11% for LSM-1; average temporal-ablation F1 0.43 vs 0.26. No weights, no code, no API. Note the sobering ceiling: 3.5M person-days of the best multimodal wearable data in existence buys you AUROC 0.754 on hypertension.

> arXiv:2506.05321 (LSM-2: Learning from Incomplete Wearable Sensor Data), full HTML

### Google LSM-1 quantifies exactly how much label-efficiency a wearable foundation model buys — and it is large
*[strong]*

LSM-1 (arXiv:2410.13638, 'Scaling Wearable Foundation Models') pretrains on 40M hours from >165,000 people, six per-minute modalities (HR, HRV, EDA, accelerometer, skin temp, altimeter). Sizes: Tiny 2M / Small 7M / Base 110M / Large 328M. Few-shot: 5-shot exercise detection 54.7% accuracy vs 43.1% supervised-from-scratch; 20-shot activity recognition 51.2% mAP vs 18.5% supervised (a 2.8x gain). Full fine-tune: exercise detection 90.3% acc / 97.0% mAP; 8-class activity 68.5% acc. This is the strongest published argument FOR pretraining in your regime — a ~3x mAP gain at 20 labels per class — which makes it doubly unfortunate that the weights are not released. It also tells you what you are giving up by staying classical: roughly a 2-3x effective-sample-size multiplier on any supervised task.

> arXiv:2410.13638v1 full HTML (few-shot tables)

### Apple's Wearable Behavior Model proves daily/hourly AGGREGATES beat raw sensor embeddings for exactly your kind of task — but weights are withheld
*[strong]*

'Beyond Sensor Data' (arXiv:2507.00191): 27 behavioral metrics at HOURLY resolution (steps, exercise minutes, active/basal energy, standing time, flights, resting HR, walking HR, HRV, respiratory rate, SpO2, wrist temperature, walking speed, step length, steadiness, falls, VO2max, 6-minute walk distance, body mass, BMI) — nearly a superset of your feature vector. Input = 168 hours x 54 channels (27 values + 27 missingness masks). Architecture: Time Series Transformer tokenizer with global-mean imputation feeding a bi-directional Mamba-2 backbone; pretrained with regularized InfoNCE using subject-level positive pairs and token-dropout augmentation, on 15.14M person-weeks / 2.5B hours from 161,855 participants. Results: infection/illness AUROC 0.749, pregnancy 0.864, diabetes status 0.765, injury 0.680, sleep duration R^2 0.590, age MAE 3.67y, sex AUROC 0.999. Critically, WBM beat the raw-PPG foundation model on behavior-driven tasks (sleep duration R^2 0.590 vs 0.110) while PPG won on physiology (diabetes 0.829 vs 0.765); the fusion was best (pregnancy 0.921). Authors: 'We are unable to release model weights and code due to the specifics of the informed consent.' The transferable lesson: your daily-aggregate feature vector is a legitimate modeling substrate, not a degraded one — you do not need raw waveforms to do infection-adjacent detection.

> arXiv:2507.00191v1 full HTML

### Infection detection from wearables tops out near AUROC 0.75 even with 160k participants — this should set your product expectations
*[moderate]*

Apple WBM's 'infection/illness' time-varying task reached AUROC 0.749 and 'injury' 0.680 with 161,855 pretraining participants and ~25k-27k labelled evaluation subjects. Your post-op infection/readmission/MUA detection problem is harder (rarer, more confounded by surgical recovery itself) and you will have 3-4 orders of magnitude less data. Any internal target above ~0.75 AUROC for wearable-only early infection detection is not supported by the literature, and you should not promise clinicians better. Your existing composite index + EWMA/CUSUM is plausibly already in the same performance band, because most of the signal is in resting HR and skin temperature deviation, which control charts capture directly.

> arXiv:2507.00191v1 (time-varying health state task table)

### PH-LLM shows fine-tuning an LLM on health data helps narrative, but a strong general model was already at expert parity for one of two domains
*[strong]*

PH-LLM (arXiv:2406.06474) fine-tunes Gemini Ultra 1.0 for personal health. Key result: for FITNESS insights, 'Gemini Ultra 1.0 and PH-LLM are not statistically different from expert performance' — i.e. the un-fine-tuned general model already matched human experts. Fine-tuning gave significant gains only for sleep insights, where experts remained superior. Domain-knowledge exams: 79% sleep / 88% fitness, exceeding average human expert scores. Separately, the paper found that predicting self-reported sleep quality required MULTIMODAL ENCODING of the sensor data to match specialized discriminative models — text-serialized numbers were not enough. Two direct implications for you: (1) a good general LLM with good prompting is likely sufficient for your narrative task; (2) do not ask any LLM to compute or infer numeric risk from serialized sensor values — your existing 'LLM writes narrative only, never numbers' rule is exactly what this paper's negative result supports.

> arXiv:2406.06474 (Towards a Personal Health Large Language Model)

### PaPaGei is genuinely downloadable but needs 125 Hz raw PPG in 10-second segments — likely unavailable to you
*[strong]*

PaPaGei (Nokia Bell Labs, arXiv:2410.20542; github.com/nokia-bell-labs/papagei-foundation-model). Pretrained on 57,000 hours / 20M unlabeled PPG segments from public datasets. Architecture: ResNet1D with mixture-of-experts (ResNet1DMoE, n_experts=3), 512-d embedding output. Objective is morphology-aware (stress-induced sVRI/IPA signal-quality proxies) rather than plain SimCLR contrastive. Reported: +6.3% classification / +2.9% regression over SOTA on at least 14 of 20 tasks across 10 datasets (cardiovascular, sleep, pregnancy, wellbeing), beating models 70x larger. Pipeline: flatline detection via BioBSS -> bandpass filter -> resample to 125 Hz -> 10s segments. Weights on Zenodo record 13983110 (papagei_s.pt), repo BSD-3-Clause but the paper carries CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — the weight license is ambiguous and MUST be confirmed in writing before any commercial deployment. HARD BLOCKER for you: consumer wearable aggregator APIs (Apple HealthKit, Fitbit Web API, Google Health Connect, Terra, Vital) do not expose raw 125 Hz PPG. Unless you ship your own sensor or ingest a research-grade device, PaPaGei is unusable.

> arXiv:2410.20542 + github.com/nokia-bell-labs/papagei-foundation-model README

### Oxford SSL-Wearables works and is easy to load, but the licence is academic-use-only and the input is 30 Hz raw accelerometry
*[strong]*

OxWearables/ssl-wearables (Yuan et al., npj Digital Medicine 2024; arXiv:2206.02909). Pretrained on 700,000 person-days of UK Biobank accelerometry using multi-task SSL pretext tasks. Variants harnet5 / harnet10 / harnet30 = 150 / 300 / 900 samples at 30 Hz (5/10/30s windows), 3-channel input, shape (batch, 3, samples). One-line load: torch.hub.load('OxWearables/ssl-wearables','harnet10',class_num=5,pretrained=True). Fine-tuned mean F1: ADL .829 (+100% rel.), PAMAP2 .789 (+30.4%), Opportunity .595 (+55.4%), REALWORLD .792 (+12.3%), WISDM .810 (+18.4%), Rowlands .796 (+14.4%), Capture-24 .726 (+2.5%); median relative F1 improvement 18.4%. LICENCE: academic use only — 'commercial use requires negotiation with Oxford University Innovation Limited'. For a for-profit RTM platform this is a legal blocker, not a formality. And again: 30 Hz raw accel is not what a daily-aggregate API returns.

> github.com/OxWearables/ssl-wearables README + arXiv:2206.02909

### Pulse-PPG shows field-collected pretraining beats clinical pretraining, but release status is a promise not a fact
*[moderate]*

Pulse-PPG (arXiv:2502.01108): first open-source PPG foundation model pretrained on RAW field data — a 100-day field study of 120 participants — rather than curated clinical recordings. Central finding: pretraining on messy field data 'surprisingly outperforms' pretraining on clinical data on many downstream tasks, i.e. domain match to deployment noise matters more than data cleanliness. The abstract states the authors 'plan to release' the model; release and licence were not confirmed at the paper page. Same raw-PPG input barrier as PaPaGei. Practical takeaway you CAN use without the model: if you ever do pretrain, pretrain on your own noisy production data distribution, not on a clean public cohort.

> arXiv:2502.01108

### Chronos-Bolt is the only general time-series foundation model that is simultaneously Apache-2.0, tiny, CPU-fast, and quantile-native
*[strong]*

amazon/chronos-bolt-{tiny 9M, mini 21M, small 48M, base 205M}, Apache-2.0, T5-efficient encoder-decoder trained on ~100B time-series observations, direct multi-step quantile forecasting (output shape [num_series, num_quantiles, prediction_length]). Up to 250x faster and 20x more memory-efficient than original Chronos; Bolt-Base beats Chronos-Large on accuracy at >600x the speed. Input is a plain 1D tensor (or list of tensors), no frequency metadata required. Usable via `chronos-forecasting` (BaseChronosPipeline.from_pretrained) or AutoGluon-TimeSeries. Limitation for you: univariate only — it forecasts resting HR from resting HR history, with no channel mixing and no covariates in the base pipeline, so it cannot express 'HRV dropped AND temp rose'. With 30-90 daily points per patient you are far below the 512-2048 context these models were tuned for.

> huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-bolt-base model card

### TimesFM 2.0 is Apache-2.0 but univariate with uncalibrated quantiles; Moirai 2.0 is licence-blocked; Lag-Llama is tiny and needs fine-tuning; TimeGPT is API-only
*[strong]*

google/timesfm-2.0-500m-pytorch: 500M params, Apache-2.0, max context 2048, flexible horizon (recommend horizon <= context), UNIVARIATE only, requires a categorical frequency flag (0=daily-or-finer, 1=weekly/monthly, 2=quarterly+), and its 10 quantile heads are explicitly 'not calibrated after pretraining' — so do not use them for alerting thresholds. Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small: 11.4M params, decoder-only, multi-token prediction, quantile loss — but CC-BY-NC-4.0, i.e. NON-COMMERCIAL, disqualified for a startup product. Lag-Llama: 2.45M params, Apache-2.0, probabilistic, trained at context length 32, 'performs best when finetuned', tune context 32-1024 and LR 1e-2 to 5e-4 with 50-epoch early-stopping patience; the tiny context (32) is actually a decent fit for 30-90 daily points, but 2.45M params of generic pretraining will not beat a per-patient EWMA. TimeGPT (Nixtla): hosted API or self-hosted enterprise deployment, no open weights, supports exogenous variables and has a built-in anomaly-detection endpoint — but no published HIPAA/BAA statement on the docs page, which makes sending post-op patient vitals to it a compliance question you must resolve before evaluation, not after.

> HF model cards: google/timesfm-2.0-500m-pytorch, Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small, time-series-foundation-models/Lag-Llama; nixtla.io/docs

### IBM Granite Tiny Time Mixers (TTM) is the one general TSFM whose design actually fits your constraints
*[moderate]*

ibm-granite/granite-timeseries-ttm-r2, Apache-2.0, models start at ~1M parameters (not billions), runs on laptops and CPU-only machines — meaning it can plausibly live inside an AWS Lambda container image alongside your existing engine. Unlike Chronos/TimesFM it explicitly supports MULTIVARIATE forecasting with channel-independence AND decoder channel-mixing enabled at fine-tune time, exogenous-variable infusion, and static categorical infusion (this is how you would inject procedure type, age, BMI). Branch naming is <context_length>-<prediction_length>-<freq_tuning>-<loss>-<release>; r2 covers minutely-to-hourly and r2.1 adds DAILY/WEEKLY resolution — the r2.1 daily variants are the only foundation-model weights in this entire report that natively accept daily health aggregates. Reported to beat TimesFM, Moirai and Chronos zero/few-shot while being orders of magnitude smaller, and to reach competitive accuracy fine-tuned on just 5% of training data. Install: pip install granite-tsfm (tsfm_public).

> huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-timeseries-ttm-r2 + arXiv:2401.03955 (Tiny Time Mixers)

### The NeurIPS 2024 ablation: deleting the LLM from an LLM-based forecaster usually IMPROVES it
*[strong]*

'Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?' (arXiv:2406.16964, NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight) ablated three published LLM-based forecasters by removing the LLM component or replacing it with a single basic attention layer. In most cases performance IMPROVED after removal; pretrained LLMs did no better than the same architecture trained from scratch, and simple patching + attention matched LLM-based forecasters at a fraction of the compute. Conclusion: pretrained LLMs do not represent sequential dependencies in time series. Direct instruction for MedPull: do not build any pipeline that serializes vitals into text for an LLM to forecast or score. Keep numbers in numpy/scipy and keep the LLM strictly on prose, exactly as your engine already does.

> arXiv:2406.16964

### Medical-adapted LLMs beat their own base models only 12.1% of the time — buy prompting, not fine-tuning
*[strong]*

'Medical Adaptation of Large Language and Vision-Language Models: Are We Making Progress?' (arXiv:2411.04118) compared seven public medical LLMs and two medical VLMs head-to-head against their exact base models, with SEPARATE prompt optimization per model (the key methodological fix — prior papers optimized prompts for the medical model only) and explicit statistical-uncertainty accounting. Result in the 3-shot setting: medical models outperformed base in 12.1% of cases, were statistically comparable in 49.8%, and were WORSE in 38.2%. All medical VLMs and nearly all medical LLMs failed to consistently improve over base. Authors' conclusion: state-of-the-art general models already carry strong medical knowledge and reasoning. This is the single most decision-relevant paper for your narrative and extraction tasks and it says: do not fine-tune a medical LLM, and do not swap your general model for Meditron/BioMistral/OpenBioLLM on faith.

> arXiv:2411.04118 (EMNLP 2024)

### MedGemma is the best-supported open medical LLM and still is not right for your two LLM tasks
*[strong]*

MedGemma (arXiv:2507.05201) = Gemma 3 4B and 27B adapted for medicine; weights on HuggingFace under Health AI Developer Foundations terms (a click-through licence, NOT Apache-2.0 — review the acceptable-use clauses before commercial deployment). MedGemma 27B text: MedQA 87.7% 0-shot vs Gemma 3 27B's 74.9%, MedMCQA 74.2% vs 62.6%, PubMedQA 76.8% vs 73.4%, MMLU-Med 87.0% vs 83.3% (with test-time scaling). Paper-level gains: 2.6-10% on medical multimodal QA, 15.5-18.1% on CXR finding classification, 50% error reduction on EHR retrieval after fine-tuning. Model card states outputs 'are not intended to directly inform clinical diagnosis, patient management decisions, treatment recommendations'. Serving the 27B unquantized needs Colab-Enterprise-class GPU (~A100 80GB or 2xL4 quantized) — that is not an AWS Lambda workload and would cost roughly $700-1500/month on a dedicated GPU instance versus cents per call on your existing Groq path. Its wins are on exam-style multiple-choice QA and imaging, neither of which is your task; your tasks are style-controlled prose and structured extraction, where the 12.1% finding applies.

> arXiv:2507.05201 + huggingface.co/google/medgemma-27b-text-it

### OpenBioLLM / Meditron / BioMistral / Med-PaLM 2 — sizes, licences, and why their benchmark claims should not move you
*[moderate]*

OpenBioLLM-70B (aaditya/Llama3-OpenBioLLM-70B): Llama-3-70B-Instruct base, QLoRA + DPO on 8xH100 for 4 epochs, Meta Llama 3 licence; claims 86.06% average across 9 biomedical datasets vs GPT-4 82.85% and Med-PaLM 2 84.08% — but these are self-reported multiple-choice benchmarks with documented contamination risk, the model card itself warns against clinical use and notes no RCT or real-world validation, and the 12.1% head-to-head finding above covers exactly this class of claim. Meditron 7B/70B (EPFL, Llama-2 base, continued pretraining on PubMed + guidelines) and BioMistral-7B (Mistral-7B base, Apache-2.0, PubMed Central continued pretraining) are among the models covered by that negative head-to-head result. Med-PaLM 2 has NO public weights and no general API — it is not procurable and should be treated as literature only. Net: none of these is a defensible swap for a well-prompted general instruction model on prose generation or JSON extraction, and adopting a 70B open model would replace a cents-per-call API with a five-figure annual GPU bill.

> huggingface.co/aaditya/Llama3-OpenBioLLM-70B model card; arXiv:2411.04118

### The SSL objective you would actually use for sparse daily multivariate health data is masked reconstruction with an explicit missingness mask, not SimCLR
*[moderate]*

Two independent large-scale results converge here. LSM-2's AIM treats real missingness and artificial masking as a union so the encoder never sees an imputed value it believes; this is what produced the missingness-robustness gains (temporal-ablation F1 0.43 vs 0.26). Apple's WBM concatenates an explicit per-channel missingness mask (27 values + 27 mask channels = 54) and uses contrastive InfoNCE with SUBJECT-LEVEL positives — i.e. two different weeks from the same person are a positive pair — plus token dropout as the only augmentation. Note what both AVOIDED: generic SimCLR augmentations (jitter, scaling, time-warp, permutation) are not the mechanism at scale; subject identity is the free label. CLOCS (arXiv:2005.13249, ICML 2021) established the same principle for cardiac signals — patient-specific positive pairs across space/time/patients — and reported strong generalization with only 25% of labelled training data, consistently beating BYOL and SimCLR on both linear evaluation and fine-tuning. For MedPull the practical version is: if you ever self-supervise, use masked reconstruction over your 6-8 daily channels with an explicit missingness mask, plus same-patient-different-week contrastive positives. But see the sample-size finding — this needs ~10^4 person-weeks to pay off, and you will have ~10^3 at best.

> arXiv:2506.05321 (AIM); arXiv:2507.00191 (InfoNCE + mask channels); arXiv:2005.13249 (CLOCS)

### TabPFN v2 is the one foundation model built for your actual constraint — small n, tabular, rare labels
*[moderate]*

TabPFN (Nature 2025, 'Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model', DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6; github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN) is a prior-data-fitted transformer pretrained on millions of synthetic tabular datasets that performs in-context learning: you pass train rows + test rows in a single forward pass, no gradient training. Explicitly designed for the small-data regime where tuned gradient boosting needs hours of HPO. v2 handles up to 100,000 rows x 2,000 features; CPU inference is viable to ~1,000-5,000 samples, which is your entire dataset. pip install tabpfn, Python 3.10+. LICENCE WARNING: the code is under the Prior Labs License (Apache-2.0 plus attribution) but the newest default checkpoint (TabPFN-3) is NON-COMMERCIAL — you must pin an earlier commercially-licensed checkpoint and get that in writing. Known gap: the docs do not address class imbalance, so with 3% event rates you must still handle rare-label calibration yourself (stratified CV, calibration curves, and precision-at-fixed-recall rather than accuracy).

> github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN README + Nature 2025 DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6

### Sample-size arithmetic: you are 3-4 orders of magnitude short of pretraining, and 1-2 orders short of fine-tuning
*[strong]*

Pretraining cohorts: LSM-1 165,000 people / 40M hours; LSM-2 60,440 people / 3.58M person-days; Apple WBM 161,855 people / 15.14M person-weeks; Yuan et al. 700,000 person-days; PaPaGei 57,000 hours / 20M segments. Your ceiling at 1,000 patients x 90 post-op days = 90,000 person-days = 2.5% of LSM-2 and 0.06% of Apple's hour count. Training a wearable foundation model from scratch is therefore off the table permanently, not just for now. Fine-tuning a released model is also gated: with single-digit-percent event rates, 1,000 patients yields ~30-80 positive outcome events total. Standard events-per-variable guidance (>=10-20 events per estimated parameter) means you can honestly fit a model with roughly 3-8 free parameters. That is a logistic regression or a shallow calibrated scorer — not a fine-tuned transformer, and not even a linear probe on a 512-d embedding unless you first reduce to <=8 principal components.

> Cohort sizes from arXiv:2410.13638, 2506.05321, 2507.00191, 2206.02909, 2410.20542


## Implications for backend

- INPUT FORMAT IS THE GATE — resolve it before any model decision. Audit exactly what your aggregator returns: if it is daily aggregates only, then PaPaGei (125 Hz PPG), Pulse-PPG (raw PPG), and SSL-Wearables (30 Hz tri-axial accel) are all structurally inapplicable and should be removed from the roadmap, not deferred. The only foundation-model weights in this entire survey that natively accept daily multivariate health aggregates are IBM Granite TTM r2.1 daily branches (Apache-2.0, ~1M params). Write this audit down as an ADR in the engine directory so the question is not re-litigated every quarter.
- NOTHING IN THE WEARABLE-FM LANDSCAPE CHANGES YOUR ENGINE ARCHITECTURE. Google LSM/LSM-2, Apple WBM, and PH-LLM are all closed — Apple states outright that participant informed consent prevents releasing weights or code, which means these will likely never open. Your existing EWMA/CUSUM + composite index + rule-based tiering is the correct architecture for your data scale, and the honest performance ceiling for wearable-only illness detection in the literature is ~AUROC 0.75 even at 160k participants. Do not let FM hype trigger a rewrite.
- THE HIGHEST-ROI CHANGE IS STATISTICAL, NOT ML: replace your fixed SD floors with hierarchical Bayesian partial pooling (numpyro on CPU, fit nightly offline, cache posterior means per patient). This directly fixes the short-baseline problem the SD floor is currently masking, works from ~10 patients per procedure, requires zero labels, and is fully explainable to a clinician. Budget one engineer-week. Same technique upgrades the fixed per-procedure logistic recovery curve into a per-patient curve with credible intervals, turning your ratio-to-expected threshold into a calibrated posterior-predictive-percentile flag.
- FREEZE THE LLM SURFACE AREA AND HARDEN IT INSTEAD OF UPGRADING IT. The evidence says medical-adapted LLMs beat their own base models in only 12.1% of head-to-head 3-shot cases, and that deleting the LLM from a time-series forecaster usually improves it. So: (a) do not adopt MedGemma/OpenBioLLM/Meditron/BioMistral — a 27B or 70B model also converts a cents-per-call Groq path into a four-to-five-figure annual GPU bill; (b) never serialize vitals into a prompt for scoring or forecasting; (c) DO add a numeric-consistency validator that regex-extracts every number in generated narrative and asserts membership in the input payload, with retry-once-then-deterministic-template fallback. That validator enforces your 'LLM writes narrative only, never numbers' invariant mechanically rather than by prompt convention, and it belongs in the test suite alongside your pinned golden tiers.
- PLAN THE LABEL PIPELINE NOW, BECAUSE LABELS — NOT MODELS — ARE YOUR BINDING CONSTRAINT. At 1,000 patients and single-digit-percent event rates you will have ~30-80 positive outcomes, which supports a model with roughly 3-8 free parameters. Concretely: build outcome capture (infection, readmission, MUA, with dates) into the clinical workflow from day one; always split by patient (GroupKFold on patient_id, never row-level); report precision-at-fixed-recall rather than AUROC; and wrap the eventual classifier in split-conformal calibration (MAPIE) so the 'high' tier carries a stated false-alarm guarantee. Set the gates explicitly: >=50 events unlocks penalized logistic regression, >=200 events unlocks benchmarking TabPFN v2.
- DEPLOYMENT REALITY ON LAMBDA: any torch-based model (TTM, Chronos-Bolt-tiny, TabPFN) pulls in a ~2GB torch dependency, which exceeds the 250MB zipped Lambda layer limit — you would need a Lambda container image (10GB limit) or to move inference to Fargate/ECS, plus cold-start budget. Weigh that infrastructure change against the fact that the models in question are not expected to beat your existing numpy/scipy engine at your data scale. numpyro/jax on CPU is the lighter path and produces posteriors you can precompute offline and cache in SQLite, keeping the request path pure-numpy.
- RUN LICENCE REVIEW BEFORE TECHNICAL EVALUATION, NOT AFTER. Disqualified or gated for a commercial RTM product: Oxford SSL-Wearables (academic-use only, commercial use requires negotiating with Oxford University Innovation Limited), Moirai 2.0 (CC-BY-NC-4.0), TabPFN-3 default checkpoint (non-commercial — pin an earlier commercially-licensed checkpoint), PaPaGei (repo BSD-3-Clause but paper CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; weight licence ambiguous, get it in writing), MedGemma (Health AI Developer Foundations click-through terms, not Apache-2.0), OpenBioLLM (Meta Llama 3 licence). Clean Apache-2.0: Chronos-Bolt, TimesFM 2.0, Lag-Llama, IBM Granite TTM. Separately, TimeGPT is API-only with no published HIPAA/BAA statement — sending post-op patient vitals to it is a compliance decision, not an engineering one.

## Recommended stack

- **Deviation detection on daily vitals (resting HR, HRV RMSSD, wrist temp, SpO2, RR, sleep) — your current EWMA + CUSUM** — KEEP CLASSICAL. Retain EWMA (lambda=0.3, L=2.66, flag on >=2 consecutive OOC days) and one-sided CUSUM (k=0.5, h=5.0, 14-day window). Add ONE upgrade: replace the per-patient SD-floor baseline with a hierarchical Bayesian partial-pooling baseline — patient-level mean and SD drawn from a procedure-level hyperprior — so a patient with 5 baseline days borrows strength from the cohort instead of falling back to a hand-set floor.
