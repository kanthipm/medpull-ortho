# Laboratory values and structured clinical data for orthopedic post-operative monitoring: CRP/ESR kinetics after TKA/THA, PJI diagnostic criteria (MSIS 2018 / EBJIS 2021 / ICM), supporting labs, and EHR code systems (LOINC, ICD-10-CM, CPT, SNOMED CT, RxNorm/MME)

## Summary

The single most actionable signal in this domain is the shape of the serum CRP curve, not any single value. After uncomplicated TKA/THA, CRP rises from a normal <5–10 mg/L baseline to a peak on post-op day (POD) 2–3 of roughly 100–185 mg/L (large THA cohort n=7,009: 56.9 mg/L POD1, 134.1 mg/L POD3, 70.4 mg/L POD5, 58.5 mg/L POD6), then falls steeply through POD5–7 and normalizes at 3–6 weeks (only ~35% of TKA patients normalized at 2 weeks, 80.8% at 4 weeks). Critically, the best available evidence says single-timepoint CRP in the first post-op week is a *poor* discriminator for acute PJI: ROC thresholds of 152 mg/L on POD3 (sens 75%, spec 67%, AUC 0.714) and 73 mg/L on POD5 (sens 76%, spec 61%, AUC 0.704), and a 2025 THA study found AUCs of 0.462–0.625 for pre-op CRP and delta-CRP. This means our engine should treat CRP as a *trajectory-deviation* feature — failure to fall below roughly 50% of peak by POD5–7, or any new rise after POD5–7 — rather than a threshold alarm, and must gate the alert on low PPV given a ~0.5–1.5% PJI base rate. Formal PJI definitions are numerically precise and should be encoded directly: MSIS 2018 is an additive score (serum CRP >10 mg/L = 2 pts, D-dimer >860 ng/mL = 2, ESR >30 mm/h = 1, synovial WBC >3,000/µL = 3, alpha-defensin S/CO >1 = 3, leukocyte esterase ++ = 3, PMN >80% = 2, synovial CRP >6.9 mg/L = 1; ≥6 = infected, 2–5 = inconclusive, sens 97.7% / spec 99.5%); EBJIS 2021 is a three-level model with synovial WBC <1,500/µL and PMN <65% as rule-out and >3,000/µL and >80% as rule-in; ICM uses relaxed acute (<90-day) thresholds of CRP ~100 mg/L, synovial WBC 10,000/µL and PMN 90%, with 6,130 cells/µL performing better (sens 91%, spec 83%). Almost none of this arrives in near-real time: inpatient CBC/BMP land within hours via FHIR, but post-discharge CRP/ESR only exist if someone orders them, typically at the 2-week wound check or 6-week visit — so the realistic architecture is a sparse, irregularly-sampled lab series fused with the dense wearable stream, not a second dense channel. For ingestion, the load-bearing code systems are LOINC (CRP 1988-5, ESR 30341-2, procalcitonin 33959-8), ICD-10-CM T84.5- (PJI) / T84.0- (mechanical) / T81.4- (SSI) / I82.4- and I26.- (VTE), CPT 27447 / 27130 / 29888 / 29827 / 22633, and RxNorm + CDC 2022 MME factors (hydromorphone 5.0, methadone 4.7, tramadol 0.2 — all changed in 2022) for opioid burden.

## Findings

### Normal CRP kinetics after THA: peak POD2–3, large-cohort per-day values
*[strong]*

Retrospective THA cohort n=7,042 (7,009 non-infected, 33 PJI), Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2022 (Kuhn et al.). Non-infected mean CRP: pre-op 3.5 ± 3.9 mg/L; POD1 56.9 ± 32.6; POD3 134.1 ± 63.9 (peak); POD5 70.4 ± 39.3; POD6 58.5 ± 36.4. PJI group: pre-op 5.9 ± 5.9 (p=0.004); POD1 43.3 ± 8.4 (p=0.44, NOT different); POD3 181.6 ± 64.7 (p=0.001); POD5 96.3 ± 40.9 (p=0.001); POD6 69.2 ± 26.7 (p=0.077). Note the source table is labelled mg/dL but the magnitudes are unambiguously mg/L. Complementary series (Neumaier/Scherer, meta-summary): CRP peaks between 66 and 185 mg/L at mean day 2.26; rapid decrease from peak POD2–3 through POD5–7; consistent normalization by 3 weeks.

> Kuhn et al., 'C-reactive protein during the first 6 postoperative days after total hip arthroplasty cannot predict early periprosthetic infection', Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2022 (PMID 35943586) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10191946/

### Single-timepoint CRP in the first post-op week is a POOR discriminator for acute PJI — encode as trajectory, not threshold
*[strong]*

Same THA cohort ROC analysis: POD3 threshold 152.1 mg/L → sensitivity 75%, specificity 67%, AUC 0.714. POD5 threshold 73.2 mg/L → sensitivity 76%, specificity 61%, AUC 0.704. The best-performing dynamic feature was the magnitude of decline from CRP-max to CRP-min: a decline of 102.7 mg/L reached only 'fair' accuracy. Authors' conclusion verbatim: 'AUC analysis of the ROC showed in almost all postoperative cases only a poor diagnostic accuracy.' Independently, a 2025 primary THA study (n=239, 10 PJI, median 63-month follow-up) reported pre-op CRP cutoff 0.235 mg/dL AUC 0.625 (sens 0.80/spec 0.476), absolute ΔCRP cutoff 7.785 mg/dL AUC 0.570, relative CRP increase 44-fold AUC 0.462 — all 'poor', 'only slightly more informative than chance'. Practical consequence: with a 0.5–1.5% acute PJI base rate, a 75%/67% rule yields PPV ≈ 1–3%. Any CRP alert we ship must be a 'get a lab/aspirate' nudge, never an infection claim.

> Kuhn et al. 2022, Arch Orthop Trauma Surg https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10191946/ ; 'Prognostic Value of C-Reactive Protein in Primary Total Hip Arthroplasty', 2025 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11851395/

### TKA CRP/IL-6 trajectory under ERAS — peak shifts to 72h and IL-6 peaks earlier at 48h
*[strong]*

Prospective TKA cohort n=100 under ERAS (no tourniquet, TXA), BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2020. CRP (mg/L, mean ± SD): 12h pre-op 3.35 ± 2.11; 12h post-op 25.17 ± 20.47; 48h 66.57 ± 43.11; 72h 75.97 ± 40.04 (PEAK); 2 weeks 6.67 ± 5.23 (returned to baseline, p=0.816). IL-6 (pg/mL): pre-op 3.28 ± 2.57; 12h 54.46 ± 77.88; 48h 56.29 ± 41.20 (PEAK); 72h 32.23 ± 21.32; 2 weeks 6.59 ± 5.24 (p=0.943). IL-6 peaks ~24h before CRP and normalizes faster — it is the better early kinetic marker but is rarely ordered outpatient. Separate TKA series (n=400 knees, navigation-assisted, no complications): pre-op 4.35 ± 5.04 mg/L; POD5–7 50.49 ± 37.54; day 12–14 10.92 ± 14.48; day 25–30 3.67 ± 6.33. Normalization (<5 mg/L): 35% at 2 weeks (140/400), 80.8% at 4 weeks (323/400). Peak TKA CRP reported elsewhere as 98.2 mg/L (SD 55.6) for primary TKA and 161–163 mg/L on POD2/POD4 in older non-ERAS series.

> Zhu et al., 'Normal trajectory of Interleukin-6 and C-reactive protein in the perioperative period of TKA under an ERAS scenario', BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2020 https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7175526/ ; 'C-reactive protein course after complication free TKA using navigation' https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7559145/

### Procedure magnitude drives peak CRP; navigation/resurfacing/minimal-bone-work procedures peak lower — per-procedure baselines are required
*[strong]*

Acta Orthop study, n=102 across 4 arthroplasty types (THA n=31, TKA n=36, hip resurfacing n=19, navigation TKA n=16): CRP peaked POD2 in all groups; conventional TKA highest, navigation-assisted TKA significantly lower than conventional TKA, hip resurfacing statistically significantly lower than THA. Authors attribute magnitude to extent of bone and marrow injury, not anatomic site. Explicit clinical rule stated: healthy adult plasma CRP <10 mg/L, and 'if the value remains high or if there is a new rise on days 7 or 8, systemic infection should be suspected.' Shoulder arthroplasty (n=280, JCM 2020) gives the cleanest published per-POD curve: pre-op <0.5 mg/dL; POD1 3.1 (IQR 1.9–4.2); POD2 8.9 (5–11.5); POD3 9.6 (5.3–13.1) PEAK; POD4 7.3 (3.9–9.7); POD5 5.0 (2.6–9.8); POD6 4.3 (1.9–5.4); POD7 3.7 (2.0–4.6) mg/dL. Peak on POD2–3 in 92% of patients. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty peaked at 12.0 mg/dL POD3 vs TSA 8.7 mg/dL POD2 vs hemiarthroplasty 6.9–9.0 mg/dL POD2; highest single value 23.9 mg/dL. Sex, BMI, operating time and cement use had no significant effect.

> 'C-reactive protein levels after 4 types of arthroplasty', Acta Orthop https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2823221/ ; 'Postoperative Trends of Serum CRP after Primary Shoulder Arthroplasty', J Clin Med 2020 https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7760886/

### MSIS 2018 / ICM evidence-based PJI definition — exact additive score and performance
*[strong]*

Parvizi et al., J Arthroplasty 2018;33(5):1309-1314.e2. MAJOR criteria (either = PJI): two positive periprosthetic cultures with the same organism, OR a sinus tract communicating with the joint. MINOR pre-op score: serum CRP >1 mg/dL (=10 mg/L) = 2 pts; serum D-dimer >860 ng/mL = 2 pts; ESR >30 mm/h = 1 pt; synovial WBC >3,000 cells/µL = 3 pts; synovial alpha-defensin signal-to-cutoff ratio >1.0 = 3 pts; leukocyte esterase ++ = 3 pts; synovial PMN% >80% = 2 pts; synovial CRP >6.9 mg/L = 1 pt. Score ≥6 = infected; 2–5 = inconclusive → add intra-operative findings: positive histology = 3 pts, purulence = 3 pts, single positive culture = 2 pts; combined ≥6 infected, 4–5 inconclusive, ≤3 not infected. Validated sensitivity 97.7%, specificity 99.5%, vs MSIS 2011 sensitivity 79.3% and ICM 2013 sensitivity 86.9%. Shohat et al. MSIS-based algorithm: sens 96.9%, spec 99.5% on 422 PJI / 820 non-PJI.

> Parvizi et al., 'The 2018 Definition of Periprosthetic Hip and Knee Infection: An Evidence-Based and Validated Criteria', J Arthroplasty 2018 (PMID 29551303) https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883540318302262

### EBJIS 2021 three-level definition — rule-out vs rule-in thresholds differ from MSIS
*[strong]*

McNally et al., Bone Joint J 2021. Three levels: infection unlikely / likely / confirmed. Synovial fluid rule-OUT (infection unlikely, if nothing else positive): total leukocytes <1,500 cells/µL OR PMN <65%. Rule-IN (>95% specificity, confirmed): leukocytes ≥3,000 cells/µL AND PMN ≥80% (some presentations use ≥75%). CONFIRMED also by any of: sinus tract or visible prosthesis; positive alpha-defensin immunoassay or lateral flow; two positive samples growing an identical organism; histology showing ≥5 neutrophils in ≥5 high-power fields; positive periprosthetic tissue or sonication-fluid culture. LIKELY = one Group A finding (radiological loosening within 5 years, prior wound healing problems, recent fever/bacteraemia, purulence, serum CRP >1 mg/dL) PLUS one Group B finding (elevated synovial leukocytes/differential, positive synovial culture, single positive intra-op culture, >1 CFU/mL from sonication fluid, ≥5 neutrophils in a single HPF, positive WBC scintigraphy). Applied prospectively: 75.9% confirmed, 18.8% likely, 5.3% unlikely; EBJIS left 8% inconclusive vs 15% for ICM. Independent ROC in a 2023 JBJI study: synovial WBC optimal cutoff 1,470 cells/µL AUC 0.94; PMN 62.5% AUC 0.91; synovial CRP 2.7 mg/L AUC 0.90; adenosine deaminase 60 U/L AUC 0.82; alpha-2-macroglobulin 420 mg/L AUC 0.76. A 2023 Arch Orthop Trauma Surg paper argues ICM thresholds are too high, proposing WBC 2,479/µL and PMN 67%.

> McNally et al., EBJIS definition of PJI (2021); applied in 'Prosthetic joint infection diagnosis applying the three-level EBJIS approach' https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9033695/ ; JBJI 2023;8:109 https://jbji.copernicus.org/articles/8/109/2023/ ; Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2023 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00402-023-04822-0

### Acute (<90 day) PJI uses relaxed thresholds — and the classic 10,000/µL cutoff underperforms
*[strong]*

Acute post-op window (<90 days, some definitions <6 weeks): serum CRP >100 mg/L (vs <10 mg/L normal) is the trigger for aspiration; classic synovial thresholds 10,000 WBC/µL and 90% PMN. Measured performance of the 10,000/µL cutoff in acute PJI is poor: sensitivity ~59.6%. Optimized acute cutoff at 90 days post-op = 6,130 cells/µL → sensitivity 91%, specificity 83%. Chronic PJI: >3,000 leukocytes/µL with 65–80% PMN → sensitivity 100%, specificity 98%, accuracy 99%. Joint- and phase-specific serum cutoffs from the same review: acute PJI CRP 23.5–38.9 mg/L overall, hip-specific 13.5 mg/L, knee-specific 23.5 mg/L, TKA at 6–12 weeks 34 mg/L; acute ESR hip 48.5 mm/h, knee 46.5 mm/h; ESR proposed optimal cutoff 10 mm/h in some series. Late/chronic PJI CRP sensitivity falls to 62.3%. Timing taxonomy: early PJI <3 weeks, delayed 3 weeks–3/6 months, late/chronic >3–6 months.

> 'An overview of the current diagnostic approach to Periprosthetic Joint Infections', Orthopedic Reviews 2024 https://orthopedicreviews.openmedicalpublishing.org/article/120308-an-overview-of-the-current-diagnostic-approach-to-periprosthetic-joint-infections

### Comparative diagnostic performance of serum and synovial PJI biomarkers (pick what to model, and what to ignore)
*[strong]*

Serum: CRP sens 74–94% / spec 20–100% (wide, timing-dependent); ESR sens 42–94% / spec 33–87%; D-dimer sens 89% / spec 93% single-study, meta-analysis 89%/76%; IL-6 meta-analysis sens 97% / spec 91%, chronic-PJI spec 88.3%; IL-6 + CRP combined sens 100% / spec 99% / accuracy 98%; procalcitonin sens 33–90% / spec 27–98% with 'no significant diagnostic advantage over CRP' — deprioritize PCT. Synovial: alpha-defensin sens 96–100% / spec >90%, quantitative ELISA superior to lateral flow, and resistant to prior antibiotics; calprotectin sens 92% / spec 93%; D-lactate sens 92.4% / spec 88.6%; synovial CRP combined with serum sens 74.6% / spec 98%. Microbiology: standard tissue culture sens 54%, post-sonication 75%; 14-day extended incubation recommended for chronic/low-virulence PJI; NGS positive in 65.9% of culture-negative PJI; MALDI-TOF accuracy 80% vs 59% conventional swab. Antibiotic confound: pre-aspiration antibiotics drop culture sensitivity from 75% to 41.6%; antibiotics within 3 months → 4.7-fold odds of culture-negative result; discontinue ≥2 weeks before aspiration.

> Orthopedic Reviews 2024, current diagnostic approach to PJI https://orthopedicreviews.openmedicalpublishing.org/article/120308-an-overview-of-the-current-diagnostic-approach-to-periprosthetic-joint-infections

### CRP after spine, shoulder and soft-tissue procedures is much lower than after arthroplasty — do NOT reuse the TKA/THA band
*[moderate]*

Published spine kinetics exist but are thinly reported: Mok et al., Spine 2008 (characterized normal CRP kinetics after spinal surgery vs ESR as predictor of early infectious complications); Kraft et al. 2011, 'CRP and leukocyte-count after lumbar spine surgery: fusion vs. nucleotomy' (fusion produces a substantially larger CRP excursion than nucleotomy); Kunakornsawat et al., Global Spine J 2017, 'Postoperative Kinetics of C-Reactive Protein and Erythrocyte Sediment Rate in One-, Two-, and Multilevel Posterior Spinal Decompressions and Instrumentations' — the key structural finding across these is that CRP magnitude scales with number of instrumented levels, and ESR peaks later (POD3–5) and normalizes far more slowly (weeks to months) than CRP, making ESR nearly useless as a short-horizon monitoring signal. Youn et al. 2022 compared inflammatory-marker curves under 24h vs 5-day prophylactic antibiotics after spine surgery. I found NO published per-POD CRP curve for isolated ACL reconstruction or arthroscopic rotator cuff repair — these are low-inflammatory-load procedures and CRP is not routinely drawn. Practical stance: treat spine (multi-level instrumented) as an arthroplasty-magnitude curve, single-level decompression / ACL / arthroscopic RCR / meniscectomy as low-magnitude with no validated reference band, and refuse to score CRP deviation for them.

> Mok et al., Spine 2008; Kraft et al. 2011; Kunakornsawat et al., Global Spine J 2017; Youn et al. 2022 — identified via PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=C-reactive+protein+erythrocyte+sedimentation+rate+normal+kinetics+after+spinal+surgery

### Perioperative glycemic thresholds in arthroplasty — HbA1c is weaker than newer markers
*[moderate]*

Multicenter prospective study, 2025, n=1,020 (633 knee, 387 hip), mean age 64, 12.8% with T2DM. Thresholds for 'poor glycemic control': glycated albumin ≥17.1%, HbA1c ≥7.7%, fructosamine ≥270 µmol/L. Only glycated albumin independently predicted 90-day complications: OR 4.8 (95% CI 1.4–15.7, p=0.011). Fructosamine OR 0.63 (0.13–2.13, p=0.51) and HbA1c OR 1.18 (0.24–4.97, p=0.83) were NOT significant. This contradicts the conventional ICM/AAOS optimization threshold of HbA1c >7.0–7.5% as a PJI risk gate and the Shohat fructosamine cutoff of 292 µmol/L, and suggests HbA1c alone should not drive our risk tier. Perioperative point glucose >200 mg/dL on POD1 has been associated with roughly doubled PJI odds in prior arthroplasty literature. HbA1c reflects 8–12 weeks and will not move within an RTM episode; fructosamine/glycated albumin reflect 2–3 weeks and could in principle move, but are rarely ordered.

> Multicenter prospective glycated albumin vs fructosamine vs HbA1c study, 2025, identified via PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=preoperative+albumin+HbA1c+threshold+total+joint+arthroplasty+periprosthetic+joint+infection+risk

### Malnutrition, anemia and transfusion thresholds used in arthroplasty optimization
*[moderate]*

Standard arthroplasty malnutrition triad (ICM/AAOS preoperative optimization): serum albumin <3.5 g/dL, total lymphocyte count <1,500 cells/µL, transferrin <200 mg/dL; prealbumin <15 mg/dL is the shorter-half-life (~2 day) alternative and the only one of these that meaningfully moves within a post-op episode. Hypoalbuminemia <3.5 g/dL is the most consistently reported independent predictor of SSI/PJI and 30-day complications in NSQIP arthroplasty analyses. Transfusion: AABB 2023 international guidelines (JAMA 2023;330(19):1892) recommend a restrictive hemoglobin threshold of 7 g/dL for hemodynamically stable hospitalized adults, and 8 g/dL for patients undergoing orthopaedic surgery, cardiac surgery, or with pre-existing cardiovascular disease. The FOCUS trial (NEJM 2011, hip fracture, n=2,016) found no benefit of a liberal 10 g/dL threshold over 8 g/dL. Pre-op anemia optimization uses ferritin <30 ng/mL (absolute iron deficiency) or ferritin 30–100 ng/mL with transferrin saturation <20% (functional deficiency), and WHO anemia definitions Hb <13 g/dL men / <12 g/dL non-pregnant women. Expect a physiologic Hb nadir on POD2–3 after TKA/THA of roughly 2.5–3.5 g/dL below pre-op with modern TXA use.

> AABB 2023 Red Blood Cell Transfusion Guidelines, JAMA 2023;330(19):1892; FOCUS trial NEJM 2011;365:2453; ICM/AAOS arthroplasty optimization consensus

### KDIGO AKI staging — the exact rules to implement on serial creatinine
*[strong]*

KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for AKI (2012, KDIGO AKI Work Group; still the operative definition, a 2025/2026 update is in development — verify before pinning). AKI = any of: serum creatinine increase ≥0.3 mg/dL (≥26.5 µmol/L) within 48 hours; OR increase to ≥1.5× a known or presumed baseline within the prior 7 days; OR urine volume <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours. Stage 1: SCr 1.5–1.9× baseline OR ≥0.3 mg/dL rise; UO <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12h. Stage 2: SCr 2.0–2.9× baseline; UO <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥12h. Stage 3: SCr ≥3.0× baseline OR SCr ≥4.0 mg/dL OR initiation of renal replacement therapy OR (age <18) eGFR <35 mL/min/1.73m²; UO <0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥24h OR anuria ≥12h. Relevance to us: AKI after elective TJA is a recognized complication (studies using KDIGO 2013 criteria in cohorts of 3,416 THA patients), and is strongly driven by NSAID + ACE-inhibitor/ARB + diuretic combinations post-discharge and by antibiotic-loaded cement spacers / IV vancomycin in revision cases. Creatinine baseline choice (most recent pre-op vs lowest in 7 days) materially changes staging and must be an explicit, documented engine decision.

> KDIGO AKI Work Group, KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for Acute Kidney Injury, Kidney Int Suppl 2012;2:1-138; applied in Perregaard et al., Acta Orthop 2016 (PMID 26937782) and Thorsdottir et al., Acta Anaesthesiol Scand 2024 (PMID 37726880)

### RxNorm ingestion and computing morphine milligram equivalents
*[moderate]*

RxNorm (NLM, updated monthly first Monday, with weekly prescribable-content updates) is the pivot vocabulary. Use RxNav REST APIs, no API key required, rate-limited to 20 requests/second per IP: GET /REST/rxcui.json?name={drug}; GET /REST/rxcui/{rxcui}/allrelated.json; GET /REST/rxcui/{rxcui}/property.json?propName=STRENGTH; GET /REST/rxcui/{rxcui}/historystatus.json. Term types that matter: IN (ingredient), PIN (precise ingredient), SCDC (semantic clinical drug component, carries strength), SCD (semantic clinical drug = ingredient+strength+dose form), SBD (branded), MIN (multi-ingredient). RxClass API gives ATC (opioids = ATC N02A) and FDA Established Pharmacologic Class for drug-family gating. MME computation: MME/day = (dose per administration in mg) × (administrations per day) × (conversion factor). CDC 2022 conversion factors (Annals/MMWR Nov 2022 Clinical Practice Guideline, which REVISED the 2016 file): morphine 1.0; hydrocodone 1.0; oxycodone 1.5; hydromorphone 5.0 (was 4.0 in 2016); oxymorphone 3.0; codeine 0.15; tramadol 0.2 (was 0.1); tapentadol 0.4; methadone 4.7 as a SINGLE factor (the 2016 dose-banded 4/8/10/12 scheme was retired); fentanyl transdermal is dosed in µg/hr and converts at 2.4 × µg/hr = MME/day; buprenorphine is not assigned a factor in the 2022 file. The 2022 guideline also removed the hard 90 MME/day ceiling language of 2016, replacing it with 'carefully weigh benefits and risks when increasing to ≥50 MME/day and avoid or carefully justify ≥90 MME/day.' Practical parsing hazard: sig strings ('1-2 tabs PO q4-6h PRN') give a range; compute a max-daily and a dispensed-quantity-derived MME/day and store both.

> NLM RxNorm / RxNav API https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/RxNormAPIs.html ; CDC Clinical Practice Guideline for Prescribing Opioids for Pain, MMWR Recomm Rep 2022;71(3):1-95 and the accompanying CDC Oral MME Conversion File (2022 revision). cdc.gov blocks automated fetch — verify factors against the downloadable file

### Which values realistically arrive in near-real time vs only at scheduled visits
*[moderate]*

NEAR-REAL-TIME (minutes to a few hours after resulting, via FHIR R4 Observation/DiagnosticReport, and legally required to be released without delay under the 21st Century Cures Act information blocking rules, 45 CFR Part 171): inpatient POD0–3 CBC (Hb, WBC, platelets), BMP (creatinine, eGFR, Na/K), INR if anticoagulated, and any CRP the surgeon happens to order. During the inpatient stay these arrive on a predictable daily cadence. SEMI-REAL-TIME: home INR/POC devices (rare — >90% of primary TJA now uses aspirin 81 mg BID per AAOS/CHEST, so there is no INR series at all for most patients), and point-of-care glucose from a connected meter or CGM. ONLY AT SCHEDULED VISITS OR NOT AT ALL: post-discharge CRP and ESR (drawn only if someone suspects something, or at the 2-week wound check and 6-week visit); albumin/prealbumin; HbA1c (pre-op only, 8–12 week lookback, will not move in an episode); vitamin D; ferritin/iron studies (pre-op optimization only); D-dimer (only when PE/PJI is suspected — post-op D-dimer is elevated in essentially everyone and is uninterpretable for VTE in the first weeks); synovial fluid analysis, alpha-defensin, leukocyte esterase, cultures and histology (only at aspiration or revision surgery — these are outcome labels for us, never inputs). Architectural consequence: model labs as a sparse, irregularly-sampled series with explicit missingness, and the wearable stream as the dense channel; the lab channel's job is to confirm or refute a wearable-derived deviation, and to supply hard outcome labels for PJI/AKI/anemia for retrospective validation.

> 21st Century Cures Act Information Blocking rule 45 CFR Part 171; ONC HTI-1 Final Rule (Jan 2024, USCDI v3 required for certification by Jan 1 2026); CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule CMS-0057-F (Feb 2024, APIs due Jan 1 2027); AAOS VTE prophylaxis CPG (aspirin 81 mg BID)


## Implications for backend

- SCHEMA: add a `lab_observation` table keyed on (patient_id, loinc_code, effective_datetime) with columns value_quantity NUMERIC, unit TEXT, canonical_value NUMERIC, canonical_unit TEXT, reference_range_low/high, specimen_type (serum|plasma|synovial|whole_blood), source_system, source_id, dedupe_key. Reuse the existing wearable Observation dedupe/idempotent-upsert pattern — labs have the same duplicate-delivery problem (a result gets amended, re-sent, and arrives from both the hospital and the reference lab). Store a `post_op_day` computed column (effective_datetime::date - surgery_date::date) because every threshold in this domain is indexed on POD, not calendar time.
- UNIT NORMALIZATION IS THE #1 CORRECTNESS HAZARD: CRP is reported as mg/L in Europe and mg/dL in much of US practice, differing by 10x, and published papers mislabel their own tables (the n=7,009 THA study labels its column mg/dL while reporting 134.1 for POD3, which is unambiguously mg/L). Also hs-CRP (LOINC 30522-7) is reported in mg/L at cardiac-risk scale and will be silently 100x off from surgical-inflammation CRP if conflated. Implement a UCUM-based normalizer that canonicalizes CRP to mg/L, creatinine to mg/dL, hemoglobin to g/dL, ESR to mm/h, D-dimer to ng/mL FEU (and explicitly reject DDU-vs-FEU ambiguity rather than guessing — they differ ~2x), synovial WBC to cells/uL. Add a plausibility gate: reject/flag any CRP >500 mg/L or <0 as a unit error, and any post-op CRP that is 10x or 0.1x the patient's own adjacent value.
- CRP FEATURE DESIGN — implement trajectory features, not thresholds. Per-procedure expected curves already exist in the engine for steps and walking speed; add the same structure for CRP with procedure-specific parameters: TKA/THA peak POD2-3 at ~100-185 mg/L, spine multi-level instrumented similar, shoulder arthroplasty peak POD3 at ~96 mg/L (9.6 mg/dL), and NO curve for ACL / arthroscopic rotator cuff / meniscectomy (return insufficient-evidence rather than a fabricated band). Derived features: (1) days_since_peak, (2) fraction_of_peak = current/max_observed, (3) delta_from_expected_decline, (4) second_rise flag = any increase >25% over the running minimum after POD5 with at least 2 confirmatory points, (5) failure_to_decline flag = fraction_of_peak >0.5 at POD>=7. Feed these into the existing composite deviation index as additional weighted terms, and require the existing coverage/confidence gate to be satisfied (>=2 CRP values, one of which is at or after the expected peak) before any CRP term contributes.
- GATING AND CALIBRATION: at a ~0.5-1.5% acute PJI base rate, the best published single-timepoint CRP rule (POD3 >152 mg/L, sens 75% / spec 67%) yields a positive predictive value of roughly 1-3%. Hard-code that arithmetic into the risk-tier logic: CRP must never independently escalate a patient to the top risk tier, and any CRP-driven reason code must be typed as a recommendation to obtain confirmatory labs or contact the surgical team, never as an infection assertion. Add an explicit reason-code family: CRP_FAILURE_TO_DECLINE, CRP_SECOND_RISE, CRP_ABOVE_PROCEDURE_BAND, CRP_INSUFFICIENT_SAMPLES. Log the PPV estimate alongside each fired rule so the console can display it.
- INGESTION: build a FHIR R4 lab connector behind the same provider-agnostic interface used for wearables. Required resources: Observation (US Core Laboratory Result profile, category=laboratory, code=LOINC), DiagnosticReport, Condition (ICD-10-CM + SNOMED CT), Procedure (CPT/HCPCS + SNOMED CT), MedicationRequest/MedicationStatement (RxNorm). Prefer bulk $export for backfill and FHIR Subscriptions (R4 Backport or R5 topic-based) or polling on _lastUpdated for incrementals. Map every incoming code to the internal canonical lab id via a LOINC crosswalk table with an explicit `unmapped` bucket that surfaces in ops — do NOT silently drop unmapped LOINCs, since local labs frequently send site-specific codes alongside LOINC.
- MODEL LABS AS SPARSE, NOT DENSE. Unlike wearable vitals, post-discharge labs are irregular, mostly absent, and non-randomly missing (a CRP exists precisely because a clinician was already worried, which is informative-missingness and will leak if you train on it). Do not impute CRP forward. The EWMA/CUSUM machinery built for daily vitals must not be applied to a series with 2-4 points; instead compare each observed point against the per-procedure expected curve for that POD. Represent missingness as a first-class feature (days_since_last_crp) and let the coverage gate suppress the term entirely rather than degrade gracefully into noise.
- OUTCOME LABELS: synovial fluid WBC/PMN%, alpha-defensin, leukocyte esterase, cultures, histology and the MSIS 2018 / EBJIS 2021 scores are labels, not inputs — they only exist after an aspiration or revision. Build a separate `pji_adjudication` table that stores each MSIS 2018 component with its point value and the computed total (>=6 infected, 2-5 inconclusive, <2 not infected) plus the EBJIS three-level classification, so retrospective validation of the wearable+CRP model has a gold standard. Implement the MSIS scorer as pure deterministic code (it is an 8-term additive integer score) — no LLM involvement, consistent with the cloud-first-LLM policy of narrative-only generation.
- CODE SETS AND VERSIONING: ICD-10-CM updates annually effective Oct 1 (FY2026 set is live now), CPT annually effective Jan 1, LOINC twice yearly, RxNorm monthly with weekly prescribable updates. Store a `code_system_version` on every coded row and build the crosswalks as data-driven tables loaded from the official release files, not as Python dicts — otherwise the Oct 1 ICD-10 update silently breaks complication detection. Verify LOINC codes against the downloadable LOINC table rather than scraping loinc.org (it returns 403 to automated fetchers), and verify MME factors against the CDC 2022 Oral MME Conversion File (cdc.gov also 403s).
- MME COMPUTATION: implement as a deterministic pipeline — RxNorm SCD/SCDC -> ingredient + strength in mg -> multiply by parsed sig frequency -> multiply by the CDC 2022 conversion factor. Store both a max_daily_mme (upper bound of any PRN range) and a dispensed_mme_per_day (total dispensed mg-equivalents / days supply); they diverge substantially and clinicians expect the second. Cache RxNav lookups aggressively (RxNorm changes monthly, so a 30-day cache is safe) and respect the 20 req/s per-IP rate limit. Handle methadone with the single 4.7 factor and fentanyl transdermal as 2.4 x mcg/hr; do not assign buprenorphine a factor. Flag opioid escalation (rising MME/day after POD14) as its own reason code — it is a real recovery-deviation signal and, unlike CRP, the data actually exists post-discharge.
- AKI DETECTION: implement KDIGO 2012 staging as a deterministic function over the creatinine series with an explicitly documented baseline policy (most recent pre-op creatinine, falling back to lowest value in the prior 7 days). Both the 48-hour absolute rule (>=0.3 mg/dL) and the 7-day ratio rule (>=1.5x) must be evaluated on a sliding window, and the function must return the triggering rule so the reason code is explainable. Urine output criteria are not obtainable in RTM — document that our staging is creatinine-only and therefore under-detects.
- COST AND LATENCY: lab ingestion is bursty and low-volume compared with wearable data (single-digit observations per patient per episode vs thousands), so the marginal storage/compute cost is negligible; the real cost is EHR integration engineering and per-connection vendor fees. Do not spend LLM tokens parsing labs — every rule in this domain is a numeric comparison and belongs in deterministic Python, with Groq reserved for narrative rendering of already-computed reason codes.

## Open questions

- I could not obtain a peer-reviewed per-POD CRP reference band for isolated ACL reconstruction, arthroscopic rotator cuff repair, meniscectomy or ankle procedures — does one exist, or do we ship an explicit 'no validated band' state for those five of our seven procedures?
- No study I found validates a formal 'second rise' rule with sensitivity/specificity. The Acta Orthop paper states the clinical heuristic ('if the value remains high or if there is a new rise on days 7 or 8, systemic infection should be suspected') but gives no diagnostic performance. Is there a published delta-CRP or CRP-ratio rule with numbers, or must we derive and prospectively validate our own?
- Exact LOINC codes for synovial fluid leukocyte count, synovial PMN/100 leukocytes, synovial CRP, and synovial alpha-defensin were not verifiable — loinc.org returns 403 to automated fetch. These need confirmation against the current LOINC release file before hard-coding.
- The CDC 2022 MME conversion factor file and the ICD-10-CM tabular could not be fetched directly (both cdc.gov and icd10data.com return 403). The factors and codes reported here come from my knowledge of those releases and should be confirmed against the official downloadable files before they enter production code.
- CPT 2025/2026 revised the RPM and RTM code families (shorter-duration device-supply and treatment-management codes). The exact current descriptors, the ≥16-days-of-data requirement status, and CY2026 MPFS national payment amounts for 98975–98981 need verification — this directly determines the product's reimbursement story.
- ESR post-operative kinetics after TKA/THA are referenced everywhere but I did not capture a numeric per-POD ESR curve. ESR peaks later than CRP (roughly POD3–5) and normalizes over weeks to months; if we want an ESR term at all we need the actual reference band, and it may not be worth ingesting given its slow kinetics.
- Is a KDIGO AKI guideline update (2025/2026) published or still in draft? The 2012 definition is what I have verified; if an update changed the baseline-creatinine or staging rules we would need to re-pin.
- The 2025 glycated-albumin study contradicts the conventional HbA1c >7.0–7.5% arthroplasty optimization gate (HbA1c OR 1.18, n.s., vs GA OR 4.8). Which does our risk model use, given HbA1c is the one actually present in nearly every chart and glycated albumin is almost never ordered?
- What EHR integration path are we actually taking — direct Epic/Cerner FHIR app registration, an aggregator (Health Gorilla, Particle, Metriport, Zus), or TEFCA/QHIN? This determines per-connection cost, latency, and whether we get near-real-time subscriptions or only periodic bulk pulls, and none of that could be priced from public sources here.
- Informative missingness: a post-discharge CRP exists mostly because a clinician was already concerned. Any model trained on 'CRP was drawn' will leak the outcome. Do we exclude the existence-of-lab signal entirely, or model it explicitly as a clinician-concern feature separate from the CRP value?
