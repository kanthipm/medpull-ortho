The primary study (Windred DP, Burns AC, Lane JM, Saxena R, Rutter MK, Cain SW, Phillips AJK, 'Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration', Sleep 2024) analysed n=60,977 UK Biobank participants (mean age 62.8 ± 7.8, 55% female), with 1,859 deaths (4.84 per 1,000 person-years) over a MEAN follow-up of 6.30 ± 0.83 years — not n=88,975, not 3,010 deaths, not 7.1-year median. The headline effect is reported as a protective gradient (higher regularity associated with 20%–48% lower all-cause mortality risk and 16%–39% lower cancer mortality risk), not as the specific HR 1.53 / 1.88 / 1.36 triple quoted. n=88,975 appears to be the generic UK Biobank accelerometry analytic sample cited in other papers, not this one; the researcher has almost certainly merged two different studies. - **CLAIM:** Claim 1: Choi 2011 allows 'an interruption of up to 2 minutes of non-zero counts provided the 30-minute upstream AND downstream windows flanking the interruption are all-zero.'
  - **CORRECTION:** The published rule is OR, not AND. Choi L, Liu Z, Matthews CE, Buchowski MS, 'Validation of accelerometer wear and nonwear time classification algorithm', Med Sci Sports Exerc 2011;43(2):357-64, specifies an 'allowance of 2-min interval of nonzero counts with the upstream OR downstream 30-min consecutive zero-count window.' Requiring both flanks (AND) is a stricter rule than the paper and than the reference implementations, and will systematically classify LESS non-wear than Choi 2011. The 90-min window and 2-min allowance are otherwise correct.   - *src:* https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20581716/
- **CLAIM:** Claim 3: 'Current default nonwear_range_threshold = 150 mg (not the classic 50 mg)' and "nonwear_approach default = '2023', which flags the FULL 60-min window as non-wear (the older '2013' approach flagged only the central 15-min window)."

That drop is comparable to the median MDC of 0.14 m/s from Claim 6, which is the real reason single-day speed is untrustworthy: the signal and the noise floor are the same size.
- Apple's design set is larger than the validation set the claims quote: Table 4/5/6 design = 528 participant-visits (359 unique participants), 15,487 walkovers, vs validation 250 visits (179 unique), 7,440 walkovers. Cohort A participants attended up to two in-lab visits at least 8 weeks apart over a year, and each carried TWO iPhones (one self-placed, one proctor-placed contralaterally), which is why device-visits exceed participant counts.
- Apple's white paper has a self-contradiction worth knowing about before you cite it: the Figure 8 caption calls 9.8% a 'false negative rate' but then parenthetically describes the 262 cases as 'false positives from 2671 symmetry-classified device-visits'. The claim silently repairs this. If a reviewer opens the PDF, expect the question.
- Apple's own Discussion volunteers two limitations neither the claims nor most citations mention: the study population 'didn't span all adult ages' and was 'limited to individuals residing in the Santa Clara Valley'. Combined with 80% musculoskeletal prevalence and mean age 74.7, this is a narrow geographic and demographic base.
- Cohort B (the asymmetry design cohort) has age range 26-55, mean 37.5 — calling them 'young adults' understates the spread, and 16F/35M is heavily male.
- The Physiological Reports LOA values the claims omit are wider than the ones quoted: Polar Grit X Pro RMSSD LOA -14.30 to 23.60 ms (a 38 ms span), WHOOP RMSSD -12.50 to 10.94, Oura Gen3 RMSSD -11.43 to 6.43, Polar RHR -5.36 to 2.06 bpm. Oura Gen3 also averages heart rate into 10-min segments before the 5-min HRV windowing, and Polar's 4-hour post-sleep-onset window applies to both RHR and HRV, not just HRV.
- The Sleep Advances cohort is 84% male (52M/10F) with mean BMI 28.8 — a sleep-clinic-shaped sample. Sleep-stage performance in that population may not transfer to a mixed post-surgical cohort, and none of the six devices was tested on a night disrupted by post-operative pain.
- The PLOS SpO2 cohort was clinically enriched: 42 of 49 participants had pulmonary disease and 5 had cardiovascular disease. That strengthens the relevance to post-op pulmonary complications but means the MAE figures are not from healthy volunteers.
- CADENCE-Adults spans ages 21-85 and tested 21 devices, versus 117 youth and 8 devices in CADENCE-Kids. If you want a defensible slow-speed penalty for an adult TKA cohort, the adults catalog is the correct source and gives a gentler picture: 40% MAPE at 0.8-3.2 km/h, 7% at 4.0-6.4 km/h, wrist 15% vs waist 3% at normal speeds.
- Two of the strongest claims in the set rest on sources I could not fully open: the PeerJ 2026 full text (peerj.com returns 403 to automated fetch) and Apple's developer documentation (JS-rendered).
